Section 2
Macro

The Federal Reserve's September 16 quarter-point hike — its first tightening move since 2023 — has recalibrated the macro landscape: the policy rate is back in restrictive territory and the door to near-term cuts has effectively closed. Major banks have moved the prime rate to 7.00%, and the transmission to mortgage rates, auto loans, and corporate credit lines is accelerating.

Stronger-than-expected economic data is sustaining the "higher-for-longer" narrative: resilient labor markets and sticky services inflation give the Fed little reason to pivot quickly. The 10Y–2Y spread has re-steepened modestly to +31 basis points, suggesting bond markets are repricing for durable growth rather than imminent recession, though higher borrowing costs will increasingly test that thesis into Q4.

Section 3
Equities

U.S. equities closed Friday's session modestly higher: SPY +0.54% to 771.35, QQQ +0.46% to 744.50, and IWM +0.11% to 281.97, with small-caps underperforming as rate sensitivity weighs more heavily on the Russell. The broad advance was narrow, with mega-cap tech providing most of the lift while rate-sensitive sectors lagged.

Notable movers:

  • INTC (Intel) –3.45% to $123.00 on heavy volume (96M shares), pressured by ongoing competitive concerns in the AI chip space and broader sector rotation away from legacy semiconductor names.
  • AAL (American Airlines) +3.90% to $13.87 with 75M+ shares traded, bouncing on revised capacity guidance and a slightly improved near-term fuel outlook despite elevated crude.
Section 4
Fixed Income

The 10-year Treasury yield reached 5.18% as of September 24, marking a significant move from the 4.96% level just two sessions prior. The 2-year yield stands at 4.87%, placing the 10Y–2Y spread at a modestly positive +31 basis points. The curve is steepening from its prior flat/slightly inverted configuration — a sign that long-end investors are beginning to price in a durable "higher-for-longer" cycle rather than a near-term recession trade.

The TLT (20+ Year Treasury ETF) closed at $79.32, reflecting the sustained pressure on duration. Credit spreads via HYG remain relatively contained at $77.86, suggesting the market is not yet pricing systemic credit stress despite the elevated rate environment.

Section 5
Currencies

EUR/USD trades at 1.1379, maintaining its position above the 1.13 handle as the euro draws modest support from improving European sentiment and narrowing rate differentials at the margin. The pair remains vulnerable if U.S. data continues to outperform, which would give the Fed more room to keep rates elevated.

USD/JPY sits at 157.56, near multi-decade highs for the yen, reflecting the persistent divergence between the Bank of Japan's ultra-loose policy stance and the Fed's renewed tightening cycle. Intervention risk is elevated above 158–160, though Tokyo has shown patience in recent months. A break above 158 would likely prompt verbal warnings from Japanese authorities.

Section 6
Commodities

Gold: $4,162.63/oz — Bullion continues its remarkable run, trading above $4,160 as investors balance a strong dollar and elevated real yields against persistent geopolitical uncertainty and central bank buying. The metal's resilience at these levels suggests structural demand, not merely speculative positioning; a pullback in yields or fresh safe-haven demand could propel it toward $4,200.

WTI Crude: $96.41/bbl — Oil pulled back from $101.44 (Sep 18) to the mid-$96 range, easing slightly on demand concerns tied to slowing global growth and higher borrowing costs. Supply discipline from OPEC+ and strong travel-season demand have kept the floor elevated above $95. Energy is a key input to the inflation narrative — a sustained move back above $100 would complicate the Fed's calculus considerably.
