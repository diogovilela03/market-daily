Section 02

Macro

The rate backdrop remains unambiguously restrictive, with the 10-year Treasury at 4.88% and the 2-year at 4.56%, leaving real yields elevated across the curve. The yield curve has steepened modestly to a +32bps positive spread, suggesting markets may be pricing in an eventual easing cycle while acknowledging that the Fed has little room to pivot near-term. USD/JPY above 159 continues to highlight the divergence between the Fed's hold-and-watch posture and the Bank of Japan's cautious normalisation path, sustaining yen carry dynamics that could unwind sharply on any dovish US data surprise.

Section 04

Fixed Income

The US 10-year Treasury yield stands at 4.88%, while the 2-year yields 4.56%, producing a +32bps positive spread — a curve that has steepened from near-inversion levels seen earlier in the cycle. The steepening reflects the market's tentative acceptance that short rates may peak while long-end inflation and fiscal risk premiums remain sticky. At current levels, Treasuries continue to compete credibly with equities on a risk-adjusted basis, limiting the multiple expansion available to growth stocks despite Wednesday's rally.

Section 05

Currencies

EUR/USD at 1.1079 reflects relative dollar resilience underpinned by the Fed's higher-for-longer stance and structurally elevated US yields versus European peers. The pair has stabilised above 1.10, suggesting the market is not pricing an aggressive USD breakout but respecting support for the euro on any European growth surprise. USD/JPY at 159.83 remains in extended yen-weakness territory; the BOJ's cautious pace of policy normalisation versus the Fed's elevated hold keeps carry trades active, though any sharp US data downturn could trigger rapid JPY short-covering and dislocated cross-asset moves.

Section 06

Commodities

Gold spot at $3,960.60/oz is within striking distance of the $4,000 psychological milestone, sustained by central bank accumulation trends, persistent geopolitical uncertainty, and demand from investors hedging dollar debasement risk. The metal's advance despite elevated real yields is notably unusual and underscores structural demand shifts. WTI crude at $99.64/bbl is flirting with the $100 threshold, a level that carries both psychological and policy significance — a sustained breach could renew consumer inflation pressure and complicate the Fed's data-dependent calculus heading into the December meeting.
