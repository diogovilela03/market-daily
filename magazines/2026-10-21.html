Macro

The Federal Reserve's higher-for-longer posture remains intact as the 10-year Treasury yield held at 5.15%, its third consecutive session near multi-year highs. The 2-year yield at 4.77% keeps the curve in positive territory (+38 basis points), a modest steepening that reflects markets beginning to price longer-dated term premium rather than near-term easing. USD/JPY extending to 158.17 underscores the divergence between Fed restraint and the Bank of Japan's reluctance to tighten aggressively, keeping intervention risk elevated above the 158–160 zone. Crude oil's sustained break above $100 per barrel adds a stagflationary overlay that complicates the soft-landing thesis heading into year-end.

Section 3

Equities

US equities delivered a bifurcated session on Tuesday (Oct 20). The S&P 500 proxy SPY added 0.34% to close at 769.05, while the Nasdaq-100 tracker QQQ dipped marginally to 717.63 (−0.10%), dragged by large-cap tech underperformance ahead of megacap earnings. The standout was the Russell 2000 via IWM, which surged 1.66% to 275.78 — its strongest single-day outperformance versus large-cap benchmarks in several sessions — as investors rotated toward domestic cyclicals less sensitive to global macro crosswinds.

Notable movers: Braze (BRZE) exploded 51.7% on Q3 earnings that materially exceeded consensus, with volume of 64.5 million shares (roughly 50× average daily turnover). ATGL (Alpha Technology Group) added 52.6%. On the downside, CJMB collapsed 65.6% and GME warrants (GME+) shed 44.8%, reflecting idiosyncratic deleveraging in speculative names.

Section 4

Fixed Income

US Treasury yields remain at cycle highs. The 10-year yield held at 5.15% while the 2-year settled at 4.77%, leaving the curve positively sloped by 38 basis points and continuing to steepen from the near-zero and inverted levels seen through most of 2024–2025. A steepening curve at these absolute yield levels reflects term premium re-pricing rather than rate-cut optimism — investors are demanding more compensation for holding duration, not pricing in looser policy. For equity markets, a 5%+ risk-free rate continues to compress multiples for long-duration growth assets and raises the hurdle rate for capital allocation decisions heading into Q4 planning cycles.

Section 5

Currencies

EUR/USD held firm at 1.1223, near multi-month highs against the dollar, as improving Eurozone data has narrowed the rate differential narrative — though the pair remains range-bound ahead of the next ECB decision. USD/JPY trades at 158.17, within striking distance of the 160 level that previously prompted Bank of Japan intervention. The yen's persistent weakness reflects the BOJ's cautious tightening pace against the Fed's extended hold; any acceleration in Japanese inflation data or hawkish BOJ communications could trigger sharp yen appreciation. FX desks are closely watching this level as the most likely site of official intervention in G10 markets.

Section 6

Commodities

Gold trades at $4,114.03/oz, near record territory, supported by a combination of central bank accumulation, de-dollarization flows from emerging markets, and residual geopolitical risk premium. The metal's strength despite a high-yield environment underscores its evolving role as a diversifier against systemic risk rather than a pure inflation hedge. WTI crude holds at $101.91/bbl (latest data as of Oct 14), sustained above the psychologically significant $100 level by continued OPEC+ supply discipline and elevated Middle East risk premium. Oil above $100 reinforces inflationary pressures and materially reduces the probability of near-term Fed easing, with energy-sector earnings set to benefit disproportionately from the current price environment.
