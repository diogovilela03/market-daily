Market Drivers

  • Hawkish FOMC minutes reprice the long end. The 10-year Treasury has pushed to 4.69% on hawkish minutes and energy-driven inflation fears, dragging homebuilders (KB Home, Meritage, LGI, Hovnanian) sharply lower and forcing a rethink on rate-sensitive equity leadership.

  • Gold catches a bid at record levels. Newmont +3.18% on Friday, with spot gold above $4,600 confirming that the safe-haven and dollar-hedge trades remain fully in force even as equities grind higher.
Section 02

Macro

The Fed's newly hawkish tone — flagged in the latest minutes — is the defining macro variable: markets are being asked to accept fewer cuts and a slower balance-sheet run-off, and the long end has responded with a clean +4bp move on the 10-year. Energy-driven inflation risk is being cited alongside the minutes as a reason yields refuse to compress, keeping mortgage rates elevated and the housing complex on the defensive. With no major US data prints inside the next 48 hours, the tape is free to trade almost exclusively on rates, gold, and single-name earnings risk.

Equities

Small caps outperformed on Friday, an unusual reading given the back-up in yields, and pointing to positioning-driven mean reversion rather than fundamental rotation. Among mega-caps, Goldman Sachs +3.73% on rising yield volatility (favourable for market-making), and Moderna +8.9% on above-average volume were the standouts.

Fixed Income

US10Y 4.69% (+4bp), US2Y 4.19% (flat). The 2s10s spread stands at +50bp — a clearly positive, mildly steepening curve, driven entirely by the long end. This is the classic "hawkish steepener": short-rate expectations are anchored while term premium re-expands on hawkish Fed communication and energy-inflation risk. Homebuilders are the first place the pain is showing up (Hovnanian bearish −0.44 sentiment, KB/Meritage/LGI plummeting on the yield move); regional banks and life insurers are the mirror trade on the other side.
