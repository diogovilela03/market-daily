A broad risk-off session swept US equities on Thursday, with all three major indices shedding roughly 2% as semiconductor names led the retreat — AMD fell nearly 8% and NVDA gave back over 3% amid persistent pressure from long-end Treasury yields pinned above 5%. Safe-haven assets held firm, with gold near $3,551 underscoring investor caution heading into a heavy earnings week.

Section 01

Market Drivers

  • Semiconductor sector selloff: AMD plunged 7.72% on 70.5M shares, its sharpest single-day decline in recent weeks, pulling NVDA (-3.30%) and the 3× leveraged SOXL (-8.04%) lower. The move pressured QQQ disproportionately and reinforced a rotation away from high-multiple tech.
  • ANT surges on massive volume: ANT shares rocketed +17.07% with 847 million shares traded — the highest volume on the board — signaling a major company-specific catalyst, potentially related to a listing event or significant corporate development.
  • Rates acting as a valuation ceiling: The 10-year Treasury yield held near 5.07%, a level that continues to compress equity risk premiums. Inverse ETFs SPDN (+2.08%) and SQQQ (+6.62%) attracted heavy buying, confirming broad hedging activity.
  • Small-caps matched large-cap losses: IWM declined 2.18%, reflecting broad selling rather than a narrow mega-cap story, with the equal-weight nature of the decline pointing to macro rather than idiosyncratic forces.

Section 02

Macro

The macro backdrop remains defined by stubborn long-end yields: the 10-year Treasury at 5.07% and the 2-year at 4.80% place the 2Y–10Y spread at a modestly positive +27 basis points, a gradual normalization from prior inversion that nonetheless leaves absolute borrowing costs high. With no Fed decision or major CPI/NFP release on the immediate horizon, markets are navigating a data-light period where the prevailing rate level — not near-term policy shifts — drives the daily tone. USD/JPY at 159.21 reflects the sustained yield differential between the US and Japan, keeping intervention risk elevated in Tokyo, while EUR/USD near 1.0990 suggests relative euro stability despite global risk-off pressures.

Section 03

Equities

All three major index ETFs closed sharply lower: SPY at $715.87 (-1.96%), QQQ at $645.04 (-2.19%), and IWM at $264.22 (-2.18%). The uniform decline across market caps indicates a macro-driven rather than sector-rotation dynamic.

Notable movers: ANT led the most-actively-traded board with a +17.07% surge on 847M shares — extraordinary volume by any measure — pointing to a significant event-driven catalyst. On the downside, AMD was the standout decliner among large-caps, falling -7.72% ($489.19) on 70.5M shares in what appears to be sector-wide pressure on semiconductor AI plays. INTC bucked the trend with a modest +1.92% gain.

Section 04

Fixed Income

The US Treasury curve remains in a modestly positive slope: the 10-year yield stands at 5.07% (as of November 18) while the 2-year yield holds at 4.80%, producing a 2Y–10Y spread of +27 basis points. The curve is gently steepening — a signal that markets may be beginning to price in future Fed easing — but the absolute level of rates remains historically elevated, sustaining meaningful pressure on equity valuations and credit markets. At 5.07%, the 10-year yield exceeds the S&P 500 earnings yield for many index members, reinforcing the case for caution in long-duration equities.

Section 05

Currencies

EUR/USD traded at 1.0990, holding near recent levels as the US dollar maintained its broad bid amid risk-off positioning. The euro's relative stability suggests European data has not been the dominant driver. USD/JPY climbed to 159.21 — well into the zone that Japanese authorities have historically cited as a trigger for verbal or direct intervention. The yen's persistent weakness is a function of the still-wide US-Japan rate differential; until the Bank of Japan accelerates policy normalization or the Fed signals cuts, JPY depreciation pressure is likely to persist, keeping FX intervention risk elevated.

Section 06

Commodities

Gold spot held near $3,551/oz, reflecting persistent safe-haven demand amid elevated equity volatility and macro uncertainty. The metal's resilience at historically high levels suggests investors continue to hedge against rate uncertainty, geopolitical risk, and currency debasement. WTI crude last traded at $89.53/bbl (as of November 17), declining from the $94–$100 range seen in early November. The pullback aligns with softer near-term demand signals and potential oversupply concerns, though prices remain well above levels seen earlier in the year — keeping energy a significant driver of inflation expectations.
