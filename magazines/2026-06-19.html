01 — MARKET DRIVERS

What's Moving Markets Today

  • Fed hawkish hold under Warsh: The FOMC kept rates steady at its June 18 meeting but revised the dot plot to project a potential additional hike by year-end — catching markets off-guard, spiking the 2-year yield 15bps, and triggering a sharp initial selloff in rate-sensitive and dividend-paying equities (PEP, HSY, SHOP all fell 3–5%).
  • Friday recovery led by Nasdaq-100: Markets are rebounding broadly after Wednesday-Thursday's FOMC shock; the Nasdaq-100 (+2.51%) is outperforming, consistent with investors recalibrating growth-stock valuations at higher discount rates rather than capitulating from risk assets entirely.
  • US-Iran diplomatic breakthrough: A peace agreement between Washington and Tehran is compressing the geopolitical risk premium in energy and defense markets — RTX fell 3.47% Thursday as conflict-driven demand for defense contractors faded, and crude oil has been trending meaningfully lower.
  • Bank of Japan hikes to 3-decade high: The BOJ raised its benchmark rate to levels unseen since the 1990s, boosting Japanese financial stocks (MUFG +3.13%) but providing only a muted lift to the yen as carry-trade dynamics and the persistent Fed-BOJ rate differential continue to dominate FX flows.

Advancing US-Iran negotiations represent a meaningful geopolitical de-escalation that markets are beginning to price as a reduction in energy and defense risk premiums, reinforcing the decline in WTI crude and weighing on RTX and peers.

04 — VOLATILITY

VIX Fear Gauge

VIX: 16.40 (−12.44% from prior session; prior close ~18.44). The fear index sits comfortably below the 20 threshold that historically signals elevated uncertainty, having retreated sharply from yesterday's post-FOMC spike. The rapid normalization of implied volatility is consistent with a market that is interpreting the Fed's hawkish signal as a contingent tightening path rather than an imminent hike, and with options dealers unwinding the event-day hedges they had accumulated ahead of the June 18 decision. 05 — FIXED INCOME

Treasuries & Yield Curve

The 10-year Treasury yield stands at 4.49% (as of June 17, +6bps from prior session), while the 2-year yield surged to 4.20% (+15bps) — an asymmetric move that directly reflects the short-end's sensitivity to near-term Fed rate expectations following the hawkish dot-plot update. The disproportionate rise in the 2-year is a clean signal that the market is repricing the probability of a year-end hike, not the longer-run neutral rate.

The 2s/10s spread holds at +29 basis points, maintaining a normally-sloped curve that offers no immediate recession signal. However, the short end's sharper reaction represents modest flattening pressure; a sustained move toward inversion from this level would require either further hawkish Fed rhetoric or a deterioration in the growth outlook. For now, the curve's shape is consistent with a soft-landing scenario in which the Fed retains the option to hike once more without triggering a credit or growth shock.

06 — CURRENCIES

FX Markets

EUR/USD: 1.1452 — the euro holds near multi-month highs against the dollar, supported by a relatively stable ECB rate path and positive European risk sentiment, even as the Fed's hawkish shift places modest upward pressure on the greenback globally. The pair's resilience above 1.14 suggests the euro zone's rate trajectory is not materially diverging from the US at this stage.

USD/JPY: 161.25 — the yen remains near historic lows despite the Bank of Japan's landmark rate hike, underscoring the dominance of the Fed-BOJ rate differential (approximately 390bps) and the persistence of yen-funded carry trades. A sustained USD/JPY above 160 historically invites verbal guidance or outright intervention from Japanese authorities; traders are watching the 162–163 level as potential action thresholds. The tension between BOJ rate normalisation and the entrenched carry dynamic makes USD/JPY one of the most event-sensitive pairs in the current macro environment.
