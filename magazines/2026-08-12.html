Macro

The July payrolls report — delivering a surprise contraction alongside subdued wage growth — marked a decisive inflection in the Fed's rate trajectory, effectively removing near-term hike risk and opening the door to a potential September cut or extended hold. Markets are now pricing the Fed as firmly on pause, with any further labor market deterioration potentially accelerating the timeline for easing. Gold's sustained climb to all-time highs above $4,399 and the 10Y yield's elevation at 4.72% reflect a nuanced picture: investors are hedging both a prolonged low-growth scenario and residual inflation risk that hasn't fully cleared. The dollar continues to weaken on the dovish repricing, reinforcing the broad commodity rally and supporting EM currencies.

Fixed Income

The US 10-year yield stands at 4.72% while the 2-year yield holds at 4.25%, producing a positive 47-basis-point spread — a normally sloped, gently steepening curve. The steepening dynamic reflects the post-payroll repricing: short-end yields fell more aggressively as near-term Fed hike expectations evaporated, while the long end remains elevated on residual inflation risk and robust Treasury supply. No inversion risk is present near-term, and the curve's positive slope reduces recession signal risk. The 10Y–2Y differential has been widening since the jobs shock, a pattern historically consistent with the early phases of a dovish pivot cycle.
