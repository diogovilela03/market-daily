Market Drivers
  • Jobs shock reprices the rate path: July payrolls contracted unexpectedly, sending the S&P 500 to a new record last Thursday and effectively eliminating residual bets on further Fed rate hikes. The pivot to a data-dependent hold is now the consensus Fed posture.
  • Precious metals in full breakout: Gold surpassed $4,360/oz and gold mining ETF GDX posted its best weekly gain since 2008 (+21%), driven by USD weakness, declining real yields, and accelerating safe-haven positioning.

Section 2
Macro

The July payrolls contraction has materially repriced Fed expectations: rate-cut probabilities have jumped sharply while rate-hike bets have effectively been priced out, leaving the Fed in a data-dependent hold posture heading into its next meeting. Treasury markets reflect the shift — the 10-year yield eased to 4.65% and the 2-year to 4.19% (each falling 4–6bps from the prior session), with the positively sloped curve (+46bps spread) signaling growing confidence in a soft-landing scenario. A weakening US dollar compounds the repricing dynamic, accelerating capital flows into commodities and non-dollar assets as real yield expectations compress.

Equities

US equities slipped modestly on Monday as markets consolidated from last week's record highs: SPY –0.03% ($773.03), QQQ –0.30% ($720.87), and IWM –0.52% ($299.98), with small-caps leading the pullback as rate-sensitive names gave back gains. Among notable individual movers: Spotify rallied approximately 5% on a strong Q3 2026 revenue outlook projecting ~€5 billion with 32.9% gross margins, validating its premium subscriber model. Arm Holdings (ARM) fell 4.6% amid sector-wide profit-taking and mounting concerns over a cooling in near-term AI infrastructure investment, raising questions about the breadth and durability of the AI spending cycle.

Section 4
Volatility

VIX printed at 15.46 on Monday, up 3.62% on the session but firmly within the historically complacent zone (below 20). The modest single-session uptick reflects incremental hedging demand ahead of a busy earnings week — with Cisco, Applied Materials, and Coherent all reporting — rather than any acute fear signal. A VIX below 16 amid a market sitting at all-time highs suggests options markets are pricing limited near-term downside, though the elevated iv30 reading (89.8%) indicates longer-dated volatility expectations remain elevated relative to spot.

Section 5
Fixed Income

The US Treasury curve ended Monday with the 10-year yield at 4.65% and the 2-year at 4.19%, producing a +46bps spread — a positively sloped, steepening configuration that reflects markets pricing out near-term rate hikes while keeping longer-run inflation and growth expectations intact. Both tenors fell last week following the payrolls shock, with the 2-year dropping more sharply as the instrument most sensitive to near-term Fed rate expectations. The un-inversion of the 2s10s from its prolonged negative posture continues to be read by fixed-income strategists as a soft-landing confirmation signal, though history warns that the sharpest curve steepening episodes have often preceded, not accompanied, recessionary turns.

Section 6
Currencies

EUR/USD at 1.1542 reflects the dollar's broad retreat as reduced US rate-hike premium narrows the interest rate differential that had anchored dollar strength through 2025. The euro's advance aligns with improved eurozone manufacturing data and the ECB's slightly hawkish hold stance relative to a dovishly pivoting Fed. USD/JPY at 159.29 keeps the yen under sustained pressure — a paradox given the Bank of Japan's gradual rate normalization — with carry-trade demand and the still-wide US-Japan rate differential more than offsetting BoJ tightening expectations. Japanese bank equities (MUFG +3.1%, MFG +4.2%) remain a key beneficiary of the higher-rate-for-longer BoJ narrative even as the yen lags.

Section 7
Commodities

Gold at $4,360.94/oz is at generational highs, driven by a confluence of USD weakness, declining real yields following the payrolls shock, and accelerating safe-haven positioning. The metals complex is broadly in rally mode — Newmont (NEM) rose 3.0% and 5.8% in consecutive sessions, Wheaton Precious Metals (WPM) gained 6.9%, and gold miners as a sector posted their best weekly performance since 2008 (+21% GDX). WTI crude at $81.96/bbl (most recent available, as of 3 August) has pulled back meaningfully from the $86–$93 range seen in late July, reflecting OPEC+ supply uncertainty, softening Chinese demand signals, and a modest demand headwind from a potential US growth slowdown now priced into futures markets.
