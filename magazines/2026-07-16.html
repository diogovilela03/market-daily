Volatility

VIX settled at 15.67, down 5.30% from the prior session, sitting comfortably in the sub-16 range that signals complacent hedging demand. Despite IBM's dramatic collapse, a geopolitical escalation in the Middle East, and ongoing bifurcation in tech, the market is pricing limited near-term tail risk. The muted implied volatility level entering a dense earnings window — TSM, Netflix, and UnitedHealth all report today; Tesla and Alphabet in six days — warrants attention. Section 5
Fixed Income

The 10-year Treasury yield eased to 4.58% (from 4.62% the prior session) while the 2-year fell to 4.18% (from 4.26%), leaving the 10Y–2Y spread at a mildly positive +40 basis points. The curve is gently steepening at the short end as markets incrementally price earlier Fed easing following the soft CPI print. The positive slope removes an inversion-risk flag for now, but the spread remains historically compressed and could compress further if upcoming macro data surprises to the downside or the Fed signals a delayed pivot. Bond market reaction to Thursday's earnings slate — particularly TSM's read on semiconductor supply chains — could move yields in either direction.

Section 6
Currencies

EUR/USD holds at 1.1471, supported by relative ECB hawkishness and a softer dollar following the benign U.S. CPI print. The euro's continued resilience above the 1.14 handle reflects narrowing rate differentials and improving European growth expectations, with the market pricing the ECB as less likely to cut aggressively than the Fed.

USD/JPY remains elevated at 162.04, near multi-decade highs for the yen and within range of prior Bank of Japan intervention levels. The yen's structural weakness persists despite Japan's improving banking profitability — MUFG this week surpassed Toyota to become Japan's most valuable company by market cap, highlighting a paradox: a resurgent Japanese financial sector co-existing with a historically weak currency. BoJ intervention risk increases as the pair approaches 163–165.
