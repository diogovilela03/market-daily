Section 2
Macro

The Federal Reserve's July FOMC meeting minutes are scheduled for release this week — a critical inflection point that could clarify the committee's rate-path intentions following a surprisingly weak July jobs report that briefly rattled markets. With the 10-year Treasury at 4.68% and the 2-year at 4.17%, the yield curve has steepened to a +51 basis-point spread, reflecting a meaningful repricing of the near-term rate trajectory. Moderating inflation — flat July PPI, softening CPI — is giving the Fed room to maneuver, while geopolitical uncertainty and currency market volatility (USD/JPY near 159.74) add complexity to the committee's framework. The Empire State Manufacturing Survey and Canada CPI data are also due today, offering early reads on regional economic momentum.

Section 3
Equities

All three benchmark ETFs edged lower in Monday's session: SPY at 772.67 (-0.47%), QQQ at 729.87 (-0.16%), and IWM at 304.06 (-0.34%). The broad but modest pullback follows a strong prior-week rally fuelled by the soft payrolls surprise and comes amid a wait-and-see posture ahead of the week's macro and earnings catalysts. Among notable movers, IPST surged +235.9% on extraordinary volume (~100M shares), reflecting a company-specific catalyst, while EYPT collapsed -67% (~52M shares) on material adverse news. Mega-cap technology was broadly range-bound, with NVDA near $225.

Section 4
Volatility

The CBOE VIX stands at 15.19, up +6.19% on the session (+0.94 points), yet remains firmly in "complacent" territory below the 20 threshold. The intraday uptick signals hedging activity building ahead of this week's FOMC minutes release and the concentrated retail earnings calendar. At current levels, options markets are not pricing in elevated near-term tail risk, though the session's directional drift bears watching if FOMC language proves more hawkish than expected.

Section 5
Fixed Income

The US 10-year Treasury yield sits at 4.68% with the 2-year at 4.17%, maintaining a positively sloped curve with a spread of +51 basis points. The curve has been steepening as markets price out near-term rate hikes: front-end yields have fallen faster than long-end yields following the weak July payrolls print and moderating inflation data. A steeper curve at this level is broadly consistent with improved economic growth expectations and reduced recessionary pressure, though the FOMC minutes could prompt a re-rating of the curve's shape if committee commentary signals a more cautious or hawkish stance than currently priced.

Section 6
Currencies

EUR/USD trades at 1.1578, reflecting sustained euro strength as markets scale back Federal Reserve tightening expectations and the dollar softens on the rate-cut pricing narrative. USD/JPY holds at 159.74, hovering near the psychologically significant 160 level that has historically attracted scrutiny from Japanese monetary authorities. The Bank of Japan has previously intervened verbally and directly to support the yen at elevated levels; a sustained break above 160 could reinvigorate intervention discussions. The yen's weakness reflects the persistent policy divergence between the Fed — still in hold mode — and the BoJ, which remains on a gradual normalisation path.

Section 7
Commodities

Gold trades at $4,398.77/oz, approaching the $4,400 psychological threshold in a rally driven by a weakening US dollar, declining real yields, and broad safe-haven demand amid geopolitical uncertainty. Precious metals miners have benefited disproportionately, with gold streaming companies like Wheaton Precious Metals (WPM) reporting record H1 revenues of US$1.8 billion. WTI crude stands at $84.77/bbl, supported by ongoing OPEC+ production discipline and stronger-than-expected demand signals from Asian markets. Energy sector stocks tracking long-term contracts and oil price exposure — including names in offshore services and midstream infrastructure — have attracted renewed interest as oil holds above $80.
