  • Fed holds, markets pivot: The Federal Reserve kept rates unchanged at its July 30 meeting amid persistent above-target inflation and below-expectation GDP growth. After an initial three-day losing streak, investors quickly re-focused on earnings momentum and pushed equities sharply higher.

Section 2
Macro

The Fed's July 30 decision to hold rates steady underscores the central bank's continued vigilance on inflation, even as GDP growth trails expectations. Chair Powell's commentary reinforced a "higher for longer" posture, with the committee signalling it needs sustained disinflation before pivoting to cuts. Meanwhile, geopolitical pressures — particularly in the Middle East — continue to prop up energy prices, adding an inflationary impulse that complicates the Fed's path. The Eurozone, by contrast, is showing incremental improvement, as reflected in strong European bank results and a firmer euro.

Section 3
Equities

The S&P 500 (SPY) advanced 1.68% to close at $741.69, snapping a three-session losing streak as earnings momentum overwhelmed policy uncertainty. The Nasdaq 100 (QQQ) outperformed with a 3.30% gain to $683.55, its strongest session in several weeks, powered by Microsoft's double-digit surge. Small-caps (IWM) gained 1.39% to $292.59, reflecting broad-based risk appetite extending beyond mega-cap technology.

Notable movers (July 30):

  • MSFT +15.51% to $451.10 (vol: 109M shares) — blowout cloud and AI revenue beat drove one of the largest single-day gains for the stock in years.
  • INTC +11.30% to $91.13 (vol: 136M shares) — surprise profitability recovery provided relief to investors who had written off Intel's turnaround.
Section 4
Volatility

The CBOE VIX settled at 17.09 on July 30, tumbling 3.57 points (−20.89%) from the prior session's close above 20. The sharp reversal confirms that last week's spike in implied volatility was driven primarily by Fed uncertainty and pre-earnings anxiety rather than systemic risk. At 17.09, the VIX sits in neutral territory — above the "complacent" sub-15 threshold but well clear of the "elevated fear" zone above 20. Options markets are now pricing a materially calmer near-term outlook, consistent with the broader risk-on tone across equities.

Section 5
Fixed Income

The 10-year Treasury yield stands at 4.67% (as of July 29), while the 2-year yield is at 4.22%, producing a positive spread of approximately +45 basis points. The curve shape is modestly steepening — a constructive signal relative to the deep inversions seen in 2022–2023 — but elevated absolute yield levels reflect the market's expectation that the Fed will remain on hold for an extended period. The Fed's hawkish hold does not alter the near-term fixed income picture materially; duration risk remains a consideration given persistent inflation and ongoing Treasury supply pressures.

Section 6
Currencies

EUR/USD trades at 1.1519, with the euro well-supported by improving Eurozone economic data and strong earnings from major European banks — Santander (+5.16%) and BBVA reported robust Q2 results earlier in the week. The single currency's strength also reflects relative optimism about the ECB's policy trajectory versus the Fed's hawkish pause. USD/JPY remains elevated at 160.39, as the yen continues to struggle against the backdrop of the Bank of Japan's accommodative stance. The yen's persistent weakness at these levels adds pressure on Japanese authorities and keeps intervention risk on the table.

Section 7
Commodities

Gold commands a spot price of $4,077.50/oz, holding above the psychologically significant $4,000 level driven by a combination of geopolitical safe-haven demand, central bank accumulation, and ongoing dollar-hedging amid fiscal uncertainty. WTI crude stands at $84.25/bbl (as of July 27), elevated from mid-year lows near $69. Oil's recent strength is attributed to Middle East supply concerns and OPEC+ production discipline; however, the hawkish Fed stance and demand uncertainty cap the upside. Consumer lender stress — highlighted in Thursday's news flow — may signal softening demand ahead.
