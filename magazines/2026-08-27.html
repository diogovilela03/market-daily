Macro

The Treasury market provided a mildly constructive signal on Wednesday as both the 10Y (4.64%) and 2Y (4.17%) yields edged lower from prior-day levels, with the 2-year falling from 4.24% — potentially reflecting modest repricing of near-term Fed cut expectations. Equities

U.S. large-cap equity indices ended near flat on Wednesday: SPY +0.02% ($766.08), QQQ +0.09% ($711.37), with small-cap IWM dipping -0.10% ($298.93) as risk appetite waned at the margins ahead of a packed earnings calendar. The session's standout movers:

  • ANF (Abercrombie & Fitch) +35.6% ($147.69): A blockbuster earnings beat sent shares surging to new highs, cementing its position as a best-in-class specialty retailer defying broader consumer caution. Expected move was far exceeded.
  • SNAP -8.5% ($5.42): Snap's miss on revenue and guidance drew heavy selling (68M shares traded), reflecting the ongoing weakness in social media advertising budgets from mid-tier advertisers.

NVDA (-1.6%, $209.66) was the session's most actively traded name on 128M shares as the market pre-positioned for its post-close results. Marvell Technology (MRVL) faces similar scrutiny with its own earnings due post-market today.

Section 4
Volatility

The CBOE Volatility Index (VIX) stands at 15.21, down 1.58% on the session, solidly in complacent territory. A reading below 15.5 signals the options market is pricing in limited near-term risk, even as Nvidia's earnings — a catalyst that could meaningfully reprice the entire AI narrative — land after the bell. Historically, a VIX in the mid-teens ahead of major single-stock catalysts (particularly mega-cap tech) can compress quickly if the print disappoints and implied volatility in index options catches up. The current low-VIX backdrop provides little cushion if sentiment shifts abruptly.

Section 5
Fixed Income

The U.S. Treasury yield curve continues its steepening trajectory. The 10-year yield stands at 4.64% (down from 4.70% on Aug 24), while the 2-year yield is at 4.17% (down from 4.24%), producing a 10Y–2Y spread of +47 basis points — a positively sloped curve. This steepening dynamic, driven by falling short-end yields more than long-end rates, suggests the market is beginning to price a modestly more dovish Fed near-term path while keeping longer inflation and growth expectations anchored. The higher absolute level of yields continues to weigh on housing, growth stocks, and consumer credit, even as the structural inversion that triggered recession concerns has fully resolved.

A continuation below $82 would put the demand-slowdown narrative more firmly in focus.
