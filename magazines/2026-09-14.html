Section 02

Macro

The US labor market is delivering an uncomfortable surprise for rate-cut bulls: Friday's jobs report printed well ahead of expectations, reinforcing the Federal Reserve's "higher for longer" posture and pushing market-implied rate hike probabilities materially higher. The 10-year Treasury yield's advance toward 5% is the direct consequence — a psychologically significant threshold that, if broken durably, could trigger broader equity multiple compression, particularly in longer-duration growth and housing-linked names. Geopolitically, no acute escalation is driving markets, but persistent inflation, a hawkish Fed, and a resilient-but-bifurcating labor market define the macro backdrop for the week ahead.

Section 04

Volatility

The CBOE VIX closed at 15.84, down -2.00 pts (-12.63%) on the session — a reading in the complacent zone (below 20). Despite the macro overhang from rising Fed rate hike odds and a 10-year yield pushing toward 5%, implied volatility is subdued, suggesting options markets are not pricing in near-term stress. Complacency at these levels, particularly with a potentially hawkish FOMC meeting cycle approaching, warrants attention: VIX at sub-16 can transition quickly when macro catalysts materialize.

Section 05

Fixed Income

The US Treasury curve is positively sloped and steepening: the 10-year yield stands at 4.95% while the 2-year sits at 4.56%, producing a 2s10s spread of +39 basis points. The curve has un-inverted from the prolonged inversion that dominated 2023–2024, and the ongoing steepening reflects markets pricing out near-term recession risk while simultaneously building in the possibility of a Fed tightening resumption. The 10-year approaching 5% is the key level to watch: a sustained break above would likely increase pressure on equity valuations — particularly rate-sensitive and growth segments — and could attract fresh buying from yield-hungry institutional allocators.
