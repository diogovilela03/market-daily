Equities softened on Thursday as small caps bore the brunt of a broad retreat, with the Russell 2000 shedding 1.6% while large-cap indices posted modest declines — a divergence that reflects mounting sensitivity to elevated Treasury yields holding near 5%. Crude oil extended its sharp descent from October highs above $107, while gold approaches the $3,900 milestone as markets recalibrate ahead of a pivotal earnings week anchored by NVIDIA, Home Depot, and Walmart.

Section 01
Market Drivers
  • Small-cap underperformance: IWM shed 1.64% versus SPY's 0.35% decline, underscoring growing stress among smaller, rate-sensitive companies with floating-rate debt obligations facing a 10-year yield near 5%.
  • Tech sector selling: NVIDIA fell 4.34% and Super Micro Computer (SMCI) plunged 11.3% ahead of NVIDIA's Q3 earnings on November 17, with investors reducing risk in AI-infrastructure names after a prolonged run-up.
  • Yield headwinds persist: The 10-year Treasury yield settled at 4.98% — approaching the psychologically significant 5% level — reinforcing the "higher for longer" narrative and weighing on equity valuations, particularly in growth.
  • Oil demand concerns: WTI crude slipped below $90, extending a sharp correction from late-October highs near $107, as softening global manufacturing data and rising US inventories offset OPEC+ supply discipline.
Section 02
Macro

Treasury yields remain elevated, with the 10-year at 4.98% and the 2-year at 4.73%, sustaining a modest +25bp positive spread that signals a normalizing but still-tight curve after an extended inversion. The Federal Reserve's restrictive posture continues to anchor front-end rates, with market participants watching incoming retail data — from this week's wave of consumer-sector earnings — as a bellwether for whether household demand is finally cracking under the weight of prolonged high rates. Geopolitically, a firmer dollar, with USD/JPY pressing 160, adds pressure on emerging-market currencies and reinforces the "dollar wrecking ball" dynamic for global capital flows.

Section 03
Equities

The S&P 500 (SPY) fell 0.35% to $757.12, and the Nasdaq 100 (QQQ) declined 0.32% to $688.17 on Thursday — contained losses at the index level, but the Russell 2000 (IWM) told a different story, dropping 1.64% to $278.44 and flagging elevated credit stress among smaller issuers. Among notable movers, NVIDIA (NVDA) fell 4.34% to $166.19 on heavy volume ahead of its fiscal Q3 report on November 17, where consensus expects EPS of $2.49; the stock has become a bellwether for AI capex confidence. Super Micro Computer (SMCI) extended its recent decline with an 11.3% drop to $26.99, as governance concerns and competitive pressure from white-label ODMs continue to weigh on sentiment.

Section 04
Fixed Income

The US 10-year Treasury yield climbed to 4.98%, up 10 basis points from the prior session's 4.88%, approaching the 5% threshold that rattled markets in October. The 2-year yield rose to 4.73%, leaving the curve at a +25bp slope — positive territory after a prolonged inversion, but insufficient to signal a meaningful easing in financial conditions. The steepness of the recent move in long-end yields suggests the market is re-pricing the neutral rate higher, a development that could force a re-evaluation of equity risk premiums if the 10-year decisively breaks above 5% and holds.

Section 05
Currencies

EUR/USD trades at 1.1069, holding steady as European growth data has shown modest resilience, though ECB rate-cut expectations continue to cap upside versus a Fed that remains on hold. USD/JPY sits at 159.85, uncomfortably close to the 160 level that has historically triggered verbal intervention and speculation of Bank of Japan action; yen weakness persists as the carry trade remains attractive given the wide US-Japan rate differential, with the BOJ's incremental normalization pace still lagging market expectations.

Section 06
Commodities

Gold advanced to $3,898.34/oz, edging toward the $3,900 mark in a continuation of its structural bull trend driven by central bank reserve diversification, persistent geopolitical risk premiums, and sticky long-term inflation expectations that make real assets attractive. WTI crude oil fell to $89.62/bbl, extending a sharp correction from the $107+ highs recorded in late October — a decline of roughly 15% in under two weeks. The move reflects softening demand signals from Chinese and European manufacturing PMIs, rising US crude inventories, and reduced geopolitical risk premium, though OPEC+ compliance remains a potential floor.

Section 07
Earnings Radar

Next week brings a heavy slate of market-moving reports. NVIDIA on Monday evening is the marquee event — consensus expects $2.49 EPS and investors will scrutinize data-center revenue guidance for signs of AI capex continuation. Home Depot and Walt Disney report the same day, offering reads on consumer durables spending and media/parks demand. Lowe's, Target, and Palo Alto Networks follow on Tuesday, with Walmart capping the week on Wednesday as the definitive read on broad-based consumer health.
