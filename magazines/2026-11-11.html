Macro

EUR/USD is steady at 1.1160, suggesting relative calm in transatlantic rate expectations, while the yen's slide to 158.79 against the dollar keeps the Bank of Japan's intervention calculus in focus. Gold at $4,062 — well above the $4,000 threshold — underscores persistent demand for hard-asset hedges in an environment of elevated nominal rates and geopolitical uncertainty.

Section 3

Equities

All three major US equity benchmarks finished lower on Monday: SPY −0.37% (749.92), QQQ −0.68% (674.76), and IWM −0.68% (280.58). The uniform magnitude of the decline across large-cap, tech, and small-cap indices suggests macro-driven de-risking rather than fundamental differentiation.   • PEPG +92.89% ($4.61) — A catalyst-driven surge on 103 million shares in a small-cap biotech name, underscoring the volatile binary event risk present in lower-capitalisation names during earnings season.
Section 4

Fixed Income

The US Treasury curve is modestly positive: the 10Y at 4.88% and the 2Y at 4.56% produce a 32-basis-point spread, a mild steepening from the prolonged inversion that characterised much of 2024–2025. This configuration suggests the market is pricing a delayed cutting cycle — the front end remains anchored by Fed policy expectations while the long end drifts higher on fiscal supply concerns and resilient growth data. For equity investors, an unambiguous steepening trend (long rates rising faster than short rates) would signal increasing term premium demands and further headwinds for duration-sensitive growth valuations.

Section 5

Currencies

EUR/USD is trading at 1.1160, holding within a stable range that reflects broadly balanced Fed-ECB rate expectations. No significant catalyst is driving the euro at present, leaving the pair in a holding pattern ahead of the next major central bank communication. USD/JPY has extended to 158.79, approaching levels that have historically prompted verbal warnings or direct intervention from Japan's Ministry of Finance. The yen's depreciation continues to reflect the structural divergence between the Fed's hold-and-wait stance and the Bank of Japan's cautious normalisation path; a sharp reversal — triggered by intervention or a Fed pivot signal — could have outsized implications for yen carry trades and cross-asset volatility.

Section 6

Commodities

Gold is trading at $4,062/oz, sustaining its position above the psychologically significant $4,000 level. The metal's resilience in the face of elevated real rates points to continued central bank accumulation and geopolitical demand rather than purely speculative flows. WTI crude last closed at $99.64/bbl (as of November 3), hovering below the $100/barrel threshold — a level that carries both economic and political significance given its impact on headline inflation. Near-term direction for crude will likely be driven by OPEC+ supply discipline, US strategic reserve policy, and any escalation in global geopolitical flashpoints.

Section 7

Earnings Radar

The coming week is headlined by NVIDIA's report on November 17 — the market's de facto proxy for AI-driven capital expenditure cycles. With NVDA already down 5.53% on session, investor anxiety is elevated. Disney and Home Depot on the same morning add consumer-facing context to what will be a high-read-across week.
