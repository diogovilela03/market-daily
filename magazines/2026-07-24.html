Section 4
Volatility

VIX closed at 18.70, up 11.02% from the prior session — a meaningful spike that signals rising options demand for near-term hedging. Intraday, the VIX briefly crossed the psychologically significant 20 level (high of 20.31) before retreating, suggesting acute session-level fear rather than a sustained structural shift. At 18.70, the market remains in the zone between complacency (<15) and elevated fear (>20), indicating cautious positioning ahead of a historically dense earnings week. The 30-day implied volatility index (IV30) sits at 90.53, unchanged in direction, pointing to a concentrated near-term event premium rather than systemic risk repricing.

Section 5
Fixed Income

The US Treasury curve is positively sloped: the 10-year yield stands at 4.67% and the 2-year at 4.31%, producing a 10Y–2Y spread of +36 basis points. This is a steepening configuration from the deep inversion seen in 2023–2024, suggesting market participants are pricing a gradual normalization of monetary policy rather than an imminent pivot. The persistently high short end continues to reflect the Fed's restrictive stance, keeping refinancing costs elevated for variable-rate corporate and consumer borrowers. The recent tick-up in 10-year yields (from 4.55% on July 17 to 4.67% on July 22) adds marginal pressure on equity valuations via the discount rate channel.

Section 6
Currencies

EUR/USD trades at 1.1394, sustaining euro strength as narrowing transatlantic rate differentials and improving eurozone macro data support the single currency. An EU interest rate decision this week is a near-term catalyst to watch. USD/JPY at 163.71 reflects the yen near multi-decade lows — the Bank of Japan's cautious normalisation pace lags Fed-level rates by a wide margin, keeping carry-trade dynamics alive and the yen structurally weak. Sustained dollar softness at these levels provides a tailwind for commodities denominated in USD and supports the earnings of US multinationals reporting offshore revenue.

Section 7
Commodities

Gold at $4,045.67/oz sustains its breakout above the $4,000 milestone, driven by persistent central-bank accumulation, dollar softness, and the asset's role as a hedge against both geopolitical risk and long-term currency debasement. Gold miners face a dual narrative: elevated gold prices support a structural earnings upgrade cycle, though rising operating costs (energy, labour) risk tempering margin expansion. WTI crude at $84.38/bbl (as of July 20) has recovered sharply from sub-$70 levels seen in early July, reflecting OPEC+ supply discipline holding and incremental demand recovery signals from Asia. The rebound in crude adds modest inflationary pressure to the macro picture and is a tailwind for the energy sector entering earnings season.
