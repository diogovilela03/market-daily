Macro

The Federal Reserve remains in a data-dependent holding pattern with the 10-year Treasury yield anchored at 5.00% — a level that has historically served as a key inflection point for equity risk appetite. The 2Y–10Y curve carries a +32 basis-point positive spread, reflecting a normalised (non-inverted) term structure after the extended inversion of 2024–2025. Oil prices above $107 per barrel add a persistent inflation risk that complicates the path to easier monetary policy, while the dollar's moderate weakness against the euro (EUR/USD 1.1119) suggests the market is not pricing aggressive further Fed tightening from current levels.

Section 4
Fixed Income

The 10-year Treasury yield stands at 5.00%, an elevated but psychologically significant threshold that markets have been testing and consolidating around in recent sessions. The 2-year yield sits at 4.68%, producing a +32 basis-point positive spread — a curve that is steepening modestly from prior near-flatness. The un-inversion of the 2Y–10Y spread that unfolded through 2025–2026 reflects a market beginning to price more normal growth and inflation expectations, though both yields remain historically elevated. A 5-handle on the long end continues to act as a drag on rate-sensitive sectors including utilities, real estate, and high-duration tech.

Section 5
Currencies

EUR/USD trades at 1.1119, reflecting a reasonably firm euro against the dollar — consistent with the ECB maintaining a restrictive stance and the eurozone avoiding the worst-case energy and growth scenarios feared in prior years. USD/JPY at 157.97 keeps the yen under pressure as the wide rate differential between the Fed and the Bank of Japan persists, though the pair has been a focal point for potential BOJ intervention at elevated levels. A break above 160 in USD/JPY would likely reignite intervention speculation and currency volatility.

Section 6
Commodities

Gold at $4,034.71/oz has decisively broken through and consolidated above the $4,000 psychologically important level, driven by a combination of persistent central bank buying, geopolitical hedging demand, and diminishing confidence in fiat currencies as rates normalize. The metal's elevation alongside higher equities suggests markets are simultaneously pricing growth optimism and tail-risk protection. WTI crude at $107.89/bbl (latest available: Oct 20) reflects sustained tightness in global oil supply, with OPEC+ output discipline and structurally higher demand from emerging markets keeping prices in an elevated range. Both commodities are providing notable support to the energy and materials complex.
