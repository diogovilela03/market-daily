Section 1

Market Drivers

  • Jobs shock reprices the Fed curve: July payrolls unexpectedly contracted while wage growth undershot forecasts, eliminating residual bets on a September rate hike. The surprise was large enough to move markets decisively—growth stocks led the rally as the front end of the Treasury curve rallied sharply.
  • Gold and miners in historic run: Spot gold has surged over 7% on the week, driving gold miners (VanEck GDX +21%) to their best weekly performance since 2008. The rally is underpinned by falling real yields, dollar weakness, and safe-haven positioning ahead of Fed recalibration.
  • Tech diverges on AI spend signals: Ciena (CIEN) surged 12.38% on new AI networking contracts and rising data-centre infrastructure demand, while Arm Holdings (ARM) slid 4.64% amid investor concerns about cooling hyperscale capex and stretched valuations.
  • Financials at record highs: Bank of America (BAC) continued its record-setting run after Q2 earnings delivered an 8% EPS beat and strong investment banking revenue, reflecting an improving capital markets backdrop as rate volatility subsides.
Section 2

Macro

The July payrolls contraction marks a meaningful inflection in the economic narrative—moving the debate squarely from "when will the Fed hike?" to "when will the Fed cut?" With wage growth undershooting and labour-market conditions visibly softening, the Fed faces a classic dilemma: inflation remains above target, but the jobs market is signalling real stress. Market participants are now pricing a first cut significantly earlier than the Fed's own projections suggested just weeks ago. EUR/USD above 1.1555 and gold near all-time highs confirm the dollar is absorbing the adjustment. The macro backdrop has shifted from "higher for longer" to a tentative soft-landing narrative—but the next CPI print will be the decisive arbitrator.

Section 3

Equities

US equities closed broadly higher as the jobs shock triggered a rotation into rate-sensitive growth. SPY +0.70% (777.88), QQQ +1.16% (732.07) outperformed as mega-cap tech and software re-rated on lower rate expectations, while IWM +0.26% (303.50) saw a more modest small-cap response—suggesting some caution around the economic growth read-through. NVIDIA (NVDA) gained 0.54% on continued AI infrastructure optimism, while Super Micro Computer (SMCI) added 4.12% on strong AI server demand.

Notable movers: Leslie's Pool Supply (LESL) crashed 41.67% on earnings disappointment and deteriorating consumer demand signals in discretionary categories. FGI Industries (FGI) surged 147.78% on heavy speculative volume—a reminder that single-day extreme moves in micro-caps can distort sentiment reads.

Section 4

Volatility

VIX stands at 14.64—firmly in the complacent zone (below 15), signalling that options markets are not pricing material tail risk despite a meaningful macro surprise in the payrolls data. The subdued volatility reading is consistent with the risk-on tone: when central bank easing is repriced forward, implied volatility typically compresses as the "bad scenario" moves further out of the money. Traders should note that low VIX environments can see sharp, fast reversals if the next data point (e.g., CPI) contradicts the soft-landing consensus.

Section 5

Fixed Income

The 10-year Treasury yield eased to 4.68% while the 2-year settled at 4.20%, leaving the 10Y–2Y spread at a positive +48 basis points—a normally-shaped, upward-sloping curve. The front end responded more aggressively to the jobs data (2Y falling faster than the 10Y) in a classic bull-steepener pattern, reflecting markets pulling forward their rate-cut expectations. At 48bps, the curve is meaningfully steeper than the inversion seen in recent cycles and signals growing market conviction that the easing cycle has begun, or is imminent. Duration-sensitive assets are benefiting.

Section 6

Currencies

EUR/USD at 1.1556—the euro is trading near multi-month highs against the dollar, as the soft payrolls print removes a key support pillar for the greenback. With the ECB on a cautious easing path and the Fed now facing pressure to follow, the rate-differential trade is compressing in the euro's favour. USD/JPY holds at 159.26—the yen has been notably restrained in its response to the dollar's broad weakness. Persistent uncertainty around the Bank of Japan's policy normalisation trajectory, and carry trade dynamics, are limiting JPY appreciation. A sustained dollar decline would typically offer the yen more support; the absence of it here is worth monitoring.

Section 7

Commodities

Gold at $4,337.78/oz—near all-time highs and accelerating. The combination of collapsing real yields, a weaker dollar, and broad safe-haven rotation is fuelling one of the most powerful gold rallies in recent memory. Gold miners are amplifying the move, with GDX gaining 21% on the week. The structural tailwinds remain intact: central bank buying, dedollarisation trends, and now a renewed dovish Fed posture. WTI crude at $84.77/bbl—oil has benefited from improved demand expectations as rate-cut repricing lifts the growth outlook. Supply discipline from OPEC+ remains a floor, while geopolitical risk in key producing regions continues to underpin prices. The $80–$90 range remains the near-term technical corridor to watch.
