Section 2
Macro

The U.S. 10-year yield at 4.98% sits near multi-year highs, reflecting persistent expectations of an extended restrictive monetary policy stance even as growth concerns accumulate. The 2-year yield at 4.61% leaves the curve modestly positive (+37 bps 10Y–2Y spread), signaling that near-term rate-cut expectations remain restrained while the long end continues to price in resilient nominal growth. With oil above $103/bbl sustaining energy-price inflation, the Fed faces limited room to pivot despite equity market stress, and any further deterioration in risk sentiment this week will sharpen the debate between rate stability and financial-system support.

Section 3
Equities

All three major U.S. equity benchmarks closed sharply lower on Friday, October 30. The S&P 500 proxy (SPY) fell 1.77% to $755.72, the Nasdaq (QQQ) dropped 3.47% to $690.49, and the Russell 2000 small-cap index (IWM) lost 1.65% to $279.33.

Notable movers:

  • Intel (INTC) −28.6% to $65.76 on massive volume (448M shares). The stock suffered its worst single-session collapse in years following earnings results that appears to have included deeply disappointing forward revenue guidance, triggering a cascade of institutional selling. The move knocked the SOXL semiconductor ETF down nearly 25% in sympathy.
  • Nvidia (NVDA) −4.2% to $204.64 on 165M shares. Contagion from the broader semiconductor selloff pressured the AI infrastructure bellwether despite its own business fundamentals remaining intact; the stock remains a key barometer for AI capital expenditure sentiment.
Section 4
Fixed Income

The 10-year U.S. Treasury yield stands at 4.98% as of October 29, within striking distance of the psychologically significant 5% threshold that historically triggers renewed volatility across credit and equity markets. The 2-year note yields 4.61%, placing the 10Y–2Y spread at a positive +37 basis points — the curve is modestly normal and gently steepening, having exited prolonged inversion territory earlier this cycle. The steepening dynamic reflects long-end yields rising faster than the front end, driven by term premium expansion and concerns about fiscal sustainability. A breach of 5% on the 10-year would likely accelerate equity market de-rating, particularly in growth and technology sectors.

Section 5
Currencies

The euro trades at EUR/USD 1.1197, reflecting relative resilience in European risk appetite despite a broadly risk-off global backdrop — a level that suggests the ECB's policy path is viewed as more predictable than the Fed's near-term trajectory. The Japanese yen remains substantially weak at USD/JPY 157.77, a level that has historically prompted verbal or outright intervention by the Bank of Japan; with U.S. yields near 5%, the yen carry trade remains highly attractive, sustaining pressure on JPY. Any sharp reversal in U.S. yields or a BOJ policy surprise could unwind the carry trade abruptly and amplify global volatility.

Section 6
Commodities

Gold spot prices at $3,906.59/oz represent elevated levels consistent with sustained safe-haven and inflation-hedge demand. Gold at nearly $3,907 reflects a confluence of drivers: sticky inflation expectations, geopolitical risk premiums, central bank reserve diversification away from the U.S. dollar, and growing concerns about long-run fiscal trajectories in major economies.

WTI crude oil at $103.37/bbl (as of October 27) remains firmly above $100 — a level that amplifies input-cost inflation across the economy and complicates the Fed's path toward price stability. Supply constraints, whether from OPEC+ discipline or geopolitical disruptions, appear to be outweighing demand concerns in the near term. Elevated energy costs add a stagflationary undertone to the macro picture.
