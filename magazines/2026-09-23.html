Section 01
Market Drivers
  • Fed rate hike ripples through credit markets: The Federal Reserve raised its policy rate for the first time since 2023, prompting major banks — JPMorgan, Citigroup, Wells Fargo, KeyCorp — to immediately lift prime rates. Financials sold off sharply, with Citigroup down 3.0% and Wells Fargo falling 3.9%.
  • Tech and AI outperform on rotation: Investors moved capital from rate-sensitive sectors into technology. QQQ outperformed the broader market (+0.81%), with Micron (MU) crossing the $1,000/share milestone and NVIDIA steadily active on AI data-center demand narratives.
  • Berkshire doubles down on housing distress: Warren Buffett's Berkshire Hathaway quietly built an almost 10% stake in Lennar even as the homebuilder reported weak earnings — analysts read this as a classic Buffett value play on long-cycle housing recovery.
  • Oil surge clouds the macro picture: WTI crude rising to $107/bbl is adding to inflation concerns and squeezing energy-consuming sectors, even as energy producers face IEA demand downgrades. The interplay between higher rates and energy prices is the key tension the market is navigating.
Section 02
Macro

The Federal Reserve's return to rate hikes — its first since 2023 — marks a decisive hawkish pivot driven by persistent inflation and a still-resilient labor market. The 10-year Treasury yield sits at 4.96%, close to multi-year highs, as the market reprices for a "higher for longer" path; the 2-year yield is unchanged at 4.76%, leaving the curve mildly positive (+20bps) and suggesting the market is not yet pricing a deep recession. With WTI crude above $107 and the U.S. dollar holding firm against the yen at 157.73, the macro backdrop remains one of late-cycle tightening: robust activity, sticky inflation, and rising cost-of-capital pressure on leveraged segments of the economy.

Section 03
Equities

The S&P 500 proxy (SPY) barely moved on the session (-0.02%), masking a sharp rotation beneath the surface: tech lifted QQQ by +0.81% while small caps (IWM +0.57%) also outperformed, supported by anticipation of continued domestic demand strength ahead of next week's earnings cycle. The large-cap index is effectively pinned near record highs as opposing forces — sector selloffs in banks and homebuilders versus tech gains — offset each other.

Notable movers:

  • JAGX +1,190.6% ($34.46): Top gainer by far — Jaguar Health surged on extraordinary volume of 22.9M shares, likely on a clinical or regulatory catalyst not yet fully captured in public filings.
  • SOS -54.4% ($0.35): The crypto-mining operator collapsed amid elevated interest rates squeezing mining economics, and broader sector pressure as digital asset sentiment remained subdued without a fresh macro catalyst.
Section 04
Volatility

The CBOE VIX closed at 14.21, falling 4.64% on the session and sitting firmly in complacent territory (below 15). Despite the Fed's unexpected rate hike and elevated crude oil, implied volatility in the options market is remarkably subdued — a sign that institutional participants are either well-hedged or do not yet anticipate a sharp drawdown. The risk is that low VIX readings at the tail end of a hiking cycle often precede sudden spikes; traders should be alert to any deterioration in credit spreads or labor data that could jolt complacency.

Section 05
Fixed Income

The U.S. Treasury curve is mildly steepening: the 10-year yield eased 5bps to 4.96% while the 2-year was unchanged at 4.76%, leaving the 2s10s spread at +20bps. The curve's return to positive territory — after a prolonged inversion — historically signals a transition phase: the short end may be anchored by Fed guidance while the long end drifts higher on inflation and supply pressures. The 10-year approaching 5% is a pivotal psychological level; a sustained break above it would tighten financial conditions further and add meaningful pressure to equities' valuations.

Section 06
Currencies

EUR/USD is trading at 1.1428, holding near multi-month highs as the euro benefits from improving European activity data and a narrowing rate differential relative to the ECB's own tightening path. USD/JPY, meanwhile, sits at a still-elevated 157.73 — well above the 150 level that previously triggered Bank of Japan intervention anxiety. The yen's sustained weakness reflects the stark divergence between the Fed's hawkish stance and the BoJ's relatively accommodative posture. A further yen weakening toward 160 would likely reignite intervention chatter from Tokyo.

Section 07
Commodities

Gold spot is pricing at a historically elevated $4,326/oz, underpinned by central bank accumulation, persistent inflation, and demand for alternative stores of value in a high-rate environment. Unlike prior cycles, gold is rising alongside real yields — a structural shift suggesting safe-haven demand and de-dollarization flows are overriding the traditional inverse relationship with Treasury yields.

WTI crude surged to $107.02/bbl (as of September 15), up roughly 4.5% from the prior session, driven by a combination of OPEC+ supply discipline, Middle East geopolitical risk premiums, and robust U.S. summer demand. The IEA's downward revision to global demand growth has so far failed to meaningfully cap prices. Above $100 oil is acting as a headwind to consumer spending and a complicating factor for central banks seeking to cool inflation without over-tightening.
