Section 02
Macro

The U.S. yield curve is modestly positively sloped, with the 10-year at 5.22% and the 2-year at 4.75%, producing a +47 basis point spread — a mild steepening that suggests markets are pricing in a higher long-run nominal rate rather than signaling imminent recession. WTI crude at $96.24 and gold at $4,188.82 together paint an inflationary picture that limits the Fed's room to ease materially; with no scheduled Fed decision or major data release (CPI, NFP) in the next 48 hours, today's bank earnings will serve as the primary macro signal for markets. Dollar strength remains a theme, particularly against the yen (USD/JPY at 158.16), reflecting the divergence between the Fed's restrictive stance and the Bank of Japan's continued accommodative posture.

Section 03
Equities

U.S. equities closed lower across the board on Monday, October 12. The large-cap S&P 500 proxy SPY dipped just 0.06% to $778.10, while the Nasdaq-heavy QQQ underperformed at -0.70% to $746.04. Small-cap IWM fell 0.37% to $277.90, reflecting broader risk-off sentiment but contained within a narrow range. Among the session's notable movers, MNDR surged 257.65% to $3.04 on extraordinary volume of 190 million shares, while LNKS added 138.37% to $2.27 on heavy turnover — both driven by likely company-specific catalysts. On the downside, Intel (INTC) declined 3.20% to $101.35 as the chip sector faced renewed selling pressure; DKI collapsed 58.15% to $0.14 in a sharp de-rating move.

Section 04
Fixed Income

The U.S. Treasury curve sits at the following levels: the 2-year yield at 4.75% and the 10-year at 5.22%, with a positive spread of +47 basis points. This modest steepening represents a gradual unwind from the deep inversion seen in 2023–2024, and suggests markets are cautiously repricing long-term growth expectations upward even as near-term rate cuts remain elusive. At 5.22%, the 10-year yield imposes significant competition against equities on a risk-adjusted basis, and the elevated carry cost continues to weigh on highly leveraged segments of the economy. Bond markets will be closely watching today's bank earnings for guidance on credit quality and loan demand.

Section 05
Currencies

EUR/USD trades at 1.1187, with the euro holding firm against the dollar, reflecting relative euro-zone resilience and perhaps some front-running of any ECB policy divergence from the Fed. USD/JPY at 158.16 continues to reflect profound yen weakness — a consequence of the Bank of Japan's persistently accommodative stance against the Fed's much tighter policy framework. At these levels, USD/JPY is approaching territory that historically invites verbal or actual intervention from Japanese monetary authorities, making it a key pair to monitor for potential sharp reversals. The dollar's broad strength against EM and commodity currencies is an ongoing headwind for risk assets priced in those currencies.

Section 06
Commodities

Gold spot at $4,188.82 per troy ounce remains near multi-year highs, sustained by a combination of central bank accumulation, geopolitical risk premiums, and demand for a real-asset hedge against persistent above-target inflation. The yellow metal's strength despite a resilient dollar and elevated real yields is notable, suggesting structural rather than purely cyclical demand drivers. WTI crude oil at $96.24 per barrel is approaching the psychologically significant $100 level, with tight OPEC+ supply discipline and resilient global demand keeping energy prices elevated. Persistent oil above $90 remains a meaningful pass-through risk for headline CPI, further complicating the Fed's path.
