Section 2
Macro

The Federal Reserve appears on hold for September, with resilient PCE inflation and a still-tight labor market leaving little room for an early pivot. Rate uncertainty continues to drive investors toward defensive positioning — life insurers and banks with higher-yielding assets are benefiting from the duration-risk repricing, while growth-sensitive sectors like housing and consumer discretionary remain under pressure. Geopolitically, global supply chains show gradual normalization, though energy markets remain sensitive to OPEC+ production signals.

Equities

All three major US equity benchmarks closed higher Tuesday: SPY +0.32% to 765.91, QQQ +0.62% to 710.72 (outperforming on tech strength ahead of NVDA earnings), and IWM +0.42% to 299.23, with small-caps staging a modest recovery. Among notable movers from the daily gainers and actives list, AIXI surged +186% to $1.31 on 239 million shares — a speculative small-cap AI infrastructure name — while NVDA led large-caps with a +2.19% gain to $213.05, easily the most consequential move of the session given tonight's earnings.

Section 4
Volatility

VIX: 15.45 (−2.59% on the session). Markets remain broadly complacent — VIX holding below 16 ahead of the most market-moving earnings release of the quarter signals that options markets are not pricing in extreme tail risk. A VIX print below 15 would signal full complacency; the current level is consistent with a market that expects NVDA to deliver broadly in line with expectations. A significant miss could spike VIX sharply from this low base.

Section 5
Fixed Income

The 10-year Treasury yield stands at 4.70%, the highest recent level in the current cycle, while the 2-year yields 4.24% — a spread of +46 basis points. The curve maintains a normal upward slope, a reversal from the prolonged inversion of 2023–2024. Higher long-end yields are supporting income-oriented sectors (life insurers, structured products) while continuing to weigh on rate-sensitive equities including utilities, REITs, and homebuilders. The steepening reflects growing term premium as fiscal concerns keep duration supply elevated.
