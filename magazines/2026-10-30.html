Macro

The US yield curve is in a modest steepening mode, with the 10-year at 5.05% and the 2-year at 4.67% — a positive spread of 38 basis points that signals the market expects long-run rates to remain higher for longer without pricing in imminent recession. The Federal Reserve's posture remains explicitly data-dependent and restrictive; with no FOMC meeting until November, the next major catalyst is payrolls and CPI. Geopolitically, the OPEC+ coalition has held output discipline firm, keeping crude north of $100 and reinforcing the "higher energy prices, stickier inflation" narrative that keeps rate cuts off the near-term table.

Fixed Income

US Treasury yields remain in restrictive territory: the 10-year note sits at 5.05% (+6bps from Wednesday's close), and the 2-year yield eased modestly to 4.67% (−3bps). The curve's 2s10s spread stands at +38bps, firmly in positive territory — a steepening dynamic that reflects the market's view that near-term growth risk is limited but long-run inflation expectations are drifting higher. With the Fed on hold and no October FOMC meeting, duration risk remains elevated heading into next week's key macro prints.
