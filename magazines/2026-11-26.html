Section 2
Macro

Treasury yields ticked fractionally lower to 4.91% on the 10-year benchmark (from 4.92% the prior session) while the 2-year held steady at 4.63%, maintaining a +28bp positive slope. The curve has gradually re-steepened from its prior inversion phase, though the spread remains historically compressed — a signal that markets are not yet pricing a dramatic Fed pivot. The Fed's higher-for-longer posture appears embedded in front-end rates; without clear disinflation evidence in upcoming CPI and PCE prints, the bar for meaningful easing remains high.

USD/JPY at 159.17 reflects the persistent Fed-BoJ policy divergence. As the pair approaches the 160 level — a threshold that has previously triggered Bank of Japan verbal and direct intervention — currency risk merits close attention. Any BoJ pivot or surprise rate action could generate sharp reversal dynamics across yen crosses and global risk assets.

Section 4
Fixed Income

The 10-year US Treasury yield stands at 4.91% and the 2-year at 4.63%, placing the 10Y–2Y spread at +28 basis points — a modestly positive slope that marks a significant shift from the prolonged inversion seen in the prior cycle. The curve's re-steepening has been driven more by the long end rising than by the short end falling, which is a less constructive dynamic for equities than a bull-steepening scenario would be.

At these yield levels, duration risk remains elevated: 10-year Treasuries remain well below their historical fair-value range on some models, and any further supply-driven or inflation-driven push higher would meaningfully pressure equity multiples, particularly in long-duration growth names. The holiday-shortened week ahead may see thin liquidity amplify moves in fixed income when US markets reopen Friday.

Section 5
Currencies

EUR/USD at 1.0974: The euro holds just below the 1.10 handle, finding relative stability against the dollar despite persistent European growth headwinds. ECB rate-cut expectations continue to weigh, while dollar softness from any shift in US rate expectations could provide periodic support. The pair remains in a broadly rangebound pattern, with 1.10 as near-term topside resistance.

USD/JPY at 159.17: The yen continues to weaken materially, approaching levels that have historically prompted Bank of Japan intervention. The pair is trading roughly 20 yen above where it was a year ago, driven by the stark divergence between the Fed's restrictive stance and the BoJ's ultra-accommodative policy. Any BoJ communication shift or unscheduled rate action could trigger a rapid and volatile reversal; traders should monitor BoJ officials' remarks closely. The 160 level remains a critical threshold to watch.

Section 6
Commodities

Gold at $3,568.08/oz: Bullion remains near historic highs, underpinned by a combination of central bank diversification away from USD reserves, geopolitical risk premia, and persistent inflation hedging demand. The $3,500 level — once considered a distant target — has become a durable floor in recent sessions. The strength of the move suggests structural flows (central bank buying, ETF accumulation) are driving prices as much as any near-term macro catalyst.

WTI crude at $82.24/bbl: Crude edged up from $81.42 the prior session (+1.0%), holding comfortably above the $80 psychological level. OPEC+ supply restraint remains the primary support factor, while demand signals are mixed: US industrial activity has been uneven, and Chinese refinery throughput data provides only a partial offset. The energy complex faces a tug-of-war between supply discipline and demand uncertainty as markets head into the seasonally slower year-end period.
