Section 2
Macro

The 10-year Treasury yield held at 5.15% while the 2-year sat at 4.88%, producing a positively sloped curve (+27bps) that reflects durable near-term policy resilience rather than any imminent pivot. Despite the Fed's restrictive posture, the curve remains well clear of inversion, suggesting markets have absorbed the higher-for-longer narrative without fully pricing a recession. Meanwhile, USD/JPY crossing 159 highlights the sharpening divergence between the Fed's stance and the Bank of Japan's cautious normalization path — a dynamic that continues to generate currency volatility and cross-asset spillovers. Gold near record highs in a rising real-yield environment underscores an unusual structural demand story driven by central bank diversification and geopolitical hedging.

Section 3
Equities

All three major index ETFs closed lower Monday: SPY fell 1.91% to $739.95, QQQ dropped 2.67% to $661.75, and IWM declined 1.66% to $272.94, reflecting broad risk-off positioning. Small-caps' relative resilience vs. QQQ confirms the selloff was disproportionately concentrated in mega-cap technology. NVIDIA (NVDA) fell 4.12% to $156.14 on volume exceeding 326 million shares — investors actively repositioning ahead of the company's Q3 results after the bell Tuesday. Intel (INTC) dropped 7.07% to $55.44 on 99 million shares, with selling pressure accelerating amid ongoing concerns about its AI accelerator roadmap and market share loss to AMD and NVIDIA.

Section 4
Fixed Income

The 10-year Treasury yield stands at 5.15% and the 2-year at 4.88%, a spread of +27bps reflecting a modestly upward-sloping curve. The curve has not inverted — reversing a multi-year inversion that defined the 2023–2024 rate cycle — and the ongoing steepening suggests markets are beginning to price some medium-term easing, even as near-term Fed expectations remain anchored. Yields above 5% on the long end continue to act as a consistent drag on equity multiples, particularly for high-duration growth names. The persistence of elevated yields despite market stress underscores the Fed's credibility in its fight against lingering inflation pressures.

Section 5
Currencies

EUR/USD held near 1.1008, exhibiting limited directional conviction as the euro found modest support from risk-off dollar demand unwinding against a backdrop of still-elevated U.S. yields. USD/JPY advanced to 159.25, with the yen approaching levels that have historically prompted BOJ verbal intervention; 160 represents a key psychological threshold where policymakers may feel compelled to act. The Fed-BOJ policy divergence — with the Fed firmly higher-for-longer while the BOJ normalizes gradually — continues to be the dominant driver of yen weakness, with carry trade dynamics amplifying moves in thin early-week liquidity.

Section 6
Commodities

Gold spot rose to $3,877.12/oz, sustaining its historically unusual rally in a high real-yield environment and pointing to structural demand from central bank diversification away from the dollar, alongside geopolitical risk premiums. The persistence of gold at these levels despite elevated Treasury yields suggests the traditional inverse relationship has been materially disrupted by institutional demand shifts. WTI crude oil's most recent available reading was $89.62/barrel (data through 11 November), with the energy complex reflecting a tense balance between OPEC+ supply restraint and demand concerns tied to slowing global growth, particularly in China.
