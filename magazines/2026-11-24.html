Nvidia's blowout fiscal-quarter earnings detonated one of the most powerful single-session AI-driven rallies in recent memory, lifting all three major index proxies by 3–7% and sending semiconductor proxies soaring by double digits. The move reaffirmed the market's conviction that AI infrastructure spending remains robust — and that NVDA remains its proxy trade of choice.
Section 1
Market Drivers
  • NVDA earnings catalyst: Nvidia surged 31.5% on 772 million shares — roughly 3× its average daily volume — after reporting results that dramatically exceeded consensus estimates, with AI data-centre revenue leading the beat.
  • Semiconductor sympathy rally: AI and chip-adjacent instruments amplified the move: SOXL (3× semi ETF) +40%, NVDL (2× long NVDA) +62%, NVDX +63%, while Intel (INTC) added 16% in a halo-effect trade on 163M shares.
  • Broad risk-on rotation: QQQ's +7% gain versus IWM's +3.6% confirms aggressive rotation into large-cap growth; the spread between the two suggests capital-weight concentration rather than indiscriminate buying.
  • Leveraged volatility: Extreme dislocations in leveraged products (NVDQ −63%, SOXS −41%) underscore the high-conviction, directional nature of the session and the acute pain for short positioning.
Section 2
Macro

The Federal Reserve remains in a data-dependent posture with no near-term policy catalyst on the immediate horizon. Treasury yields eased modestly — the 10-year down 12 basis points to 4.92% and the 2-year off 13bps to 4.63% — a mild flight from safety as capital rotated decisively into equities. With the yield curve in modest steepening territory and the AI-driven earnings cycle re-accelerating, the macro backdrop offers few countervailing headwinds to the equity bull case in the near term, though the absolute level of yields remains a persistent drag on rate-sensitive sectors and valuation multiples.

Section 3
Equities

SPY gained 4.71% to close at 722.29; QQQ surged 7.00% to 651.03; IWM added 3.56% to 265.74. The session's dominant mover was NVDA (+31.5%, 772M shares), the highest-volume US equity of the session by a wide margin and a defining catalyst for the broader tape. As a notable gainer, INTC (+16.3%, 163M shares) benefited from semiconductor sympathy flows. On the losing side, NVD (3× inverse NVDA, −63%) and NVDQ (2× inverse NVDA, −63%) suffered severe drawdowns as short-side pressure unwound rapidly — a signal of how heavily the inverse-NVDA trade had been positioned heading into earnings.

Section 4
Fixed Income

The US 10-year Treasury yield stands at 4.92% (down 12bps from the prior session's 5.04%), while the 2-year yields 4.63% (down 13bps from 4.76%). The resulting +29bp positive slope (10Y minus 2Y) represents a steepening curve and marks a continuation of the un-inversion dynamic that began earlier this year. Despite the equity euphoria, long-end yields remain historically elevated — a reflection of persistent "higher for longer" Fed expectations and ongoing term-premium repricing. No inversion signal present.

Section 5
Currencies

EUR/USD is holding at 1.0905, maintaining the 1.09 handle in a consolidative pattern that reflects a broadly steady dollar environment despite the equity surge. USD/JPY sits at 159.27, near multi-month highs for the pair, as yen weakness persists amid the Bank of Japan's cautious pace of policy normalisation versus US rate resilience. The 160.00 level warrants close monitoring — a threshold historically associated with Bank of Japan intervention risk. Any breach could trigger rapid yen support operations and cross-asset volatility.

Section 6
Commodities

Gold is trading at $3,622.57/oz, sustaining elevated levels that reflect a confluence of persistent geopolitical risk premia, central bank reserve diversification demand, and the broader de-dollarisation trend among emerging market economies. WTI crude is at $89.53/bbl (as of the most recent daily close), holding near multi-month highs balanced between OPEC+ supply discipline and softening demand signals from slowing global manufacturing PMIs. Energy markets remain watchful of any escalation in Middle East logistics routes that could add a supply-disruption premium to the front-month contract.
