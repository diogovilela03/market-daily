A broad risk-off session swept US equity markets Wednesday as persistently elevated Treasury yields — the 10-year holding firm at 5.21% — continued to compress growth multiples and force a valuation reset across technology and small caps. Semiconductors bore the brunt of the selling while gold's advance to $4,142 underscored deepening demand for hard-asset hedges.

Section 01
Market Drivers
  • Rates anchor risk appetite: The 10-year Treasury at 5.21% continues to raise the hurdle rate for equities, pressuring growth-oriented sectors and prompting a broad derating across all cap sizes. The positive 42bps spread over 2-year yields signals a steepening curve rather than recession pricing.
  • Tech and semiconductor rout: NVIDIA (NVDA) declined 3.16% while the 3x leveraged semiconductor ETF SOXL collapsed 13.32% on elevated volume, reflecting investor retreat from high-multiple AI-adjacent names in a restrictive rate environment.
  • CoreWeave unwind: CRWV fell 15.46% with over 107 million shares traded, pointing to institutional repositioning in next-generation AI infrastructure stocks that had rallied sharply in prior sessions.
  • Financials under pressure: Bank of America (BAC) dropped 4.71% ahead of Q3 results, raising concerns around net interest margin compression and loan loss provisions as credit conditions tighten heading into year-end.
Section 02
Macro

The Federal Reserve's higher-for-longer posture remains the dominant macro force, with 2-year yields at 4.79% confirming that markets see limited scope for front-end relief in the near term. The 10-year Treasury's hold near 5.21% reflects ongoing term premium repricing driven by fiscal concerns, robust labour market data, and uncertainty around the Fed's terminal rate. The yield curve's steepening to +42bps (10Y minus 2Y) marks a notable shift from the prolonged inversion seen earlier this cycle, suggesting markets are now tentatively pricing in eventual easing without abandoning the restrictive narrative — a precarious balance that keeps equity investors on edge heading into peak Q3 earnings season.

Section 03
Equities

All three major US equity ETFs declined in Wednesday's session: SPY fell 1.08% to $769.60, QQQ dropped 1.97% to $728.95 underscoring tech's outsized sensitivity to rate moves, and IWM slid 1.93% to $271.37 as small-cap companies — more reliant on floating-rate debt — face a tougher borrowing backdrop at 5%+ rates. Among notable movers, CoreWeave (CRWV) was the session's most consequential decliner at -15.46% on massive volume, signalling a potential unwind of crowded AI-infrastructure positioning. Bank of America (BAC) shed 4.71% ahead of its Q3 earnings report, adding to broader financial sector anxiety as investors reassess credit quality and margin outlook in a sustained high-rate environment.

Section 04
Fixed Income

The 10-year Treasury yield stood at 5.21% (as of October 13), while the 2-year yield settled at 4.79%, producing a positive 10Y–2Y spread of approximately 42 basis points. This steepening of the curve represents a meaningful departure from the inversion that characterised much of the 2023–2025 period and carries mixed signals: bulls read it as anticipation of Fed cuts restoring the curve's natural slope; bears see it as a fiscal risk premium asserting itself at the long end. Either way, the 5%+ long-rate continues to act as a gravitational force on equity valuations, particularly for duration-sensitive growth names.

Section 05
Currencies

EUR/USD traded at 1.1218, reflecting a relatively resilient Euro as ECB policy divergence from the Fed becomes a more discussed theme — with European rate cuts already underway versus the Fed's pause. USD/JPY remained elevated at 158.03, sustaining the yen's structural weakness as the Bank of Japan's ultra-loose monetary stance preserves the carry-trade dynamic: with US short rates at 4.79%, the interest rate differential continues to incentivise yen-funded positions in higher-yielding assets, keeping the yen under pressure absent a BoJ pivot signal.

Section 06
Commodities

Gold reached $4,142.17 per troy ounce, maintaining its historic advance as central bank accumulation, safe-haven demand from equity volatility, and concerns over long-term fiat purchasing power underpin the rally. The metal's resilience even as the dollar holds firm underscores structural demand from reserve diversifiers. WTI crude was last reported at $96.24 per barrel (data through October 6), sustaining elevated levels as OPEC+ supply discipline and geopolitical risk premiums in the Middle East counterbalance demand concerns tied to higher global borrowing costs and softer industrial activity in China.
