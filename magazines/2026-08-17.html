Section 2
Macro

The July PPI print came in flat, following a sequence of cooler CPI readings, building consensus around a softening inflation trend and bringing the Federal Reserve closer to an easing pivot. The FOMC minutes, due Wednesday August 19, will reveal the internal debate around the pace and depth of cuts — with the market firmly priced for a September move, any indication of deeper disagreement on the committee could shift expectations materially. Geopolitically, the backdrop remains broadly stable, though persistent U.S. dollar weakness — fed by easing expectations — continues to support gold, emerging market currencies, and commodity-exporting economies.

Section 3
Equities

The major U.S. equity benchmarks ended last Friday in mixed-to-soft territory: SPY dipped -0.20% to $776.34 and QQQ lost -0.14% to $731.07, while IWM (small caps) diverged positively, rising +0.52% to $305.09 — a rotation pattern consistent with markets pricing more imminent Fed easing. Among the session's notable movers, WETO surged +127.7% on volume of 58 million shares, an event-driven outlier in the small-cap space. On the downside, BZAI collapsed -49.3% on 56 million shares, underlining the idiosyncratic volatility still present in speculative tech and AI-adjacent names.

Section 4
Volatility

VIX stands at 14.25, a decline of -2.67% from the prior session and firmly in the "complacent" zone below 15. Options markets are pricing in minimal near-term turbulence, reflecting broad confidence in the soft-landing narrative. However, complacency at this level historically warrants attention — the FOMC minutes on Wednesday represent a plausible catalyst for a volatility re-rating if policymakers signal a slower path to easing than currently discounted.

Section 5
Fixed Income

The 10-year Treasury yield sits at 4.63% and the 2-year at 4.15%, placing the 2Y–10Y spread at a positive +48 basis points. The curve has been modestly steepening in recent sessions — a pattern consistent with pre-easing cycles, where the front end rallies in anticipation of rate cuts while the long end stays anchored on resilient nominal growth expectations. The spread remains well above zero; no inversion or inversion-reversal risk at current levels. Wednesday's FOMC minutes will be the near-term anchor for the 2-year.

Section 6
Currencies

EUR/USD trades at 1.1609, sustained at elevated levels by a broadly weaker U.S. dollar as rate-cut expectations erode the greenback's interest-rate advantage. USD/JPY remains elevated at 158.91, reflecting the Bank of Japan's cautious pace of policy normalization — while the BoJ has begun a tightening cycle, it has yet to accelerate meaningfully, keeping the yen on the back foot. The broad dollar softness is a key macro tailwind for commodities, EM assets, and non-U.S. equities.

Section 7
Commodities

Gold has extended its extraordinary run to $4,402.70/oz, anchored by a combination of U.S. dollar weakness, persistent inflation hedge demand, and sustained central bank accumulation globally. The metal has now broken well above $4,000 — a level that would have been considered extreme only recently — and is finding structural support from investors seeking real assets amid an uncertain rate environment. WTI crude trades at $84.77/bbl, recovering from sub-$80 levels seen earlier in August, supported by OPEC+ supply discipline and tentative improvement in Chinese demand signals. Energy equities remain constructive on the supply-constrained backdrop.
