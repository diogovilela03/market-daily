Section 02
Macro

The Federal Reserve faces a delicate balancing act: May's blowout jobs report triggered rapid repricing toward potential rate hikes rather than cuts, while rising energy prices continue to lift goods and services inflation — confirming the J.P. Morgan CIO's "hot, hot, hot" characterisation of the current environment. Despite the hawkish recalibration, equity markets have recovered strongly, interpreting solid economic data as evidence of demand resilience rather than an imminent policy error. Geopolitical de-escalation via the Israel-Iran ceasefire has provided an additional tailwind, compressing risk premiums across asset classes and sustaining the risk-on bid heading into Q2 earnings season.

Volatility

The CBOE VIX settled at 16.20 on June 15, declining 9.1% on the session as geopolitical risk premiums unwound following the Israel-Iran peace agreement. At 16.20, the index sits in neutral territory — below the >20 distress threshold but above the <15 complacency zone — consistent with a market still pricing residual macro uncertainty despite the renewed risk-on tone. The options market remained notably calm through QQQ's sharp June 5 drop, suggesting professional hedges are well-positioned and the market is not aggressively pricing tail risk at current volatility levels.

Section 05
Fixed Income

The 10-year Treasury yield stands at 4.48% and the 2-year at 4.09% (latest available data as of June 12), producing a 10Y–2Y spread of +39 basis points — a positively sloped and modestly steepening curve. The 10-year at 4.48% reflects the repriced Fed path following May's strong jobs print, which shifted market pricing toward potential hikes. The positive curve slope, however modest, signals improving long-run growth expectations relative to near-term policy risk and marks a meaningful departure from the prolonged inversion that characterised the prior macro regime. Whether this steepening dynamic holds will depend on incoming inflation prints and any Fed forward-guidance shift.

Section 06
Currencies

EUR/USD holds at 1.1594, with the euro maintaining elevated levels supported by improving global risk appetite and market reassessment that the ECB may be closer to the end of its easing cycle than the Fed — a relative policy-divergence narrative that continues to underpin euro strength. USD/JPY is pinned at 160.23, near multi-decade lows for the yen, as the Bank of Japan's accommodative stance sustains a wide yield differential and keeps yen carry trades firmly intact. Japanese authorities are likely monitoring the 160 handle closely for signs requiring intervention, as sustained yen weakness adds import-cost pressure to Japan's domestic inflation picture and risks eroding real purchasing power.

Overall sentiment: Bullish on altcoins (XRP, XLM, ETH) driven by regulatory optimism; cautious on BTC near-term given persistent ETF outflows and geopolitical dependency in the recovery thesis.
