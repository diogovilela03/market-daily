Section 01
Market Drivers
  • Fed hikes for first time since 2023: The Federal Reserve raised rates at its September meeting, prompting JPMorgan, Bank of America, Citigroup, Wells Fargo, and KeyCorp to immediately lift their prime lending rates in lockstep — a signal that borrowing costs across the real economy will rise further.
  • Treasury yields at multi-year highs: The 10-year yield hit 5.01% and the 30-year touched a 19-year high last week, amplifying pressure on housing, growth equities, and leveraged borrowers. The bond selloff that preceded this week's hike has yet to fully reverse.
  • Tech and AI hardware outperform: Micron (MU) surged past $1,000 and semiconductor names broadly outperformed, driven by AI server-driven DRAM demand that is insulating the sector from valuation compression fears. Intel (INTC) gained 7.67% on heavy volume Thursday.
  • Homebuilders rattled: KB Home, Meritage Homes, Hovnanian, and Opendoor all fell sharply this week as higher yields threaten mortgage affordability and delay the timeline to profitability for rate-sensitive real estate models.
Section 02
Macro

The Federal Reserve's resumption of rate hikes after a multi-year pause signals persistent inflation concern, placing the fed funds rate at a new cycle high and forcing markets to reprice terminal rate expectations upward. The 2Y/10Y spread of +27 basis points maintains a positive slope, but both yields are elevated in absolute terms, translating into meaningful debt-service headwinds across the economy. On the geopolitical front, a $22.9 billion Tomahawk missile contract awarded to RTX underscores sustained defense spending, while Bank of America's CEO projected a 10%+ decline in Q3 investment banking fees — a sign that the rate environment is cooling capital markets activity.

Section 03
Equities

All three major indices closed higher Thursday, led by the Nasdaq proxy QQQ (+1.73%), with the broad S&P 500 proxy SPY adding 1.13% and small-cap IWM lagging at +0.53% — consistent with a risk-on move weighted toward large-cap growth. Intel (INTC) surged 7.67% on volume of 147 million shares, its largest single-day gain in months, as enterprise demand signals outweigh rate headwinds for legacy chipmakers pivoting to AI infrastructure. NVIDIA (NVDA) added 2.54% on 92 million shares, sustaining its position as the AI data center bellwether even with the 10-year yield near 5%.

Section 04
Volatility

VIX closed at 15.44, dropping 14.70% from the prior session — approaching the complacency threshold below 15 that historically signals elevated near-term risk of a volatility spike. The sharp decline alongside a moderate equity rally reads more as short-term relief following the Fed announcement than a durable return to risk-on sentiment. With the 10-year yield at 5.01% and fiscal policy uncertainty ongoing, the floor beneath volatility remains elevated relative to the pre-hike cycle.

Section 05
Fixed Income

The 10-year Treasury yield stands at 5.01% and the 2-year at 4.74%, yielding a 2Y/10Y spread of +27 basis points — a modestly steepening curve relative to recent weeks. While the curve is no longer inverted, absolute yield levels are historically elevated: a 10-year above 5% increases the discount rate on equities and raises real borrowing costs for mortgages, auto loans, and corporate debt. The 30-year reportedly hit a 19-year high last week, and continued Fed tightening risks pushing long-end yields higher still. Rate-sensitive sectors — housing, REITs, utilities — remain in the crosshairs.

Section 06
Currencies

EUR/USD trades at 1.1488, with the euro holding firm against the dollar despite the Fed's renewed hawkishness — markets may be pricing in a pause after this hike or anticipate the ECB maintaining a parallel tightening posture. USD/JPY sits at 156.77, keeping the yen pinned near multi-decade lows as the Bank of Japan persists with ultra-loose policy in stark contrast to the Fed's resumed hiking cycle. The yen's persistent weakness raises the risk of BoJ intervention and continues to fuel Japanese export competitiveness while straining domestic purchasing power.

Section 07
Commodities

Gold spot at $4,394 per ounce — near all-time highs — reflects persistent safe-haven demand from central banks and institutional buyers even as nominal yields rise, defying the traditional inverse relationship. The metal's resilience signals elevated geopolitical anxiety and long-term dollar credibility concerns. WTI crude rose to $107.02 (up ~4.5% from the prior session), pushed higher by OPEC+ supply discipline, stronger-than-expected global demand projections, and geopolitical risk premia embedded in Middle East supply routes. Crude above $100 will feed through to consumer price inflation, complicating the Fed's rate path.
