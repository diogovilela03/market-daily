A broad-based equity rally swept US markets on Monday, with tech outperforming as the Nasdaq-100 gained 3.5% — its strongest single-day advance in weeks. Risk appetite was robust across the board, though NVIDIA bucked the trend, sliding 4.3% on heavy volume even as semiconductor ETFs surged. Investors are positioning ahead of key earnings from Cisco and Applied Materials this week, with NVIDIA's own print looming the following week.

Section 1
Market Drivers
  • Broad risk-on surge: All three major US equity benchmarks rallied sharply — SPY +2.4%, QQQ +3.5%, IWM +3.2% — signalling broad-based appetite for equities across large-cap, tech, and small-cap segments.
  • Tech leadership, with nuance: Semiconductor ETF SOXL surged 22.3%, yet NVIDIA — the sector's bellwether — slid 4.3% on 455M shares traded, its heaviest volume day in months, suggesting sector rotation or profit-taking ahead of its earnings on 17 November.
  • INTC rebound: Intel gained 5.5% on 143M shares, adding to positive sentiment in legacy semis as investors reassess competitiveness in a structurally resilient chip cycle.
  • Yield curve holding steady: The 10Y–2Y spread sits at +35bps, maintaining a normal upward slope that supports bank net interest margins and signals no immediate recession pricing by the bond market.
Section 2
Macro

The US macro backdrop remains mixed but broadly constructive. The Federal Reserve's restrictive posture continues — with the 2Y yield at 4.51% and the 10Y at 4.86%, markets are pricing in a prolonged "higher for longer" regime. WTI crude just below $100/bbl adds modest inflationary pressure to the energy complex, while the strong dollar (USD/JPY at 157.94, well above the BoJ's traditional comfort zone) keeps currency tensions with Japan in focus. No FOMC meeting or CPI/NFP release is scheduled within the next two days, so macro risk is primarily second-order for this session.

Section 3
Equities

SPY closed at 752.69, up 17.40 points (+2.37%) on 47.5M shares, extending its recent uptrend. QQQ was the standout, up 23.25 points (+3.54%) to 679.35 on 53.6M shares, as tech names broadly outperformed. IWM added 8.84 points (+3.23%) to 282.51, with small-caps participating meaningfully in the rally — a constructive breadth signal.

Notable movers:

  • SOXL +22.3% (256M shares): The Direxion 3× Semiconductor Bull ETF surged, reflecting aggressive leveraged positioning into the chip sector ahead of earnings season. Accompanied by its inverse SOXS falling 22.3%, confirming a one-directional sector bet.
  • NVDA −4.3% (455M shares): Despite the broader tech rally, Nvidia was the notable laggard on the most actively traded list, possibly reflecting earnings nervousness ahead of its November 17 report (consensus EPS: $2.49).
Section 4
Fixed Income

The US Treasury curve remains positively sloped, with the 2Y yield at 4.51% and the 10Y yield at 4.86%, giving a 2Y–10Y spread of +35 basis points. This gentle steepening from recent sessions reflects ongoing confidence that the Fed will eventually ease, albeit with no urgency. The curve is neither inverted nor dramatically steep — a "wait and see" posture from the bond market. At these yield levels, duration risk remains elevated for long-maturity Treasuries, while shorter-dated paper continues to offer attractive carry relative to inflation.

Section 5
Currencies

EUR/USD trades at 1.1236, holding above the 1.12 handle as the euro maintains resilience against the dollar despite the Fed's hawkish rates backdrop. The pair's bid/ask spread is tight (1.12362–1.12366), suggesting liquid and calm FX conditions. USD/JPY sits at 157.94, deep in territory that has historically triggered Bank of Japan intervention concerns. With the yen structurally weak against a high-carry dollar, any softening in US yields or a shift in BoJ policy stance could trigger a sharp yen appreciation. Market participants should monitor BoJ communication closely at these levels.

Section 6
Commodities

Gold (XAU/USD) is priced at a remarkable $4,116.89/oz, reflecting sustained demand from central banks, geopolitical risk hedging, and real rate dynamics. At these levels, gold has more than doubled over the past two years — underpinned by de-dollarisation trends and persistent inflation uncertainty. WTI crude oil last printed at $99.64/bbl (as of 3 November), hovering just below the psychologically important $100 level. Crude is caught between OPEC+ supply discipline supporting prices and demand concerns from a slowing global manufacturing cycle. A close above $100 would renew inflationary pressure discussions at the Fed.
