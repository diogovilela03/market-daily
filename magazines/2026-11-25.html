  • Gold at record territory: Spot gold at $3,520.70/oz reflects sustained demand for hard assets amid persistent fiscal concerns and an elevated rate environment, while WTI crude at $89.53/bbl signals ongoing energy market tightness.
Section 2

Macro

Treasury yields remain elevated with the 10-year at 4.92%, reflecting a Fed posture that continues to lean restrictive despite moderating inflation — markets are pricing in a prolonged "higher-for-longer" environment with cuts still distant on the horizon. The 2Y–10Y spread at +29bps is a modest positive slope but remains far below historical norms, underscoring market skepticism about durable growth in a costly capital environment. With Thanksgiving shortening the trading week, macro catalysts are sparse domestically, though any unexpected data surprises or geopolitical headlines could have outsized impact in thin-volume conditions.

Section 3

Equities

SPY closed at $718.78 (−0.49%), a modest drawdown reflecting broad but contained selling pressure. QQQ was harder hit at $638.10 (−1.99%), dragged lower by concentrated mega-cap technology weakness. IWM stood out with a gain to $267.14 (+0.53%), outperforming as investors rotated into small-cap domestic names less exposed to rate-sensitive growth multiples.

Notable mover — NVDA (▼ −5.86%): NVIDIA shed nearly $10 on the session with 379M shares trading hands, one of its most active sessions in recent months. The move coincided with broad semiconductor sector pressure and elevated SOXL/SOXS leveraged ETF flows. INSM (▲ +14.13%) was a bright spot in the healthcare space, trading 78M shares on what appears to be a catalyst-driven move.

Section 4

Fixed Income

The US 10-year Treasury yield held at 4.92% while the 2-year sat at 4.63%, producing a 10Y–2Y spread of +29bps — a steepening configuration that marks a normalisation from the deep inversion seen through much of 2023–2024. While no longer technically inverted, the spread remains well below the 100–150bps historically associated with a healthy growth cycle, keeping market participants cautious on the medium-term economic outlook. With no major Fed speakers scheduled ahead of the Thanksgiving holiday, the bond market is likely to consolidate at current levels absent a fresh catalyst.

Section 5

Currencies

EUR/USD traded at 1.0943, holding near the lower end of its recent range as the dollar retains a modest bid from relative rate differentials — the Fed remaining on hold while the ECB has been more accommodative. USD/JPY at 159.30 stays elevated near multi-decade highs for the yen, as the Bank of Japan's slow pace of policy normalisation continues to weigh on the currency. Any acceleration in BoJ rate signalling or intervention risk would be a key catalyst to watch for a JPY recovery.

Section 6

Commodities

Gold (XAU/USD) at $3,520.70/oz continues to trade at historically elevated levels, underpinned by central bank accumulation, persistent geopolitical risks, and investor demand as a hedge against both inflation and fiscal deterioration. The metal has largely decoupled from the typical inverse relationship with real rates, suggesting structural rather than merely tactical demand. WTI crude at $89.53/bbl (most recent data: 17 Nov) reflects ongoing supply discipline from OPEC+ and resilient global demand, though the approaching holiday period may dampen near-term activity in energy markets. Supply-side developments and Middle East geopolitical risk remain the key price drivers.
