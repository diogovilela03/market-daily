Macro

The US 10-year Treasury yield sits at 4.99%, hovering just one basis point below the 5.00% threshold that has historically triggered equity risk-off episodes and forced a reassessment of equity valuations. The 2-year yield ticked up to 4.70%, leaving the 2Y/10Y spread at a positive +29 basis points—a modestly steepening curve consistent with a market pricing in eventual Fed cuts but with no clear timeline. Dollar strength persists across the board: USD/JPY at 157.87 keeps intervention risk live in Tokyo, while the Fed's data-dependent posture leaves investors focused on Friday's PCE data and the November FOMC meeting as the next catalysts.

Section 3
Equities

Major indices extended their advance on October 28: SPY +0.34% to 772.31, QQQ +0.60% to 725.61, and IWM +0.40% to 281.29. Breadth was broadly constructive, with the equal-weight small-cap index (IWM) keeping pace with large-caps—a sign of durable risk appetite rather than narrow leadership.

Among notable movers: Oracle (ORCL) jumped +10.58% on outsized volume, while Stellantis (STLA) surged +13.53% on recovery optimism. On the downside, JDZG (JDZ Group) collapsed -75.41% and PLCE (Children's Place) fell -54.34%, reflecting continued pressure on consumer discretionary retail names. Nvidia (NVDA) dipped -1.17% despite remaining among the most actively traded tickers, as investors rotated to the broader semiconductor cohort (SOXL +6.28%).

Section 4
Fixed Income

The 10-year Treasury yield eased 1 basis point to 4.99%, dipping fractionally from the prior session's 5.00% handle, while the 2-year yield rose 2bps to 4.70%. The resulting 2Y/10Y spread of +29 basis points reflects a modestly positive and gently steepening curve—a notable contrast to the deep inversion that characterized much of 2024–2025. The re-steepening signals that the market expects the front end to eventually ease as the Fed pivots, though the 10-year's proximity to 5.00% continues to test equity valuations and mortgage-rate-sensitive sectors. Bond investors will watch closely for any hawkish recalibration at the November meeting or in the next round of labor/inflation data.

Section 5
Currencies

EUR/USD trades at 1.1143, holding firm as the euro benefits from relative stability in the European macro picture and a pause in risk-off flows. The pair remains comfortably above parity, with the medium-term trend supported by expectations of eventual Fed easing relative to the ECB's own path.

USD/JPY sits at 157.87, uncomfortably close to the 158–160 zone that has historically prompted verbal or active intervention from Japanese monetary authorities. The yen's persistent weakness reflects Japan's ultra-low interest rate policy set against still-elevated US rates; any surprise hawkish signal from the Bank of Japan or a US jobs miss could trigger sharp yen appreciation.

Section 6
Commodities

Gold spot trades at $4,033.35/oz, maintaining its position above the $4,000 milestone amid sustained safe-haven demand, central bank buying, and the dollar's inability to derail the multi-year rally. The metal has become a core macro hedge against both inflation and geopolitical tail risk.

WTI crude settled at $103.37/bbl, down sharply from $109.72 just four sessions ago—a 5.8% slide. The retreat suggests demand-side concerns are outweighing any geopolitical risk premium, with markets watching OPEC+ compliance and global manufacturing PMIs for directional clues. A sustained break below $100 would carry meaningful implications for energy sector earnings and inflation expectations.
