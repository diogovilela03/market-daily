Section 2
Macro

The US interest rate environment remains in a historically restrictive configuration, with the 10-year Treasury yield at 5.22% and the 2-year at 4.75% — both near multi-year peaks — suggesting the market is not pricing in near-term Fed cuts and may still be probing the terminal rate ceiling. Gold's record ascent above $4,184 reflects a combination of central bank accumulation, de-dollarization flows from emerging markets, and a durable inflation-risk premium that yields alone cannot suppress. The Bank of Japan's policy trajectory remains a key macro wildcard: with USD/JPY at 158.46, sustained yen weakness keeps intervention risk elevated and adds pressure to global carry trades.

Section 4
Fixed Income

The US Treasury curve sits at its most restrictive level in years: the 10-year yield at 5.22% and the 2-year at 4.75% produce a positive 10Y–2Y spread of +47 basis points, marking an ongoing re-steepening from the prolonged inversion that characterized 2023–2025. While the direction of travel (steepening) is constructive, the absolute level of rates remains unambiguously tight — a 5.22% 10-year acts as a persistent headwind for equity valuations, mortgage demand, and corporate debt refinancing. Fixed income markets will be closely watching Tuesday's bank earnings for signals on net interest income trajectory and credit quality under sustained rate pressure.
