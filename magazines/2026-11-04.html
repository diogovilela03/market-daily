Section 4
Fixed Income

The US 10-year Treasury yield stands at 4.91% and the 2-year at 4.59%, producing a +32bps positive spread (10Y minus 2Y). The curve remains in steepening territory versus the inversions seen earlier in the cycle, though the absolute level of both yields is historically elevated. A "bear steepener" dynamic — where long rates rise faster than short rates — would signal rising term premium and could pressure equity valuations. No inversion signal is active at current levels. Duration buyers face continued headwinds as the Fed communicates no near-term pivot, keeping mortgage rates restrictive and pressuring rate-sensitive sectors including REITs and utilities.

Section 5
Currencies

EUR/USD at 1.1229 shows the euro maintaining relative strength against the dollar, holding comfortably above parity as euro zone economic data has stabilized. USD/JPY at 158.13 continues its ascent, with the yen approaching levels that historically trigger Bank of Japan intervention. The BoJ's refusal to materially tighten policy in the face of a weakening yen reflects domestic economic constraints, but the currency gap is becoming a political and trade issue — USD/JPY above 155 has historically catalyzed unilateral FX intervention. Watch this pair for signs of sudden yen strength, which could ripple into carry-trade unwinds and broader risk-off pressure across equities.

Section 6
Commodities

Gold at $4,020.64/oz has extended its remarkable bull run, holding above the psychologically significant $4,000 level. The metal's sustained bid despite high real yields is atypical and suggests structural demand from central bank reserve diversification and geopolitical hedging, beyond the conventional inflation narrative. WTI crude at $103.37/bbl remains elevated — a persistent inflationary input that complicates the Fed's path to easing and pressures consumer discretionary margins. Supply dynamics remain tight; OPEC+ production discipline and Middle East risk premiums continue to provide a floor. Oil above $100 adds meaningful cost pressure across the global economy, sustaining the stagflationary risk narrative that has kept gold elevated.
