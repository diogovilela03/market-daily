Section 02

Macro

The Federal Reserve delivered its first rate hike since 2023, snapping the pause cycle that had defined policy through 2024–2025. Major U.S. banks — including JPMorgan, Bank of America, Citigroup, Wells Fargo, and KeyCorp — have already moved to raise their prime lending rates in response. The market is now repricing whether this hike is a one-off recalibration or the start of a renewed tightening cycle; hawkish central bank posture is creating cascading pressure across rate-sensitive sectors, compressing equity risk premiums as yields push toward multi-year highs and funding costs tighten.

Section 03

Equities

SPY declined 0.72% to $767.81, QQQ fell 0.84% to $741.21, and IWM led the selloff at −1.84% to $281.92, signaling outsized stress on smaller companies with greater sensitivity to rising borrowing costs. On the technology front, NVDA slipped 1.47% to $225.51 and INTC fell 1.02% to $122.60 amid broad sector derisking. Quantum computing play IONQ bucked the trend, gaining 4.42% to $42.54. Micron Technology has been a standout performer this cycle, recently surging past the $1,000 per share mark on strong AI server-driven DRAM and high-bandwidth memory demand.

Section 04

Volatility

The VIX closed at 14.21, declining 4.64% from the prior session and holding firmly in complacent territory below the 15 threshold. Despite surface-level equity selling, the subdued fear gauge signals that institutional participants are treating the rate hike as manageable rather than systemic. Options pricing does not currently reflect broad risk-off capitulation, though any further policy hawkishness or macro shock could rapidly reset the vol regime toward the 20 danger zone.

Section 05

Fixed Income

The 10-year Treasury yield stands at 4.96%, with the 2-year at 4.71%, producing a 25-basis-point positive spread — keeping the curve mildly upward-sloping after a prolonged period of near-zero spread. News flow points to yields trading near 3-year highs, with the yield spike directly driving a delayed profit timeline at rate-sensitive companies like Opendoor (−7%) and amplifying pressure on bank net interest margins. Elevated front-end yields are thinning equity risk premiums across sectors, a dynamic the market is actively repricing in the wake of the Fed's resumption of tightening.

Section 06

Currencies

EUR/USD trades at 1.1392, reflecting measured dollar softness as the Fed hike trajectory had been partially telegraphed. USD/JPY holds at 158.38, keeping the yen structurally weak versus the greenback despite ongoing Bank of Japan normalization rhetoric. The wide U.S.-Japan rate differential continues to anchor carry trade flows into JPY selling; Mizuho Financial Group's recent 3.66% stock gain reflects market expectations of further BoJ tightening that could gradually compress that gap over coming quarters.

Section 07

Commodities

Gold spot holds firmly at $4,286.46, benefiting from safe-haven demand and residual dollar uncertainty in the post-hike environment, with the metal well-supported above $4,200. WTI crude has pulled back sharply from its weekly high of $101.44 (September 18) to $96.41, erasing more than 5% as the IEA revised global demand forecasts lower and macro headwinds clouded the near-term demand picture. Energy equity weakness — Diamondback Energy (FANG) −8.28%, Occidental Petroleum (OXY) −5.13% — reflects the magnitude of the demand reassessment, with the $95–$97 band now serving as a key crude support zone.
