Section 2

Macro

FOMC minutes released this week reinforced a higher-for-longer policy posture, with committee members focused on energy-led upside inflation risks and a still-resilient labor market — pushing market expectations for the first rate cut further into the future. The resulting bond selloff was most pronounced at the long end, signaling that investors are now adding a meaningful term premium to reflect fiscal and inflation uncertainty rather than simply front-running the Fed. Globally, synchronous yield rises across developed markets are pressuring sovereign borrowing costs, with Japanese equities caught between yen-weakness tailwinds and global rate headwinds as the BOJ's accommodative stance keeps USD/JPY near multi-decade extremes.

Section 3

Equities

SPY shed 0.84% to $762.60, QQQ declined 0.72% to $710.93, and IWM dropped 1.34% to $297.67 — small-caps underperforming as rate sensitivity and tighter financial conditions weigh heaviest on growth and cyclical exposures. Among the most notable single-stock moves, Moderna (MRNA) collapsed 23.5% on heavy volume of 97M shares in one of the largest single-session declines for the stock this year. Walmart (WMT) shed 9.15% on 83M shares — a rare and outsized move for the retail bellwether — raising questions about consumer spending momentum ahead of what is set to be a pivotal retail earnings season.

Section 4

Volatility

VIX closed at 16.01, rising +7.00% on the session. The reading sits in the complacent-to-neutral zone — above the sub-15 threshold that signals outright market complacency, but well below the 20-level that historically flags elevated fear and systemic hedging demand. The intraday jump reflects targeted options activity around FOMC narrative risks and NVIDIA earnings uncertainty next week, rather than broad-based defensive positioning. A sustained move above 18–20 would signal a more material shift in risk appetite.

Section 5

Fixed Income

The 10-year Treasury yield stands at 4.65% (as of Aug 19) while the 2-year yields 4.19%, producing a positive 10Y–2Y spread of +46 basis points — a normally sloped curve. The curve is in a gradual steepening phase as long-end yields rise faster than short-end rates, reflecting both the market's repricing of long-run neutral rate assumptions and growing term premium demand. News flow this week flagged the 30-year Treasury reaching a 19-year high, underscoring the structural concerns around fiscal trajectory and the Fed's reduced capacity to anchor the long end without additional intervention.

Section 6

Currencies

EUR/USD trades at 1.1716, holding above the psychologically significant 1.17 handle as the euro draws support from relatively firm ECB rate expectations and a resilient eurozone growth backdrop — a remarkable show of strength given the global USD bid on rate differentials. USD/JPY at 158.89 keeps the yen near multi-decade lows, with the Bank of Japan's ultra-accommodative posture maintaining a wide yield differential that continues to suppress the currency; Japanese financial stocks (SMFG down 4.78% this week) reflect the complex interplay of yen weakness and rising global benchmark rates that squeezes domestic valuations.

Section 7

Commodities

Gold spot is at $4,561.79, maintaining a position near record highs as inflation hedging demand and geopolitical uncertainty underpin safe-haven flows. Rising real yields provide a structural headwind for non-yielding gold, yet the metal continues to hold as central bank buying and retail demand prove resilient — gold miners posted their best week since 2008 earlier this month on the surge in prices. WTI crude sits at $86.48 (as of Aug 18), near multi-month highs; elevated oil is the chief driver behind the inflationary impulse keeping the Fed in a hawkish mode, and energy stocks are positioned to benefit even as higher fuel costs threaten to compress consumer discretionary margins heading into the back-to-school and holiday spending seasons.
