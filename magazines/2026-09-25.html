Section 02

Macro

The Federal Reserve's restrictive posture remains firmly intact, with the 10-year yield's sharp 15-basis-point climb to 5.11% signaling that markets are pricing an extended stay in restrictive territory — not a near-term pivot. Geopolitically, the re-escalation of US-Iran tensions threatens a fresh energy-price overlay that could sustain headline CPI well above target and further delay the Fed's easing timeline, a scenario that is tightening financial conditions across rate-sensitive sectors. Meanwhile, the Bank of Japan's hawkish pivot adds a second front of global monetary tightening: with USD/JPY holding above 158, dollar-carry dynamics remain a structural headwind for yen-denominated assets and a source of continued global liquidity pressure.

Section 04

Fixed Income

The 10-year Treasury yield stands at 5.11% (as of September 23rd), surging from 4.96% the prior session — a 15-basis-point daily move that represents a significant duration repricing driven by geopolitical risk premia and the market's revised view on Fed persistence. The 2-year yield rose to 4.85%, leaving the curve positively sloped at +26 basis points — a modest steepening that retains some residual growth expectation signal after the prolonged near-inversion of 2024–2025. The absolute level of yields remains a meaningful constraint on equity multiples, mortgage affordability, and corporate refinancing activity, with the $5%+ threshold on the 10-year acting as a psychological and technical resistance zone for risk assets.
