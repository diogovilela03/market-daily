Section 2

Macro

The Federal Reserve's higher-for-longer posture is visibly entrenched in the yield curve: the 10-year Treasury at 4.91% and the 2-year at 4.63% represent elevated nominal rates that continue to pressure equity multiples, particularly in long-duration growth sectors. The 28-basis-point positive slope marks a meaningful un-inversion from the deep inversion that persisted through 2024–2025, though the absolute level of yields remains a constraint on consumer credit, housing, and corporate capex plans heading into 2027. US markets observed Thanksgiving on Thursday November 27, with trading resuming in an abbreviated Black Friday session, keeping macro flow thin into the weekend.

Section 3

Equities

SPY closed at $716.70 (-0.29%) and QQQ at $633.93 (-0.65%) on Tuesday November 25 — the last full trading session before the Thanksgiving holiday — reflecting modest profit-taking in large-cap tech ahead of the break. IWM was the notable outperformer at $268.87 (+0.65%), confirming the small-cap bid that has periodically challenged Nasdaq dominance. NVDA was the marquee mover, declining 3.91% to $149.30 on exceptional volume (357M shares), extending a pullback from its recent highs as the AI-capex cycle narrative faces incremental skepticism. On the upside, PLUG Energy added 14.34% to $1.065, continuing its volatile pattern of sharp intraday moves driven by short-covering and retail activity in the hydrogen energy theme.

Section 4

Fixed Income

The 10-year Treasury yield stands at 4.91% as of November 24, edging down 1 basis point from the prior session's 4.92% but remaining near multi-year highs. The 2-year holds at 4.63%, unchanged from November 23, leaving the 2s10s spread at a positive 28 basis points. The curve has fully un-inverted from the persistent inversion that characterized 2024, though the absolute yield level keeps the opportunity cost of risk assets elevated. Markets will watch for any surprise in upcoming labor or inflation prints that could shift the trajectory of Fed policy into 2027.

Section 5

Currencies

EUR/USD trades at 1.0984, holding near the mid-range of its recent corridor as the dollar retains modest structural support from the yield differential with European sovereigns. USD/JPY at 159.31 keeps the Bank of Japan's verbal intervention threshold firmly in view; with JGB yields still far below their US counterparts, the yen's weakness reflects a structural carry dynamic that policymakers have been reluctant to aggressively counter. Holiday-thinned liquidity in US markets limited significant FX moves ahead of month-end rebalancing flows, which may produce sharper moves as November closes.

Section 6

Commodities

Gold spot holds at $3,659.16/oz, near historic highs, underpinned by continued central bank accumulation, persistent geopolitical uncertainty, and the market's reading of long-term dollar debasement risk. The metal's elevation despite positive real yields signals structural demand rather than purely a rate-driven trade. WTI crude fell to $82.24/bbl — down sharply from the $94–$100 range seen in early November — as OPEC+ supply management concerns and softening global demand projections weigh on the complex. The pullback in crude offers a modest disinflationary tailwind for the Fed as it navigates its data-dependent path into 2027.
