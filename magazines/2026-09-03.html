Macro

The 10-year Treasury yield held at 4.79%, extending its August climb as the market weighs a resilient labor market against sticky services inflation — with no imminent Fed rate cut priced in. The Fed's posture remains one of patient observation, and the latest commodity surge (WTI +5%) risks adding renewed impetus to the reflation argument, complicating any dovish pivot narrative. Geopolitically, elevated energy prices are functioning as a de facto tax on consumers and a tailwind for producers, keeping the growth-versus-inflation tradeoff live into Q4.

Section 5

Fixed Income

The US 10-year yield stands at 4.79%, up 4 basis points from the prior session, while the US 2-year yield is at 4.39% (+5bp). The 10Y–2Y spread is a positive +40 basis points — a normally sloped curve that reflects a meaningful steepening from the deep inversion of 2023–2024. The un-inversion signals waning recession anxiety, but the elevated absolute yield level is keeping pressure on rate-sensitive sectors including housing, utilities, and growth equities. No imminent Fed action is priced by the market.
