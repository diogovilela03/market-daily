Section 04
Volatility

The VIX closed at 16.34, rising +8.69% on the session — a sharp one-day move reflecting genuine September anxiety, though the index remains below the 20-level threshold that typically signals elevated fear. The intraday range of 15.20–16.80 shows some bid for protection, particularly heading into AVGO earnings and a week heavy with software-sector reports (LULU, DOCU, ZS, SNOW). With VIX still in the "complacent" zone (sub-20), systemic fear has not yet materialized, but the velocity of today's move warrants monitoring. A close above 18 in coming sessions would indicate the fear premium is building meaningfully.

Section 05
Fixed Income

The 10-year Treasury yield holds at 4.75% and the 2-year at 4.34%, producing a curve spread of +41 basis points — a normal (positively-sloped) configuration that has been gradually steepening since the August low. The late-August FOMC minutes reinforced the hawkish narrative, pushing longer-dated yields higher and extending the selloff in rate-sensitive sectors including homebuilders (KB Home, Meritage, LGI Homes all hit hard) and utilities. The absence of an imminent curve inversion is a mild positive, but the absolute level of yields remains high enough to suppress housing demand and pressure growth multiples.

Section 06
Currencies

EUR/USD at 1.1577 — the euro sits near multi-month highs against the dollar, supported by relative eurozone data resilience and a USD that faces headwinds from risk-off flows that paradoxically pressure it via carry-unwind dynamics. USD/JPY at 159.70 remains structurally elevated — the yen continues to trade near multi-year lows as the Bank of Japan holds a cautious normalization pace despite above-target Japanese inflation. Any hawkish BOJ signal could trigger rapid yen appreciation and unwind of yen-funded carry trades, representing a significant tail risk for global equity markets.

Section 07
Commodities

Gold at $4,326.16/oz — the precious metal remains near all-time highs, underpinned by elevated rate uncertainty, central bank buying, and safe-haven demand amid geopolitical tensions. A pullback in spot prices earlier in the week (visible in Agnico Eagle's stock reaction) proved shallow, and the structural bid for gold appears intact. WTI crude at $83.90/bbl has eased from the upper-$80s range seen earlier in August, as demand-growth concerns — particularly from China — and a risk-off macro tone weigh on the energy complex. OPEC+ supply management continues to provide a floor, limiting the downside.
