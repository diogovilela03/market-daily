Section 2
Macro

The Federal Reserve's high-for-longer posture remains entrenched, with the 10-year Treasury yield at 5.09% reflecting both resilient economic data and lingering inflation above target. The yield curve has un-inverted meaningfully — the 2Y–10Y spread at +35bps — a structural shift from the protracted inversion of prior quarters that historically signals a credit cycle inflection point. Meanwhile, WTI crude above $100 per barrel introduces renewed upside risk to energy-driven CPI components, complicating the Fed's path toward a first rate cut. Geopolitical tensions in the Middle East and Eastern Europe continue to underpin commodity prices and safe-haven flows into gold.

Section 3
Equities

All three major US equity benchmarks finished Monday in positive territory: SPY +0.42% to 766.48, QQQ +1.21% to 718.36, and IWM +0.31% to 271.28. The outperformance in QQQ reflects concentrated buying in technology and AI-related names heading into the busiest week of earnings season. Two notable movers: Cleveland-Cliffs (CLF) surged +16.42% following better-than-expected Q3 results and raised guidance, with volume exceeding 88 million shares — among the market's most active. Intel (INTC) gained +2.43% amid a broad semiconductor rally (SOXL +5.43%), with Nvidia (NVDA) adding +1.38%, as investors positioned for upside surprises from chip names reporting later this month.

Section 4
Fixed Income

The US Treasury curve continues its steepening trend with the 10-year yield at 5.09% and the 2-year yield at 4.74%, a positive spread of approximately 35 basis points. This marks a notable departure from the prolonged inversion that characterized much of the prior two years, suggesting the market is beginning to price in a normalization cycle rather than an imminent recession. At 5.09%, the 10-year represents a meaningful real yield hurdle for equity valuations — particularly for duration-sensitive growth stocks — and adds to the appeal of short-duration fixed income as an alternative to equities. Both yields edged slightly lower on the day as bond buyers returned cautiously ahead of key earnings and economic data.

Section 5
Currencies

EUR/USD trades at 1.1233, holding above the 1.12 handle as the euro benefits from a marginally softer dollar and supportive ECB rhetoric around maintaining restrictive policy into year-end. USD/JPY at 158.05 keeps the yen near multi-decade lows, a reflection of the Bank of Japan's reluctance to meaningfully tighten even as the Fed stands pat — a divergence that continues to attract carry traders and pressure Japanese import costs. The yen's weakness at this level historically triggers intervention risk from Japanese authorities, making USD/JPY a key pair to monitor as Tokyo approaches its fiscal year-end.

Section 6
Commodities

Gold at $4,128.97/oz — a level that represents a historic milestone — continues its structural bull run driven by persistent central bank accumulation, inflation hedging demand, and de-dollarization flows from EM sovereign buyers. The move well above $4,000 reflects a market that has fundamentally repriced gold's floor. WTI crude at $101.91/bbl (+1.04% on the day) remains elevated above the $100 psychological level, buoyed by OPEC+ supply discipline and seasonal refinery demand heading into winter. Sustained crude above $100 feeds into headline CPI and could keep the Fed anchored longer than markets currently expect — a key macro risk to monitor.
