Section 2
Macro

The 10-year Treasury at 5.31% and the 2-year at 4.84% reflect a market still pricing a higher-for-longer Federal Reserve trajectory, with the 47-basis-point positive 10Y–2Y spread representing a meaningful re-steepening from the deep inversion of the prior cycle. USD/JPY holding at 158.37 keeps the Bank of Japan's tolerance thresholds squarely in focus, as policymakers navigate the tension between yield-curve-control objectives and yen defense — prior BoJ intervention episodes were triggered well below current levels. Gold at record highs reinforces that inflation expectations and reserve diversification remain active institutional positioning themes despite the Fed's extended restrictive stance.

Section 3
Equities

The S&P 500 proxy (SPY) gained 0.55% to $779.09, the Nasdaq 100 proxy (QQQ) advanced 0.46% to $759.66, while the Russell 2000 proxy (IWM) declined 0.72% to $281.34 — a classic large-cap-leads, small-cap-lags session consistent with rising rate sensitivity among smaller balance sheets.

Among notable movers, OPCH surged 32.65% to $31.00 on 65 million shares of volume, one of the session's standout large-cap moves. Intel (INTC) fell 3.18% to $112.50 on over 100 million shares — the most actively traded session in recent memory for the name — as investors continue to reassess the chipmaker's competitive positioning against AMD and Nvidia in AI-accelerator and data-center workloads.

Section 4
Fixed Income

The 10-year US Treasury yield stood at 5.31% and the 2-year at 4.84%, placing the 10Y–2Y spread at approximately +47 basis points — a positively sloped curve that marks a significant shift from the prolonged inversion of recent years. While the steepening nominally improves net interest margin prospects for banks, the elevated absolute level of rates continues to compress multiples in long-duration equities and raises refinancing costs across the leveraged credit complex. Q3 bank earnings beginning October 13 will provide a real-time read on credit quality, deposit retention, and NIM expansion at prevailing yield levels.

Section 5
Currencies

EUR/USD traded at 1.1237, maintaining relatively stable footing against the dollar as the eurozone navigates diverging growth dynamics relative to the US. USD/JPY at 158.37 places the yen deep in territory that has historically prompted verbal and physical intervention from Japanese authorities — the currency has held above 150 for an extended stretch, keeping the BoJ's policy flexibility debate at the forefront of FX markets. Any surprise shift toward further rate normalization from Tokyo could be a material catalyst for yen strengthening.

Section 6
Commodities

Gold hit a new record at $4,134.12/oz, cementing its position as the standout commodity of this macro cycle. The persistent bid reflects a confluence of factors: central bank demand from emerging market reserve managers reducing dollar exposure, geopolitical hedging, and an inflation premium that remains elevated even as the Fed holds rates restrictive. WTI crude most recently traded at $96.16/barrel, having pulled back from near $99.37 in the prior session; oil remains broadly elevated as OPEC+ supply discipline and ongoing geopolitical risk premia underpin the energy complex well above the $70s seen in early 2026.

Section 7
Earnings Radar

Q3 earnings season opens this week with consumer and travel bellwethers leading into the marquee financial sector reporting cluster on October 13, when JPMorgan Chase, Goldman Sachs, Citigroup, Johnson & Johnson, and UnitedHealth all report before the open — offering a critical read on credit, investment banking activity, and healthcare cost trends.
