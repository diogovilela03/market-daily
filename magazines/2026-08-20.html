Section 2
Macro

The dominant macro theme entering this week is the relentless climb in long-duration U.S. yields: the 30-year Treasury has reportedly touched levels not seen since 2007, reflecting the bond market's judgment that structural inflation risk has not been fully tamed despite the Fed's pause. While a surprise jobs miss earlier in August sank near-term rate-hike bets and provided equities with a relief rally, the persistence of elevated longer-dated yields signals that markets are not yet pricing a return to the low-rate regime.

Canada's CPI release today and the Empire State Manufacturing Survey offer near-term reads on North American inflation and factory activity, though the bigger near-term catalyst is NVIDIA's earnings on 26 August — a result that will serve as a proxy for AI infrastructure spending durability and could meaningfully shift the tech narrative. The Fed's next meeting remains data-dependent, with the market watching each CPI and labor print for any evidence that would justify either a cut or a resumption of hikes.

Section 4
Volatility

The CBOE VIX closed at 14.89 on Tuesday (−6.38% on the session), comfortably below the 15 threshold that analysts associate with investor complacency. The options market is effectively pricing near-zero systemic risk despite an environment of elevated long-end yields, record gold prices, and a concentrated biotech catalyst (MRNA) that illustrates how idiosyncratic shocks can materialise without warning. Historically, VIX readings sub-15 heading into a dense earnings period — particularly one featuring NVIDIA — have preceded sharp, short-lived vol spikes. Traders long complacency should be aware that the asymmetry favours vol buyers at current levels.

Fixed Income

The 10-year Treasury yield stands at 4.71% and the 2-year at 4.19%, placing the 10Y–2Y spread at +52 basis points — a curve that is positively sloped and gently steepening from recent months. With the 30-year reportedly near 19-year highs, the sell-off in long-duration bonds is the structural story of the summer: the bond market is pricing persistent inflation and growing concern about the US fiscal trajectory, even as the Fed holds rates steady.

The steepening curve creates a bifurcated environment: financials, life insurers, and banks benefit from widening net interest margins, while rate-sensitive growth and technology equities face multiple compression. For fixed income portfolios, the risk of further long-end repricing remains elevated until there is a clear downside inflation surprise or a credible signal from the Fed on the terminal rate path.

Section 6
Currencies

EUR/USD trades at 1.1685, reflecting a broadly softer dollar as markets price lower odds of additional Fed tightening following recent labor market softness. The euro is benefiting from dollar weakness rather than any European-specific catalyst, and the pair remains in a constructive medium-term uptrend.

USD/JPY stands at 158.43, near the upper end of its recent range as the yen continues to bear the brunt of the US-Japan yield differential. With the Bank of Japan maintaining its gradual policy normalisation, the yen's structural weakness persists; a move above 160 would likely revive Bank of Japan intervention speculation, similar to episodes seen in 2024–2025. Japanese equities (SMFG fell 4.78% Tuesday) are also suffering as global yield fears trigger broad sell-offs in rate-sensitive financial stocks.

Commodities

Gold spot has climbed to $4,488/oz, extending an extraordinary bull run that pushed gold miners to their best week since 2008. The rally is being driven by a confluence of factors: dollar softness, structurally elevated inflation expectations, safe-haven demand from geopolitical uncertainty, and central bank accumulation. The fact that gold is surging alongside elevated nominal yields — rather than waiting for rate cuts — signals that real-rate dynamics and reserve diversification are the dominant drivers, not simply the opportunity cost of holding bullion.

WTI crude stands at $86.48/bbl (as of 18 August), remaining elevated on supply-side constraints and resilient demand, particularly from emerging markets. The persistence of crude above $85 is adding to inflation stickiness and complicating the Fed's path; energy stocks are in focus as oil sustains levels that support production economics while keeping fuel-cost pressure in the consumer basket.
