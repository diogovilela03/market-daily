Section 2

Macro

The macro backdrop is being defined by the relentless climb in U.S. long-term yields. With the 30-year reportedly touching levels last seen around 2007, the market is effectively pricing in a higher-for-longer rate regime driven by term premium expansion, fiscal concerns, and diminishing Fed rate-cut expectations. The 10-year at 4.72% and 2-year at 4.19% represent a curve that has steepened by +53 basis points—a sign that long-end sellers are driving the move rather than front-end policy expectations shifting.

July's flat Producer Price Index offered a supportive disinflation data point, but the bond market is telling a different story: structural supply concerns and strong economic resilience are keeping yields elevated even as headline inflation cools. Canada's CPI release and Empire State manufacturing data arriving Monday add near-term data risk.

Section 4

Volatility

The CBOE VIX stands at 15.84, up +4.10% on the day (+0.65 points). At this level, volatility remains in complacent territory below the 20 threshold associated with elevated fear. The modest uptick may reflect early hedging activity in response to surging long-end yields and semiconductor sector stress, but options markets are not yet pricing in acute systemic risk. A sustained move above 20 would signal a more meaningful repricing of risk.

⚡ WATCH Section 5

Fixed Income

The U.S. yield curve steepened further, with the 10-year Treasury yielding 4.72% (+4 bps) and the 2-year at 4.19% (+2 bps), producing a 10Y–2Y spread of +53 basis points. The curve's positive slope reflects a long-end-driven selloff rather than front-end policy repricing—the 30-year reportedly hit a 19-year high, a development generating outsized impact on equity valuations through discount rate expansion. Life insurer and annuity stocks, conversely, are benefiting from the higher yield environment. The fiscal backdrop—persistent deficits and heavy Treasury issuance—remains the structural driver pressuring the long end.

Section 6

Currencies

EUR/USD trades at 1.1597, holding above 1.15 as relative yield stability in Europe provides a floor for the euro. The pair reflects a market pricing in policy divergence narrowing between the Fed and ECB rather than widening, consistent with a higher-for-longer U.S. rate environment that the ECB is partially tracking.

USD/JPY is at 159.26, extending yen weakness and approaching levels that previously prompted verbal intervention from Japanese authorities. The Bank of Japan's cautious normalization pace—against a backdrop of rising U.S. yields—continues to keep the yen under structural pressure. Traders will be watching for any signs of Ministry of Finance intervention commentary around the 160 handle.

Commodities

Gold spot trades at $4,348.84/oz, holding near all-time highs and extending a structural bull run fuelled by central bank diversification away from dollar-denominated reserves, geopolitical risk premiums, and fiscal expansion across major economies. Wheaton Precious Metals' record first-half results validate the theme at the producer level, and analysts see limited near-term headwinds unless real yields surge materially.

Oil prices remain supported by OPEC+ supply discipline, while demand signals from the U.S. consumer earnings season this week may refine the near-term demand outlook. Energy sector stocks—including GeoPark, Tidewater, and Targa Resources—are flagged as positioned to benefit from the sustained elevated price environment.
