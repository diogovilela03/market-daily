Section 02
Macro

The Federal Reserve's September rate hike — its first since 2023 — marks a decisive pivot back toward tightening, with major US banks including JPMorgan, BofA, Citi, and Wells Fargo having raised their prime rates in response. The 10Y yield at 5.26% reflects a market pricing in sustained restrictive policy, exerting pressure on duration-sensitive assets and housing affordability. Geopolitically, US-Iran hostilities that flared during the summer continue to keep a risk premium embedded in energy and defense, with Raytheon's $22.9B Tomahawk missile contract underscoring elevated defense spending expectations. Housing remains under structural pressure as elevated mortgage rates weigh on builders and REITs, even as Berkshire Hathaway's contrarian $212M stake in Lennar signals long-horizon value interest.

Section 03
Equities

US equity benchmarks closed Q3 on a mixed note: the S&P 500 proxy SPY finished at 762.63 (−0.21%), QQQ at 739.77 (+0.25%), and the small-cap IWM at 277.89 (−0.40%). The Nasdaq's modest outperformance reflects continued investor preference for large-cap technology — with NVDA holding at $228.38 and INTC gaining 3.7% — while small-caps remain caught in the crosscurrents of higher rates and softer economic visibility.

Among notable single-stock movers: LQDA (Liquidia Corp) plunged 57.2% in heavy volume (22.6M shares traded), while TGE surged 85.5% on exceptional turnover of 183M shares, reflecting broad dispersion in small-cap names at quarter-end.

Section 04
Fixed Income

The 10-year Treasury yield stands at 5.26% (+2bp), and the 2-year at 4.89% (−3bp), producing a positive 10Y–2Y spread of +37 basis points. The curve is modestly upward-sloping — not inverted — and the recent divergence between the two tenors (10Y grinding higher while 2Y eases slightly) points toward gradual bear-steepening. Markets are pricing a "higher for longer" Fed posture; near-term, attention focuses on Friday's NFP report as the next catalyst for yield direction. The 30Y segment of the curve and corporate credit spreads (HYG trading at $77.21, −0.19%) bear watching as financial conditions tighten.

Section 05
Currencies

EUR/USD trades at 1.1321, reflecting euro resilience underpinned by ECB credibility, even as US rate differentials provide structural dollar support. USD/JPY is at 158.26, near multi-year highs for the yen-dollar pair, as the Bank of Japan continues to lag the Fed's tightening trajectory. Mizuho and other Japanese financials have seen strength amid expectations of eventual BoJ tightening, and any hawkish shift in BoJ language could trigger a sharp yen appreciation event — a key tail risk for USD/JPY longs and yen-funded carry trades that have built up considerably.

Section 06
Commodities

Gold spot holds at $4,180.64/oz — a historically elevated level — though the precious metals equity complex faces headwinds from rising real rates that erode the opportunity cost advantage of non-yielding bullion. The gold miner selloff (WPM −5.1%, AU −5.1%, CDE −7.0%) illustrates the divergence between spot prices and mining economics under a high-rate regime.

WTI crude retreated to $96.16/barrel (−3.23% from $99.37), pressured by IEA demand downgrades and broader macro anxiety. However, US-Iran tensions and OPEC+ supply discipline provide a meaningful price floor, and Diamondback Energy (FANG) and OXY saw outsized declines on the move, offering potential entry points for energy bulls if geopolitical risk re-escalates.
