Section 02
Macro

The Fed pause narrative is firmly embedded in market pricing, with the 10-year Treasury at 5.29% — a level that reflects both the economy's resilience and persistent inflationary pressure. The 2-year stands at 4.88%, leaving the 10Y-2Y spread at a modest +41 basis points — an upward-sloping curve signalling markets believe the Fed is near its terminal rate but see little prospect of near-term cuts. The strong dollar (USD/JPY 157.83) underscores deepening divergence between a hawkish Fed and the Bank of Japan's accommodative stance.

Today is the first Friday of October — the traditional Nonfarm Payrolls release day. A labor market print meaningfully stronger than consensus would further push back any remaining 2026 easing expectations and could add upside pressure to the 10-year yield and the dollar.

Section 04
Fixed Income

The US 10-year Treasury yield stands at 5.29% (up from 5.26% on September 29), while the 2-year yield sits at 4.88%. The resulting 10Y-2Y spread of +41 basis points reflects a modestly normal, steepening curve — a structural shift from the prolonged inversion that characterized the 2023–2025 period. This steepening is driven primarily by long-end selling pressure rather than genuine optimism about near-term rate cuts, as the front end remains anchored by the Fed's hold stance.

Persistently elevated long-term yields are transmitting directly into higher mortgage rates, commercial real estate cap rates, and borrowing costs for capital-intensive sectors — dynamics now appearing in earnings revisions and analyst downgrades across rate-sensitive industries.

Section 05
Currencies

EUR/USD at 1.1262 — the euro holds firm above the 1.12 handle, supported by the ECB's cautious rate stance and a modest improvement in EU economic sentiment, though a stronger-than-expected NFP today could weigh on the pair via renewed USD demand. USD/JPY at 157.83 — the yen continues to weaken toward multi-month lows as the US-Japan yield differential widens. At this level, Bank of Japan verbal intervention risk is elevated; a sustained move toward 160 would likely prompt either jawboning or direct FX action from Tokyo. Japanese bank stocks (MUFG -3.47% on Oct 1) reflect investor unease over the BoJ's constrained policy options.

Section 06
Commodities

Gold at $4,182.71/oz — the precious metal remains at historically elevated levels, driven by persistent central bank reserve diversification, geopolitical hedging demand, and uncertainty around real yields. Notably, gold's resilience in the face of USD strength suggests structural demand from non-dollar reserve managers rather than purely speculative flows. Weakness in precious metals miners (Wheaton Precious Metals -5.11%, Coeur Mining -7.02% in late September) suggests the equity complex is pricing in some near-term profit-taking risk at spot.

WTI crude at $96.16/bbl (September 29 settlement, down from $99.37 on September 28) — oil remains near cyclical highs, with OPEC+ supply discipline and resilient global demand keeping prices elevated. A stronger-than-expected NFP print adding to dollar strength could exert modest downward pressure on dollar-denominated commodities, but the supply-side picture supports the floor near $90.
