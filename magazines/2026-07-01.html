US equities staged a broad end-of-quarter rally led by tech as J.P. Morgan raised its S&P 500 year-end target to 7,800, citing AI-driven earnings momentum and resilient economic conditions. Section 01
Market Drivers
  • AI capital-expenditure cycle sustains the bull case: J.P. Morgan raised its S&P 500 year-end target to 7,800 — joining other major brokerages — citing strong earnings momentum from the AI-led investment boom. NVDA added 2.63% and INTC surged 6.0% on the session.
  • Apple–Intel partnership sparks semiconductor rally: A partnership announcement between Apple and Intel ignited a broad chip-sector advance, crushing the SOXS inverse semiconductor ETF by 12.6% as short sellers were squeezed heading into quarter-end.
  • Fed on pause with rate-cut optionality intact: The Federal Reserve held rates unchanged at its last meeting but flagged a possible cut later in 2026 as disinflation progress continues, keeping equity risk appetite supported while also driving demand for low-beta defensive names.
  • Affluent consumer resilience underpins financials: American Express data corroborates a K-shaped recovery where higher-income households continue to spend robustly; Truist raised BNY's price target to $160, citing higher net interest income and a strong equity market rally lifting trust-bank revenues.
Section 02
Macro

The Federal Reserve held rates steady at its most recent meeting while signalling openness to cuts later in 2026 — a stance that keeps equity multiples elevated but injects uncertainty into the back half of the year. Upcoming PCE and GDP data will be the next litmus tests for the Fed's timeline; a softer print could accelerate rate-cut pricing, while an upside surprise would reinforce the higher-for-longer narrative. Geopolitically, a U.S.–Iran peace deal has contributed to a partial easing of the energy risk premium. AllianceBernstein's strategists flagged sustained above-target inflation, stretched U.S. valuations, and a growing rotation toward non-U.S. equities and intermediate-duration fixed income as key H2 positioning themes.

Section 03
Equities

All three major benchmarks finished higher to close out Q2, with tech-heavy QQQ leading at +1.70% (736.40), SPY advancing +0.78% (746.77), and small-caps (IWM) posting a more muted +0.50% (300.45) — underscoring the continued dominance of large-cap growth over cyclicals and value. Two notable single-stock movers:

  • Intel (INTC) +6.0%: Shares surged to $139.63 on above-average volume of 114.8M shares, driven by a headline Apple partnership that lifted the entire chip sector and signalled Intel's renewed strategic relevance in AI hardware.
  • AT&T (T) –5.22%: The telecom giant shed 5.2% to $20.68 on 126.6M shares traded, with sector rotation out of high-yield dividend names pressuring the stock as investors favoured growth into quarter-end.
Section 04
Volatility

The CBOE VIX fell 7.29% to 16.45 — comfortably within the complacent zone below 20 — reflecting broad risk-on sentiment into quarter-end. Having opened the session at 17.68, the index gave up early hedging demand as equities rallied through the afternoon. At 16.45, options premiums are relatively compressed; while the near-term tape looks calm, the setup heading into H2 macro events (next FOMC decision, CPI, NFP) at low-VIX levels creates asymmetric risk for those underhedged.

Section 05
Fixed Income

The US Treasury curve remains positively sloped and is showing tentative steepening: the 10-year yield stands at 4.38% and the 2-year yield at 4.10%, producing a 2s10s spread of +28 basis points — a meaningful shift from the sustained inversion that characterised 2023–2024. The steepening reflects markets pricing gradual Fed easing on the front end while keeping term premium elevated on the long end due to persistent fiscal issuance, high debt-servicing costs, and sticky inflation expectations. AllianceBernstein highlighted intermediate duration as an attractive zone, consistent with a "higher-for-longer but not forever" rate narrative.

Section 06
Currencies

EUR/USD trades at 1.1405, holding near multi-month highs as a softening USD and constructive European growth narrative support the pair. USD/JPY sits at a striking 162.65 — the yen's weakest level against the dollar since 1986 — reigniting debate over Bank of Japan intervention risk and whether currency weakness will steer defensive capital toward gold and Bitcoin as alternative stores of value. The yen's sustained depreciation reflects the yawning divergence between the BoJ's ultra-loose posture and the global higher-for-longer rate cycle; any surprise BoJ policy pivot would represent a sharp volatility catalyst across global risk assets.

Section 07
Commodities

Gold (XAU/USD) holds at $3,962/oz, supported by real-yield uncertainty, geopolitical hedging demand, and USD/JPY's slide to multi-decade lows — all of which amplify the appeal of non-fiat stores of value. Despite periodic bouts of hawkish Fed rhetoric that pressured gold earlier in the cycle, the metal remains structurally bid well above the $3,000 threshold it breached in early 2026. WTI crude trades at $78.94/bbl, reflecting the partial easing of geopolitical risk premium following the U.S.–Iran peace deal. OPEC+ supply discipline continues to provide a floor, though demand visibility into H2 — particularly given China's uneven recovery — keeps upside capped in the high-$70s range.

Section 08
Digital Assets

The crypto complex enters Q3 under compounding headwinds. Bitcoin is testing critical support in the $57,832–$58,900 range, approaching a 22-month low after a bruising Q2 shaped by rate jitters and record institutional outflows: U.S. spot BTC ETFs shed $4.5 billion in June — the worst monthly figure on record, 29% worse than the prior trough, and culminating in nine consecutive days of redemptions to close the period. Ethereum remains pinned below $1,600 amid persistent ETF outflows and slowing on-chain activity. XRP dipped –1.05% to $1.0366 as leveraged positions cleared from recent peaks, though on-chain metrics are constructive: daily active addresses surged 72% over two weeks, and the network added 4,941 new wallets in a single day — a three-month high — with institutional inflows to XRP spot ETFs continuing. Overall sentiment is bearish, with CryptoQuant flagging that a sustainable bull recovery likely requires a fresh wave of institutional capital inflows.

Regulatory: The MiCA July 1 enforcement deadline is restructuring the European crypto landscape, forcing non-compliant operators out of the EU market — Utorg obtained its MiCA licence on the deadline date. Separately, Taiwan passed a comprehensive crypto regulation bill mandating licensing, cybersecurity standards, and strict reserve requirements. On the market structure front, Open USD (OUSD) — a consortium stablecoin backed by Ripple, Visa, Mastercard, BlackRock, and Google — launched on June 30, positioning XRP as a potential bridge layer in institutional payment rails.

⚡ WATCH — MiCA enforcement (July 1) marks the most significant EU crypto regulatory shift to date; further exchange and wallet-provider restructurings across Europe are expected in coming weeks.
