01 · Market Drivers

Iran De-Escalation Lifts Futures; Crypto Decouples

  • The US and Iran reached an emergency agreement to halt direct strikes over the weekend, restoring commercial vessel passage through the Strait of Hormuz and transitioning maritime disputes to technical talks in Doha (Tuesday) — reducing the immediate risk-off premium and lifting equity futures into Monday's open.
  • Bitcoin closed near $59,700 (-6.8% on the week), diverging sharply from equity relief: the $60,000 level absorbed a Monday breakout attempt and has flipped from support to resistance, while spot BTC ETFs have logged over $7 billion in net outflows over the past two months.
  • US PCE inflation running at 4.1% has locked in the Fed's higher-for-longer posture, continuing to divert institutional capital into AI infrastructure; South Korea's announced $518 billion AI chip buildout (Samsung, SK Hynix) underscores the scale of the rotation away from crypto and speculative risk.
  • The ECB's annual Sintra forum begins Tuesday with both ECB President Lagarde and Fed Chair Powell scheduled to speak — a potential catalyst for rates and currency volatility heading into key US inflation data later this week.
02 · Macro

The US–Iran emergency ceasefire agreement — halting direct strikes and reopening Hormuz to commercial traffic — removed the immediate threat of regional escalation, though structural maritime blockades and underlying nuclear negotiations remain unresolved and subject to rapid deterioration. ⚡ WATCH 08 · Digital Assets

Bitcoin Fails to Reclaim $60K; EU MiCA Review Vote Set for July 7

Bitcoin hovered near $59,700, down 6.8% for the week and 18.7% over the past month, as crypto markets conspicuously failed to participate in equity relief following the Iran de-escalation. The $60,000 level has flipped from multi-cycle support to active resistance: early Monday buying pushed BTC above $60K briefly before heavy spot selling from long-term holders and institutional investors absorbed the move and sent it lower. BTC spot ETFs have registered over $7 billion in net outflows over the past two months, and $147.4 million in crypto positions were liquidated in a 12-hour window — $64.3 million from Bitcoin alone, with $116.1 million of the total from long positions. Bitcoin is also heading into a rare back-to-back quarterly loss.

  • ETH: Four dormant OG wallets ended an eight-year holding streak, selling 33,623 ETH (~$52.5 million) — a notable distribution signal from historically patient holders.
  • XRP: XRP Ledger stablecoin market cap surged to $948.6 million (up 2.5× from $377 million in January), with RLUSD alone accounting for $810.6 million. ODL volume hit $1.2 billion in Q1 2026, up 45% year-over-year.
  • BitMEX: CEO Stephan Lutz, CFO, and head of growth were all removed; global general counsel Peter Wilkinson assumes the CEO role.
  • India USDT: Tether premium surged above 8.5% (vs. typical 3–4%) amid an Enforcement Directorate crackdown on alleged illegal money transfers via stablecoins.
  • Macro overhang: South Korea's $518B AI chip investment push (Samsung, SK Hynix) is the latest signal that the AI capital cycle is drawing institutional flows away from crypto at scale.

⚡ Regulatory watch: The European Parliament's economic affairs committee has urged the Commission to assess whether DeFi, staking, lending, and NFTs should fall under MiCA rules. A full Parliament vote is scheduled for 7 July 2026. A positive vote would materially expand MiCA's scope and create new compliance obligations across the EU crypto ecosystem.

Overall sentiment: Bearish.
