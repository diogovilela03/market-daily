01 — Market Drivers

What's Moving Markets

  • AI infrastructure leads the tape. Intel (INTC) surged 11.19% and Marvell Technology (MRVL) gained 9.63% as Nvidia's Jensen Huang publicly backed AI's early-stage thesis. Apollo and Blackstone completed a landmark $35 billion private credit deal — one of the largest ever — to fund Anthropic's GPU compute expansion via a Google TPU-backed SPV, cementing institutional conviction in AI infrastructure as a structural long-cycle investment.
  • Defense and aerospace outperform on durable geopolitical demand. General Dynamics, Howmet Aerospace (75 hedge fund holders), TransDigm, and Parker-Hannifin each carried fresh analyst upgrades and beat-and-raise Q1 results. Iran/Israel conflict risk and NATO rearming were cited as durable revenue tailwinds through 2026 and beyond.
  • Healthcare managed care stages a recovery. Morgan Stanley and Mizuho simultaneously raised UnitedHealth (UNH) price targets to $453–$460 (Overweight/Outperform), citing softer utilization trends and AI-driven cost management upside — a possible sector re-rating signal after months of pressure.
  • Consumer discretionary bounces on easing risk premiums. Retail and consumer names recovered on reports of tentative Iran/Israel ceasefire steps and retreating short-dated Treasury yields, with ISM data showing five consecutive months of industrial expansion providing a constructive macro backdrop.

02 — Macro

Rates, Fed & Geopolitics

Wednesday's US CPI release is the week's defining macro event: consensus forecasts headline inflation accelerating to +4.2% YoY (from 3.8% prior), a print that — if confirmed — would push Fed rate-hike odds, already at 68–70% for year-end, materially higher. The 10-year Treasury yield holds at 4.55%, near 2026 highs, while WTI crude near $96/bbl from the Iran/Israel conflict creates persistent inflationary feedback that complicates the Fed's path. The ECB is expected to hike 25 basis points Thursday, adding cross-market rate volatility into the back half of the week. The Fed remains in a watch-and-assess posture ahead of the print, with BNP Paribas now forecasting three Fed hikes beginning December 2026 — a sharp reversal from 2025's three cuts.

03 — Equities

US Markets

All three benchmark ETFs advanced Monday. QQQ led with a +1.56% gain ($716.07) as megacap tech reclaimed ground; IWM added +0.87% ($284.11) on improved risk sentiment; SPY gained a measured +0.23% ($739.22), reflecting a more muted broader-market backdrop outside tech.

Notable movers: Intel (INTC +11.19%) posted its strongest session in months on record volume, driven by Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang's endorsement of AI infrastructure as early-innings — lifting AI chip suppliers broadly. AMD also received a Mizuho price-target raise to $615, citing agentic AI demand keeping suppliers supply-constrained through 2027. Marvell Technology (MRVL +9.63%) extended a recent run on the same AI datacenter thesis. On the negative side, Honeywell (HON) extended its losing streak to six consecutive sessions (–11% cumulative), shedding $17B in market cap on mounting valuation concerns despite moderate operating performance.

04 — Fixed Income

Treasuries & Yield Curve

The US Treasury curve maintains a modest steepening bias. The 10-year yield sits at 4.55% while the 2-year yield retreated to 4.17%, leaving a +38-basis-point positive spread — a near-normal, un-inverted slope following the prolonged 2023–2024 inversion. The steepening reflects markets gradually pricing in eventual Fed cuts while the front end remains anchored by sticky inflation. A hotter-than-expected CPI print Wednesday could push the 2-year back above 4.20%, compressing the nascent positive spread and testing the steepening thesis.

05 — Currencies

FX Markets

EUR/USD at 1.1547 reflects a firm Euro, supported by expectations of a 25bp ECB rate hike Thursday that narrows the US–Eurozone rate differential and adds carry appeal to the single currency. USD/JPY at 160.16 keeps the yen at multi-year weakness levels historically associated with Bank of Japan intervention risk; the BOJ's yield curve control policy holds domestic rates near zero while US 10-year yields at 4.55% sustain the carry trade that has pressured the yen throughout 2025–2026. Both pairs face sharp repricing risk from Wednesday's US CPI data, where an upside surprise would reinforce USD strength and further stress yen carry positions.

06 — Commodities

Gold & Crude

Gold at $4,332.74/oz holds near record territory, underpinned by geopolitical risk hedging (Iran/Israel conflict, global safe-haven demand), relative dollar softness, and continued central bank accumulation that has structurally elevated gold's role as a reserve asset. The level reflects a multi-year secular bull run built on persistent macro uncertainty and de-dollarization momentum. WTI crude at $95.96/bbl prices in an explicit Iran/Israel conflict risk premium, driven by Strait of Hormuz supply-disruption concerns. Oil at this level feeds directly into Wednesday's CPI energy components, creating inflationary feedback that constrains the Fed's room to maneuver. A genuine ceasefire development could unwind $5–10/bbl of geopolitical premium rapidly.
