A stunning July payrolls miss — the U.S. economy shed 23,000 jobs against expectations of solid growth — obliterated September rate-hike bets on Friday and sent equities to record highs, while gold surged over 7% on the week as real-rate expectations collapsed. Markets open Monday consolidating those gains, with the macro debate now firmly pivoted from "how much tightening remains" to "when does the Fed signal the first cut."

Section 01
Market Drivers
  • July payrolls shock resets the Fed narrative. The U.S. economy shed 23,000 jobs in July — the first monthly contraction in years — while wage growth came in below expectations. The data dramatically repriced September Fed rate-hike probability lower, lifting equities, compressing yields (short-end), and unleashing a powerful bid in rate-sensitive assets including gold and small-caps.
  • Gold miners post best week since 2008. The VanEck Gold Miners ETF (GDX) surged 21% on the week, its strongest performance since the 2008 financial crisis. Spot gold climbed over 7%, with major names such as Newmont (NEM +5.79%), Agnico Eagle (AEM +6.22%), and Wheaton Precious Metals (WPM +6.06%) leading gains as real-rate expectations fell sharply.
  • AI optical networking names rally on robust data-center demand. Applied Optoelectronics (AAOI) surged 13% after nearly doubling Q2 revenue to $191.9 million and returning to profitability, driven by hyperscale demand for high-speed transceivers. Coherent (COHR) and Lumentum (LITE) each gained 8–13% on the AI infrastructure tailwind.
  • The Trade Desk (TTD) collapses on weak guidance. TTD fell 21.90% on the highest volume of the session as disappointing programmatic advertising guidance extended the stock's streak of underperformance, highlighting that advertising budgets remain under pressure even as digital consumption grows.
Section 02
Macro

The July nonfarm payrolls contraction of 23,000 — combined with softer wage growth — constitutes a meaningful shift in the labor market backdrop and materially weakens the case for any additional Fed tightening in September. Markets are now pricing a hold as the dominant scenario, with some probability beginning to accrue to a cut before year-end, reinforcing the broader risk-on move seen on Friday. The Federal Reserve faces an increasingly delicate signaling challenge: inflation has not fully extinguished to target, yet continuing to hold at restrictive rates while the labor market softens risks an unnecessary economic contraction. Geopolitical risks — including Middle East tensions that have contributed to energy market volatility — remain a secondary driver, while U.S. fiscal deficit concerns continue to provide a structural underpinning to longer-dated Treasury yields even as the short end rallies.

Section 03
Equities

Friday's jobs shock delivered a broad-based equity rally: SPY gained +0.61% to $773.26 — hitting a record closing level — while QQQ outperformed at +1.17% to $723.03 as growth and tech stocks benefited most from the dovish rate repricing. IWM also advanced +1.11% to $301.56, with small-caps catching a meaningful bid as their above-average sensitivity to borrowing costs made them a prime beneficiary of the rate reset.

Notable movers (August 7):

  • Palantir (PLTR) gained +10.32% to $172.01 on 76 million shares, with AI platform momentum and renewed government contract optimism fueling a sharp re-rating as the dovish macro backdrop reduced the discount rate applied to long-duration growth.
  • The Trade Desk (TTD) plunged -21.90% to $13.80 on 133 million shares — the most actively traded session in the stock's recent history — as weak programmatic advertising guidance disappointed investors expecting a cyclical recovery in digital ad spending.
Section 04
Volatility

The CBOE VIX closed at 14.90, down -1.68% from its prior reading of 15.15 — firmly in complacent territory below 15. Despite the jobs shock delivering a significant macro surprise, markets absorbed the news constructively: equities rallied rather than panicking, reflecting the market's interpretation that weaker labor data is net positive (via lower rates) rather than recessionary. IV30 (30-day implied volatility on the VIX itself) rose sharply to 80.99 on the day, suggesting options traders are hedging for potential volatility around future macro events even as spot VIX remains subdued.

Section 05
Fixed Income

The U.S. yield curve presents a normally shaped, moderately upward-sloping profile as of the most recent readings (August 6): the 10-year Treasury yield stands at 4.69%, while the 2-year yield sits at 4.25%, generating a +44 basis point spread (10Y minus 2Y). This positive spread reflects the market pricing in a path toward eventual Fed easing, with the short end more directly anchored to policy rate expectations and the long end retaining a term premium linked to structural fiscal concerns. Following Friday's payrolls shock, the front end is likely to face additional downward pressure in the near term as rate-cut expectations continue to be pulled forward, which could further steepen the curve. The trajectory of this spread — currently 44bps and widening — will be a key gauge of how aggressively markets reprice the rate cycle in coming weeks.

Section 06
Currencies

EUR/USD trades at 1.1559, with the euro benefiting from broad-based U.S. dollar softness following the weaker-than-expected payrolls print, which reduced the interest rate differential advantage that has supported the greenback. A sustained break above 1.16 would mark a notable technical development suggesting continued dollar weakness. USD/JPY sits at 158.43, remaining near multi-decade highs as the Japanese yen continues to face structural pressure: the Bank of Japan's ultra-accommodative monetary stance stands in stark contrast to U.S. rates that remain well above neutral, sustaining the carry trade appeal of the dollar against the yen despite intervention risk at elevated levels.

Section 07
Commodities

Gold (XAUUSD) surged to $4,357.95/oz — hovering near all-time highs — driven by three converging forces: (1) collapsing real-rate expectations following the July jobs shock, which reduces the opportunity cost of holding non-yielding bullion; (2) persistent safe-haven demand tied to geopolitical uncertainty; and (3) speculative momentum amplified by an extraordinary weekly rally in gold miners (GDX +21%, best since 2008). The precious metals complex is now pricing a significant dovish pivot in Fed policy, and any surprise re-acceleration in inflation or labor data could trigger a sharp reversal.

WTI Crude most recently traded at $81.96/bbl (August 3), down from $86.16 at the end of July — a decline of approximately 4.87% — as concerns about demand softening in a slowing U.S. economy outweighed geopolitical supply-risk premium. OPEC+ production discipline and Middle East tensions provide a structural floor, but slowing U.S. growth expectations cap the upside case for crude in the near term.
