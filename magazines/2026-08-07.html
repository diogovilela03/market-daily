Market Drivers
  • AI infrastructure boom sustains Big Tech premium. Section 02
Macro

The Federal Reserve held rates steady at its July 30 meeting — the latest in a series of pauses — as policymakers weighed still-elevated inflation against a softer-than-expected GDP print. High short-end rates continue to suppress activity in rate-sensitive sectors including residential mortgage origination (United Wholesale Mortgage fell 35.6%) and growth-stage technology valuations. The August Nonfarm Payrolls report releases this morning (August 7); a materially above-consensus print could push back rate-cut expectations further and reprice the front end of the curve, while a miss would likely revive dovish bets. Separately, cooling consumer inflation and a strengthening manufacturing backdrop suggest the soft-landing scenario remains intact — but policy timing uncertainty keeps volatility regime risks elevated heading into Q3.

Section 03
Equities

Broad U.S. equity benchmarks slipped modestly on August 6: SPY fell -0.16% to 768.56, QQQ declined -0.37% to 714.65, and IWM underperformed at -0.51% to 298.25 — small-caps remaining most exposed to the prolonged high-rate environment. The tech-heavy Nasdaq softness reflected investor caution ahead of the NFP print despite strong underlying AI earnings momentum.

Notable movers:

  • Hertz (HTZ) surged +29.49% to $2.02 on 129 million shares — one of the most actively traded sessions in recent history, driven by a sharp short-squeeze dynamic as retail interest converged.
  • United Wholesale Mortgage (UWMC) plunged -35.60% to $1.185 on 110 million shares, as persistently high mortgage rates continue to compress origination volumes and margins for non-bank lenders.
Section 04
Volatility

The CBOE VIX settled at 15.15, down -4.36% on the session — firmly in complacent territory below the 20-threshold. The market's implied volatility premium reflects broad confidence in the soft-landing narrative and continued Big Tech earnings beats, though the compressed VIX reading heading into NFP morning creates an asymmetric setup: a significant payrolls surprise to either direction could reprice risk premiums quickly. At 15.15, hedging protection is relatively cheap for positioning ahead of key macro catalysts.

Section 05
Fixed Income

The 10-year Treasury yield held steady at 4.63% (unchanged from the prior session), while the 2-year yield eased to 4.18% from 4.20%, leaving the 2Y–10Y spread at +45 basis points — positively sloped and continuing its normalization from the deep inversion seen in late 2024. The curve's steepening trajectory reflects growing market confidence in eventual Fed easing further out the curve, even as front-end rates remain anchored by the Fed's steady-hand posture. The sustained 4%+ yield environment across the curve continues to pose a valuation headwind for growth-oriented equities, while making short-duration paper an attractive risk-free carry alternative.

Section 06
Currencies

EUR/USD held at 1.1526, reflecting continued euro resilience as improving European economic data and a pause in Fed tightening narrow the interest rate differential. USD/JPY remained elevated at 158.34, keeping pressure on the Bank of Japan to continue its policy normalization cycle. The persistent yen weakness has been a structural tailwind for Japanese financials — Mizuho (MFG) and Mitsubishi UFJ (MUFG) both posted multi-percent gains this week, driven by rising net interest margins and strong institutional investor demand as BoJ rate expectations shift higher. Any acceleration in BoJ hikes or Fed dovishness could catalyze a significant USD/JPY repricing.

Section 07
Commodities

Gold spot reached $4,291.77/oz, reflecting sustained safe-haven and inflation-hedge demand as investors position defensively around the NFP print and ongoing geopolitical uncertainty. Precious metals streaming names (Wheaton Precious Metals gained 6.85% on August 5) and gold miners (AngloGold Ashanti reporting today) benefit directly from elevated spot prices. WTI crude stood at $81.96/bbl as of August 3 — down sharply from $91.74 on July 24 (-4.87% w/w) — as OPEC+ supply management concerns ease and a cooling demand outlook from slower global growth moderates oil's upside. US-Iran tensions remain a geopolitical wildcard that could introduce a risk premium if diplomatic conditions deteriorate.
