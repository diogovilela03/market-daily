Section 01
Market Drivers
  • AI monetization inflecting: Palantir's Q2 beat sparked a broad rotation into AI-adjacent software names (CrowdStrike, RingCentral, Atlassian), signaling that enterprise AI revenue conversion is overtaking speculative infrastructure spending narratives.
  • Bank of Japan driving global yield repricing: Mizuho posted a 45% jump in Q1 net profit and MUFG delivered a 48% increase, both citing rising domestic rates and surging loan demand — BoJ normalization expectations are becoming a structural market theme.
  • US manufacturing at a four-year high: ISM PMI rose to 55.6 in July — seven consecutive months of expansion — signaling durable industrial demand and underpinning the soft-landing narrative.
  • US-Iran tension de-escalation compresses oil: Ceasefire diplomacy pulled WTI from near $89 to $81.96, relieving energy-driven inflation pressures and benefiting airlines, cruise operators, and rate-sensitive consumer sectors. July NFP data due Friday, August 7 — the next critical macro read.
Section 02
Macro
The Federal Reserve held rates unchanged at its most recent meeting, signaling a data-dependent stance as inflation remains modestly above its 2% target while GDP growth has moderated but avoided contraction. The combination of easing oil prices, improving manufacturing activity, and resilient consumer sentiment reinforces the soft-landing thesis. Friday's July Nonfarm Payrolls print is the next pivotal data point: a strong number could push rate-cut expectations further into late 2026, while softness would likely accelerate easing bets and steepen the yield curve.
Section 03
Equities

All three major ETFs closed lower on August 5: SPY –0.20% to $769.79, QQQ –0.90% to $717.30, IWM –0.64% to $299.77. Tech dragged the Nasdaq composite lower — Meta's 8.61% post-earnings decline on elevated AI capex guidance weighed heavily, partially offset by Microsoft Azure crossing $100B in annual revenue and Amazon's strong Q2 beat. The SPY–QQQ divergence reflects sector rotation within equities rather than broad risk-off positioning.

Notable movers: NVDA +3.43% (155M shares traded; Blackwell GPU supply ramp and cloud hyperscaler demand sustained the AI hardware narrative). YXT +632% (extreme low-float event; no macro read-through).

Section 04
Volatility
VIX at 15.81, down 4.36% on the session — comfortably within the moderate range (below the 20 elevated-fear threshold, above the 15 complacency threshold). The intraday high of 18.43 reflected brief hedging demand early in the session, subsequently fading as dip-buyers absorbed selling in tech. Options markets are not pricing meaningful tail risk heading into Friday's NFP print, suggesting investors view any labor-data deviation as manageable within the current policy framework rather than regime-shifting.
Section 05
Fixed Income
The 10-year Treasury yield eased 7 basis points to 4.63% and the 2-year fell 5 basis points to 4.20%, as markets positioned defensively ahead of Friday's NFP report. The 2Y–10Y spread stands at +43 basis points — a normally sloped curve that marks a decisive exit from the prolonged inversion of 2022–2024. Continued steepening is likely if the Fed begins cutting later in 2026; BoJ normalization also creates upward pressure on long-end global yields, making the curve shape an increasingly watched structural signal.
Section 06
Currencies
EUR/USD at 1.1549 reflects euro strength driven by improving Eurozone growth momentum and expectations of a narrowing US–EU rate differential as the Fed approaches its next easing cycle. USD/JPY at 157.77 shows the yen still under pressure despite BoJ rate hike expectations — carry traders appear unwilling to unwind short-yen positions while the real rate differential still favors USD. A more aggressive BoJ hike path would be the primary catalyst for a sharp yen rally and potential broad carry trade unwind, a significant tail risk for risk assets globally.
Section 07
Commodities
Gold holds at $4,264.16/oz, supported by persistent central bank accumulation, geopolitical uncertainty premium, and real yield compression as rate-cut expectations build for H2 2026. WTI crude at $81.96/bbl has retreated sharply from its recent ~$89 peak as US-Iran ceasefire signals strip the geopolitical premium from energy pricing; lower crude also reduces energy-driven CPI components, potentially clearing the path for an earlier Fed pivot. OPEC+ production discipline remains the primary structural floor; a breakdown in supply coordination would be the key downside risk for oil prices.
