Markets extended their summer advance Wednesday as AI infrastructure investment drove near-20% surges in CoreWeave and Super Micro Computer, the VIX dipped to a complacent 14.55, and all three major U.S. benchmarks posted gains. A four-year manufacturing high, fading rate-hike expectations, and a strong bank earnings season underpin the soft-landing narrative heading into a retail-heavy earnings week anchored by Home Depot, Lowe's, and Target.

01
Market Drivers
  • AI infrastructure buildout accelerates: CoreWeave (CRWV) surged 19.3% and Super Micro Computer (SMCI) jumped 19.0% on robust hyperscaler cloud demand, while NVIDIA added 3.0% on continued Blackwell platform adoption — optical networking names rallied in sympathy after Applied Optoelectronics nearly doubled quarterly revenue on AI datacenter orders.
  • Manufacturing at a four-year high: U.S. manufacturing activity marked its seventh consecutive month of expansion in July, bolstering the soft-landing narrative and lifting industrial and materials names across the board.
  • Rate-hike expectations extinguished: Weaker payrolls data released earlier this week erased lingering Fed tightening bets, sending growth stocks broadly higher; the S&P 500 subsequently hit fresh record territory.
  • Strong Q2 earnings season: Bank of America delivered an 8% EPS beat with 34% investment banking fee growth and now trades at record highs; MUFG reported a 48% net income surge — setting a confident tone heading into AMAT's post-market print tonight.
02
Macro
The Federal Reserve's tightening cycle appears effectively concluded, with weaker-than-expected payrolls data this week eliminating residual rate-hike bets and shifting market focus squarely to first-cut timing. Inflation continues to moderate — consumer staples companies cite easing input cost pressures, and consumer sentiment is visibly strengthening — while seven consecutive months of manufacturing expansion at a pace not seen since 2022 argues for durable economic momentum. Geopolitically, yen weakness at 159.40 against the dollar reflects the enduring U.S.–Japan rate differential and keeps Bank of Japan normalisation in the spotlight; any sharp yen appreciation remains the key macro tail-risk for global equity positioning.
03
Equities

All three major U.S. benchmarks advanced Wednesday in broad-based fashion. SPY gained 0.25% to $772.49; QQQ outperformed with a 0.73% rise to $723.70 as tech and growth led; IWM added 0.57% to $302.71, signalling healthy participation beyond mega-cap names.

Notable movers: CoreWeave (CRWV) +19.3% to $107.73 on AI cloud infrastructure momentum; Super Micro Computer (SMCI) +19.0% to $37.61 on strong datacenter order flow; Intel (INTC) +3.3% to $100.95 in exceptionally heavy volume, extending its semiconductor recovery; NVIDIA (NVDA) +3.0% to $224.09 on sustained Blackwell system demand from cloud hyperscalers.

04
Volatility
The CBOE VIX settled at 14.55, declining 5.0% on the session and breaching the conventional "complacency" threshold of 15. The subdued reading reflects broad investor confidence in the soft-landing scenario and limited near-term macro uncertainty. Historically, sustained VIX readings below 15 can signal crowded positioning — any meaningful miss on tonight's AMAT print, or an upside inflation surprise in the coming days, could trigger a sharper-than-expected volatility repricing.
05
Fixed Income
The 10-year Treasury yield eased to 4.70% and the 2-year declined to 4.22%, leaving the 10Y–2Y spread at a constructive +48 basis points — well within positive territory and continuing the post-inversion normalisation trend. This modest steepening bias aligns with improving growth expectations rather than reignited inflation fears, keeping duration risk manageable for now. The long end remains sensitive to any upside surprise in the upcoming August retail sales or jobs data; a meaningful re-acceleration in those prints could push the 10Y back toward 5%.
06
Currencies
EUR/USD holds firm at 1.1523, underpinned by a softer dollar as U.S. rate-hike expectations fade and eurozone data prints remain resilient. USD/JPY sits at 159.40, with the yen under persistent structural pressure despite the Bank of Japan's cautious policy normalisation steps — the enduring U.S.–Japan yield differential continues to attract yen-funded carry trades. A sharp and rapid yen appreciation would be the most significant near-term risk flag for global equities, given the potential scale of carry-trade unwinds still embedded in positioning.
07
Commodities
Gold spot extended its historic run to $4,375.37/oz, supported by dollar weakness, persistent central bank accumulation, and residual safe-haven demand. Gold miners posted their best weekly performance since 2008 earlier this month — Newmont (NEM) and Wheaton Precious Metals (WPM) each surged over 5–6% in recent sessions — as the metal's continued breakout attracts momentum flows. WTI crude firmed to $84.77/bbl, buoyed by the manufacturing revival's implied demand uplift and OPEC+ production discipline, though further upside faces headwinds from demand uncertainty in China and global consumer softness.
