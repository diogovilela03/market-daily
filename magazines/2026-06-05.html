Market Drivers
What’s Moving Markets
  • Broadcom’s AI chip miss cracked the semiconductor trade. AVGO shed 12.6% — its largest single-session drop in years — after a disappointing AI revenue forecast on 80M shares, pulling AMD, CRWD, and the broader semi complex lower and raising pointed questions about whether AI hardware capex growth is decelerating from its breakneck pace.
  • Wells Fargo surged as the Federal Reserve lifted its decade-long asset cap. WFC gained ~4% after the Fed officially terminated its long-standing enforcement action, a structural catalyst unlocking balance sheet expansion. MUFG added 3.3% in sympathy on Bank of Japan rate-hike expectations, anchoring the financial sector as tech sold off.
  • Oil near $98/barrel reignited the inflation and rate-hike debate. Yardeni Research issued an explicit call for a Fed rate hike on July 29 — an outlier view, but grounded in 3.8% YoY CPI and OPEC+ supply discipline. Consumer-facing stocks (DECK, CSV, RMAX, AXP) and rate-sensitive names bore the brunt of the re-pricing.
  • Bank earnings delivered a bifurcated verdict. NII-driven strength at BNS, MUFG, and OFG contrasted with elevated credit card delinquency (~13%) at US consumer-exposed banks — a “have vs. have-not” dynamic within financials tied to balance sheet positioning and regulatory exposure rather than macro direction alone.
Macro
The Fed, Oil, and a Narrowing Policy Corridor

The Federal Reserve faces a rapidly narrowing policy corridor: CPI at 3.8% YoY and WTI crude near $98/barrel are stoking reflation fears, yet credit card delinquency rates at 13% signal mounting consumer financial stress that makes aggressive tightening both economically and politically risky. Yardeni Research’s call for a July 29, 2026 rate hike — an explicit outlier against the consensus hold expectation — injected fresh hawkish uncertainty into rate markets, pushing both the 2Y and 10Y yields approximately 3 basis points higher on the session. A US–Iran ceasefire agreement had offered transient crude relief in May 2026, but renewed geopolitical tensions and persistent OPEC+ supply discipline have reasserted upward oil-price pressure heading into June, complicating the Fed’s inflation calculus and keeping dollar-denominated safe havens — gold, short-duration Treasuries — in firm demand.

Equities
Tech Stumbles, Small Caps Rotate Higher

The S&P 500 (SPY) eked out a +0.38% gain to $757.09, with financial sector strength from WFC (+~4%) and MUFG (+3.3%) offsetting deep tech weakness. The Nasdaq (QQQ) fell −0.48% to $740.61, dragged by Broadcom’s 12.6% plunge — the most consequential mega-cap decline of the session, reverberating across the semiconductor supply chain. The Russell 2000 (IWM) outperformed meaningfully, rising +1.51% to $292.01, consistent with a tactical rotation out of elevated-valuation AI and mega-cap names into domestic small-cap value.

Notable movers: AVGO −12.6% to $418.91 on 80M shares — AI chip revenue miss drove the session’s biggest mega-cap decline. NVDA +1.9% to $218.66 on 164M shares — relative resilience amid the semiconductor sell-off. MRVL (Marvell) +4.9% to $316.43 on 85M shares — potential beneficiary as AI infrastructure capital rotates away from Broadcom. Among standout micro-caps: STI surged +350.6% to $22.71 on 101.5M shares (likely a special situation / corporate event) and HUBC plunged −68.9% to $0.202 on 132.9M shares.

Fixed Income
Positive Slope Intact, Yields Drifting Higher

The 10-year Treasury yield stands at 4.49% and the 2-year at 4.08% (both as of June 3, 2026), with both tenors up approximately 3 basis points on the session. The yield curve holds a +41 basis point spread (10Y minus 2Y) — firmly in positive territory with no inversion. The curve’s modest steepening bias, driven by energy-fuelled long-end re-pricing, is consistent with markets beginning to discount a delayed but potentially higher terminal rate scenario; the front end is also rising as rate-cut pricing is unwound, but the long end is moving at a marginally faster pace as reflation risk commands a growing term premium.

Currencies
Euro Steady, Yen on Intervention Watch

EUR/USD trades at 1.1630, with the euro maintaining a firm bid against the dollar — reflecting the ECB’s more patient monetary stance relative to the growing Fed hawkish framing, alongside continued resilience in European economic indicators. USD/JPY has extended to 159.94, placing the yen at multi-year lows and keeping Japan’s Finance Ministry firmly on intervention watch. The Bank of Japan’s incremental yield curve control adjustments have provided insufficient support against the dollar’s elevated rate advantage; a sustained push through 160 could trigger direct FX intervention, as Japanese authorities have repeatedly identified that level as a threshold of heightened concern.

Commodities
Gold at $4,459 as Oil Approaches $100

Gold spot has surged to $4,459.74/oz, reflecting an extraordinary convergence of safe-haven demand drivers: persistent above-target inflation, geopolitical instability (Middle East tensions, US–Iran uncertainty), ongoing central bank reserve diversification away from dollar assets, and renewed expectations that the Fed may be forced to hike rather than cut. The price ascent shows no signs of abating while macro uncertainty remains structurally elevated. WTI crude oil stands at $95.96/barrel (latest available API data: June 1, 2026; news sources reference levels near $98 as of June 4–5, suggesting further intraday gains), with OPEC+ supply restraint and Middle East risk premia providing persistent floor support. A confirmed close above $100 would meaningfully re-accelerate inflation expectations and could force a more hawkish Fed response than currently discounted by markets.

Digital Assets

Regulatory / institutional: No new SEC, CFTC, or MiCA rulings this session. The Clarity Act legislative outcome is being actively traded in prediction markets, with Galaxy Digital executing a $10M OTC trade on Kalshi/Polymarket.
