Section 2
Macro

The Federal Reserve's higher-for-longer posture remains the dominant macro regime, with the 10Y Treasury at 4.74% reflecting persistent inflation expectations that have yet to give way despite over a year of restrictive policy. No Fed meeting or major data release (CPI, NFP) is scheduled in the next 48 hours, leaving the market to trade on the earnings calendar and yield signals. Globally, Japanese sovereign bond yields are rising sharply — Sumitomo Mitsui Financial Group shed 4.78% in a single session on August 19 as a broad sell-off in Japanese equities unfolded — adding an international dimension to fixed income volatility that may weigh on risk appetite into the week.

Section 3
Equities

All three major benchmarks closed lower Monday. SPY ended at 763.47 (−0.29%), QQQ at 706.32 (−1.00%), and IWM at 297.97 (−0.66%), with tech-heavy large-caps leading the decline. Small-caps showed relative resilience but still finished in the red.

Notable movers from Monday's session: NVIDIA (NVDA) dropped 2.91% to $208.48 on 131.7M shares — the day's most active large-cap — as options markets priced in a significant move ahead of Wednesday's post-market earnings. Intel (INTC) fell 3.12% to $87.26, continuing a pattern of underperformance vs. the broader chip complex. Semiconductor ETF SOXL declined 7.80%, amplifying sector weakness.

Section 4
Volatility

The CBOE Volatility Index (VIX) closed at 15.85, up 4.54% on the session. While this reading remains comfortably below the 20-level widely considered the fear threshold — and well beneath the "elevated stress" zone above 25 — the intraday move higher alongside broad equity weakness warrants monitoring. Markets are pricing moderate uncertainty, not systemic risk. The 30-day implied volatility measure (IV30) on VIX-linked instruments stood at 82.96%, signalling that options traders are hedging for further near-term swings, likely tied to the NVIDIA earnings catalyst on August 26.

Section 5
Fixed Income

The 10Y Treasury yield stands at 4.74% (daily close, August 21), up from 4.69% on August 20 — a move reflecting ongoing supply pressure and resilient US economic data that limits the case for near-term rate cuts. The 2Y yield is at 4.24%, similarly up from 4.19% the prior session.

The 2Y–10Y spread is +50 basis points, indicating a normal, positively-sloped curve that has steepened modestly in recent weeks. The curve is not inverted, removing one key recession signal. However, the level of yields across the curve — with the front end above 4% — continues to act as a gravitational drag on equity valuations, particularly for long-duration growth and housing-related names.

Currencies

EUR/USD trades at 1.1666, with the euro maintaining notable strength against the dollar. Dollar weakness relative to European peers reflects diverging growth narratives and the market's mixed read on Fed policy trajectory. A disorderly yen move remains a tail risk for cross-asset markets.

Section 7
Commodities

Gold spot is at $4,640.64/oz, trading at historically elevated levels and broadly sustained above $4,500 through August. The metal continues to attract demand as an inflation hedge and geopolitical safe haven, with central bank buying and real-yield compression providing structural support. Equities linked to gold production (Newmont +3.18% on Aug 21) are following spot higher, confirming the bullish consensus for now.

WTI crude oil most recently closed at $86.48/bbl (daily close August 18), rebounding strongly from the early-August low of $76.78. OPEC+ supply discipline and recovering global demand expectations — particularly from Asia — have underpinned the recovery. The $86–88 range represents a key technical area; a sustained break higher could reignite inflation concerns.
