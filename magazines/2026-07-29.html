02 · Macro
Macro Backdrop

The yield curve remains positively sloped — the 10-year at 4.65% and the 2-year at 4.31%, a +34 basis-point spread — continuing the gradual steepening from the deep inversion seen in prior years, consistent with markets pricing Fed rate cuts over a multi-quarter horizon without signaling recession. With the FOMC meeting in its standard late-July window, rate-sensitive assets will closely track any commentary on the pace and timing of eventual cuts.

03 · Equities
Equities

04 · Volatility
Volatility

The VIX settled at 18.21, off 2.53% from the prior close, sitting in the neutral band between complacency (<15) and elevated fear (>20). The absence of a volatility spike — despite Nasdaq weakness and peak-earnings uncertainty — suggests options markets are pricing event risk as contained rather than systemic. A sustained move above 20 would warrant reassessment of near-term hedging postures.

05 · Fixed Income
Fixed Income

The 10-year Treasury yield stands at 4.65% and the 2-year at 4.31%, yielding a 10Y–2Y spread of +34 basis points. The curve's positive slope signals the market's working assumption that the Fed will ease ahead of the next cycle trough — a steepening trend that has been building since the inversion of recent quarters. Both maturities ticked modestly lower on the session (10Y −4bp, 2Y −2bp), reflecting mild flight-to-quality demand without conviction toward duration. No inversion risk at current levels.

06 · Currencies
Currencies

EUR/USD trades at 1.1399, near multi-month highs, as the dollar continues to face headwinds from rate-cut expectations and a narrowing US fiscal impulse. The level reflects a meaningful euro premium over the dollar and could face resistance should the FOMC deliver a more hawkish-than-expected tone. USD/JPY at 163.53 remains historically elevated despite the Bank of Japan's gradual pivot toward policy normalization; the wide US–Japan rate differential continues to anchor the pair well above 160, keeping intervention risk in the background for Tokyo policymakers.

07 · Commodities
Commodities

The metal's march above $4,000 reflects a secular repricing of real assets in an era of persistent fiscal deficits. WTI crude (most recent available data: $84.38/barrel, July 20) remains well above the $80 breakeven level targeted by most OPEC+ members, supported by production restraint and resilient global demand despite slowing growth expectations in Europe and China.
