Macro

Fed Decision Looms; Yields Tick Higher

The Federal Reserve's July FOMC meeting concludes Wednesday, July 29, with near-universal market consensus expecting rates held steady. Treasury yields edged higher last session — the 10-year rising 4 basis points to 4.71% and the 2-year climbing 6 basis points to 4.37% — as persistent inflation prints and a resilient labour market leave policymakers with limited justification to ease. The focus will be on whether the statement acknowledges progress on disinflation or signals a longer hold than markets have priced.

USD/JPY holds above 163.48, underscoring the widening rate differential between the Fed's restrictive stance and the Bank of Japan's ultra-accommodative posture. Tokyo's verbal intervention threats have so far failed to durably reverse yen weakness, and any surprise hawkishness from the Fed on Wednesday could push the pair toward 165 — a level that would intensify pressure on Japanese authorities to act.

Equities

Bifurcated Market: Tech Sells Off as Value Holds

SPY closed Friday at 738.93 (+0.10%), essentially flat as consumer staples and financials absorbed the blow from technology. QQQ fell to 684.23 (−1.12%), weighed by the semiconductor complex and pre-earnings de-risking in mega-cap tech names. IWM slipped to 291.17 (−0.32%), with small caps showing relative resilience against Nasdaq's underperformance.

  • Intel (INTC) −7.89% on volume of 179M shares — the session's standout large-cap loser, as guidance cuts from sector peers signalled broader demand deterioration in the chip cycle. Applied Materials, Marvell, and Western Digital all fell 3–5% in sympathy.
  • STAK +602% on 58.9M shares — an extraordinary single-day spike driven by a company-specific catalyst; not representative of broader market sentiment but notable for follow-through risk.

Volatility

VIX at 18.58 — Cautious, Not Panicked

VIX closed Friday at 18.58, down 0.65% on the session, sitting in the moderate zone between the complacency threshold (<15) and the elevated-fear threshold (>20). Despite a historically heavy earnings calendar and an imminent FOMC decision, options markets are not pricing outright panic — a sign that the base case remains a soft-landing trajectory. A sustained move above 20 would signal a material regime shift and warrant repositioning in volatility-sensitive strategies.

Fixed Income

Curve Steepening Continues; 10Y at 4.71%

The 10-year Treasury yield rose to 4.71% (+4bps from the prior session), while the 2-year yield climbed to 4.37% (+6bps). The 2Y–10Y spread stands at +34 basis points, reflecting a gently steepening curve in which longer-dated rates are rising faster than the front end — a pattern typically associated with markets pricing sustained elevated rates rather than an imminent Fed easing cycle. Watch for the spread to widen further if Wednesday's FOMC statement reinforces a "higher for longer" stance; a compression back toward zero would instead signal renewed inversion fears.

Currencies

EUR/USD Steady at 1.14; Yen Remains Under Pressure

EUR/USD trades at 1.1412, holding steady as dollar demand remains contained ahead of Wednesday's FOMC outcome. A hawkish surprise — or language ruling out near-term cuts — could push the pair toward the 1.13 handle as dollar bulls step in. USD/JPY holds at 163.48, a level that has historically triggered verbal intervention from Tokyo; the yen's structural weakness reflects the 350+ basis point carry advantage the dollar commands over Japanese government bond yields, a gap the Bank of Japan shows no urgency to close. Any BOJ surprise — while unlikely — would be a high-volatility catalyst for the pair.

Commodities

Gold Above $4,100; WTI Firm at $84

Gold spot reached $4,102.16, a historically elevated level reflecting persistent safe-haven demand, sustained central bank accumulation from emerging market reserve managers, and a global fiscal backdrop that anchors real rates below nominal yields. The metal has now held above $4,000 for multiple sessions, suggesting structural demand rather than tactical repositioning.

WTI crude stands at $84.38, supported by OPEC+ supply discipline and geopolitical risk premiums in the Middle East. Demand-side risks from China's uneven recovery and rising U.S. shale output remain the primary headwinds against a sustained push toward $90, though energy sector stocks have been responding positively to the elevated price environment.
