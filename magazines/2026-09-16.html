Section 02

Macro

The US 10-year Treasury yield climbed to 4.97% — approaching the psychologically significant 5% threshold — following a robust jobs report that reinforced a higher-for-longer Federal Reserve posture. The 2-year yield rose in tandem to 4.65%, keeping the curve mildly positively sloped at +32 basis points, a reflection of back-end repricing rather than front-end easing expectations. Markets now assign minimal probability to a Fed rate cut before Q1 2027, and the "higher for longer" narrative has reasserted itself as consensus.

Elevated real yields continue to challenge growth equity valuations, compress housing affordability, and widen leverage-sensitive credit spreads. Geopolitical backdrop remains a secondary but persistent tail risk, supporting defensive asset positioning and gold demand.

Section 03

Equities

All three major US equity benchmarks declined Tuesday: SPY fell 0.46% to 757.39, QQQ dropped 0.65% to 704.54, and IWM lost 0.70% to 285.14. Small-caps underperformed modestly, consistent with elevated sensitivity to tighter credit conditions and higher floating-rate debt burdens. NVDA (+0.57%) remained resilient within the most-actively-traded universe, sustained by persistent AI infrastructure investment demand.

Notable movers (top gainers/losers):

  • VEEA +149.3% (198M shares) — high-volume speculative surge in a thin-float micro-cap; event-driven, not a macro signal.
  • FEED −69.3% — sharp sell-off on heavy selling pressure; limited large-cap read-through.

Section 04

Volatility

The CBOE VIX edged up 0.58% to 17.20, settling in the neutral zone (15–20). This level signals a market that is cautious but not panicked — investors are hedging modestly against rate uncertainty but have not yet moved into fear-driven protection demand. A sustained close above 20 would indicate elevated stress and the potential for accelerated de-risking. Current levels remain complacent relative to the underlying macro volatility in rates.

Section 05

Fixed Income

The US 10-year Treasury yield stands at 4.97%, approaching the 5% handle last seen earlier this year, while the 2-year yield sits at 4.65%. The resulting 2s–10s spread of +32 basis points keeps the curve positively sloped — an un-inversion maintained since mid-2025 — but the steepening dynamic is driven by back-end pressure from revised growth and inflation expectations rather than front-end rate-cut pricing. This bear-steepening environment is historically associated with tightening financial conditions and softer risk asset performance. Investment-grade and high-yield spreads are likely widening in sympathy with the rates move.

Section 06

Currencies

EUR/USD: 1.1551 — The euro holds above the 1.15 handle, supported by relatively firm ECB messaging and a narrowing in transatlantic rate differentials as the ECB has maintained a tightening bias. The pair is range-bound as both central banks signal data-dependency, limiting directional conviction in the near term.

USD/JPY: 155.05 — The yen remains pinned near multi-decade lows against the dollar, a persistent reflection of the stark divergence between the Fed's elevated terminal rate and the Bank of Japan's accommodative stance. BoJ intervention risk is elevated at these levels, with Japanese officials likely monitoring the 155–157 zone closely for any disorderly yen depreciation that could prompt verbal or physical intervention.

Section 07

Commodities

Gold: $4,333.74/oz — Precious metals continue to trade at structurally elevated levels, underpinned by geopolitical risk premium, sustained central bank buying (particularly from emerging market central banks diversifying away from USD reserves), and hedging demand against macro tail risks. Gold's resilience despite rising real yields underscores a structural demand shift that defies conventional rate-driven models.

WTI Crude: $97.26/bbl — Oil remains close to the $100 threshold, supported by continued OPEC+ supply discipline and robust US demand data signaled by the strong jobs report. Elevated crude prices complicate the Fed's inflation narrative and reinforce the case for a higher-for-longer policy stance, creating a self-reinforcing tightening feedback loop for energy-intensive sectors.
