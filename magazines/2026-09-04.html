Section 02
Macro

The Federal Reserve remains in a data-dependent holding pattern, with the market closely watching each payroll and inflation print for clues on the timing of any rate adjustment. Persistent inflation and signs of slowing consumer spending — highlighted in recent retail sector commentary — leave the Fed little room for complacency, even as the disinflationary trend remains broadly intact. Geopolitically, energy supply dynamics continue to exert upward pressure on commodity prices, adding a secondary inflation layer that complicates the path to the Fed's 2% target.

Section 04
Volatility

The CBOE VIX dropped 6.14% to 14.32, firmly below the 15 complacency threshold and well clear of the fear zone above 20. Markets are pricing minimal near-term tail risk, a signal that option sellers are in command and the put-protection bid remains muted. The VIX's decline alongside rising equities reflects a textbook risk-on session, though the sub-15 reading historically warrants vigilance — complacency can unwind sharply when macro surprises arrive.

Section 05
Fixed Income

The U.S. Treasury curve held steady with the 10-year yield at 4.79% and the 2-year at 4.39%, producing a positive 10Y–2Y spread of +40 basis points. The curve's return to a normal (upward-sloping) shape, after the prolonged inversion of prior years, marks a notable structural shift — suggesting markets are pricing in eventual Fed easing without panicking about near-term recession risk. The 10-year's persistence near 4.80% continues to set a high hurdle rate for equity valuations, particularly in long-duration growth names, though the market has thus far absorbed this comfortably amid strong earnings momentum.

Section 06
Currencies

EUR/USD traded at 1.1632 in early New York hours, reflecting euro resilience as the ECB navigates its own rate-path deliberations against a backdrop of sluggish European growth. USD/JPY held at 156.36, remaining elevated and far above the 150 level that previously drew Japanese Ministry of Finance intervention warnings — a level that will draw continued scrutiny as the Bank of Japan gradually moves away from ultra-accommodative policy. The yen's sustained weakness remains a pressure point for Asian equity markets and commodity importers, even as carry trade dynamics keep broad dollar strength intact.

Section 07
Commodities

Gold held above $4,469.65/oz, extending its multi-month advance driven by a combination of central bank accumulation, persistent inflation hedging demand, and geopolitical risk premia. The metal's position near record highs — significantly above the $4,300 level cited in recent Wheaton Precious Metals commentary — reinforces its safe-haven narrative even as equities rally. WTI crude oil was last priced at $91.48/bbl (September 1 close), its highest reading in weeks, supported by OPEC+ supply discipline and recovering global demand. Elevated oil prices are a double-edged sword: bullish for energy producers and energy equities, but a reflationary headwind for the Fed and consumer purchasing power.
