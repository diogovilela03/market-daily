Section 01
Market Drivers
  • Geopolitical relief rally: A US-Iran agreement to cease military exchanges triggered a broad risk-on move, sending equities higher and pulling energy prices lower. Cybersecurity and enterprise software names (Okta, F5, Palo Alto Networks, Tenable) surged as geopolitical risk premia unwound.
  • "SaaSpocalypse" fears recede: Investor anxiety around AI displacing traditional SaaS revenue models — a theme that had weighed on software valuations — eased on Monday, providing a second leg to the tech bid. QQQ outperformed with a 2.49% gain.
  • Mega-cap divergence from small caps: IWM slipped 0.29% even as SPY and QQQ rallied, underscoring that Monday's risk-on was a large-cap phenomenon. Semiconductor names (NVDA +1.27%, INTC +2.65%) contributed meaningfully; SMCI fell 8.1% on heavy volume, a notable outlier.
  • Inflation overhang persists: Huntington Bank's weekly economic brief flagged higher-than-expected recent inflation readings, reinforcing that the Federal Reserve remains in extended hold mode despite the equity rally. Rate cut expectations continue to be pushed out.
Section 02
Macro

The Federal Reserve's wait-and-see posture remains firmly intact, underpinned by sticky inflation that continues to print above target. The Middle East de-escalation has provided a significant tailwind — reducing energy price volatility and, by extension, lowering the probability of a fresh inflationary impulse from commodity costs. On the geopolitical front, the US-Iran ceasefire on military exchanges is the single most consequential near-term variable for global risk appetite, though market participants remain cautious about its durability. With no Fed decision or major data release (CPI, NFP) scheduled in the next two trading days, macro catalysts will be driven by corporate earnings and any deterioration in the Middle East truce.

Section 03
Equities

SPY closed at $741.00 (+1.65%), driven by tech and communication services. QQQ led the major ETFs at $724.08 (+2.49%), its strongest session in several weeks, as AI-adjacent and enterprise software names rallied. IWM was the session's laggard at $298.97 (-0.29%), reflecting the mega-cap concentration of the risk-on move and ongoing pressure on rate-sensitive small-cap balance sheets.

Notable movers: Satellite Digital Infrastructure (SDOT) was Monday's standout gainer, surging +67.1% to $35.85 on volume of 9.6 million shares. SuperMicro Computer (SMCI) was the headline decliner among active names, falling -8.1% to $28.15 on 89.8 million shares, continuing its protracted drawdown. Comcast (CMCSA) gained +4.53% on 133.9 million shares, and Constellation Brands (STZ) steadied ahead of tonight's earnings.

Section 04
Volatility

The CBOE Volatility Index (VIX) closed at 17.65, falling 0.76 points (-4.31%) on the day as the Middle East de-escalation drained risk premium from the options market. At 17.65, VIX sits comfortably in the neutral zone — above the complacency threshold of 15, but well clear of the elevated-fear level of 20. Intraday the index touched a low of 17.49 before settling, suggesting the market is cautiously optimistic but not fully pricing out tail risk. The iv30 (30-day implied volatility) also declined, indicating the market does not anticipate a near-term volatility spike.

Section 05
Fixed Income

The US Treasury curve is positively sloped and continuing to steepen. The 10-year yield most recently printed at 4.38% (down from 4.41% earlier in the week), while the 2-year yield stands at 4.07% (down from 4.11%). The 10Y-2Y spread is approximately +31 basis points — a steepening dynamic that represents a meaningful normalization from the prolonged inversion seen through much of 2024-2025. A positive curve slope is structurally constructive for bank net interest margins and suggests the market is beginning to price a more normal rate environment. The move lower in yields is consistent with geopolitical risk-off in Treasuries being partially unwound, though sticky inflation keeps the long end elevated on an absolute basis.

Section 06
Currencies

EUR/USD trades at 1.1409, with the euro maintaining strength relative to the US dollar. Dollar softness on Monday was consistent with the risk-on backdrop — geopolitical de-escalation typically reduces safe-haven demand for USD. The euro has benefited from relative stability in European growth expectations and a hawkish ECB communication tone.

USD/JPY sits at 162.19, with the yen remaining deeply weak against the dollar. The yen's persistent weakness reflects the wide US-Japan rate differential — with Japan's policy rate still far below US rates — and continued intervention fatigue. A USD/JPY above 160 keeps BOJ intervention risk on the table, though actual intervention has been limited. A meaningful JPY reversal would likely require either Fed rate cuts or a more aggressive BOJ pivot, neither of which appears imminent.

Section 07
Commodities

Gold spot prices remain elevated at $4,027.96/oz, sustaining their position well above the $4,000 psychological level. The persistence of gold at record-high territory even during a risk-on equity rally is notable — it speaks to structural safe-haven demand driven by reserve diversification (central bank buying), persistent inflation uncertainty, and geopolitical fragmentation rather than pure fear. Gold's resilience in the face of a positive yield curve also challenges the traditional inverse relationship between real rates and gold.

WTI crude most recently printed at $78.94/bbl, completing a dramatic decline from $99.76 on June 3 — a drop of over 20% in less than a month. Monday's Middle East de-escalation was the latest catalyst pressuring crude, adding to a confluence of bearish drivers: OPEC+ production increases, demand uncertainty from China, and easing supply disruption fears. WTI's collapse from near-$100 is deflationary at the margin and could provide the Federal Reserve with additional cover if energy disinflation begins feeding through to CPI prints in coming months.

Section 08
Digital Assets

Bitcoin is pinned below $60,000 and trading in a technical bear flag near 52-week lows, with price action struggling to find institutional support. US spot Bitcoin ETFs logged a fifth consecutive session of net outflows on June 29, shedding $231.1 million — with BlackRock's IBIT alone redeeming $300 million. Ethereum spot ETFs registered their eighth straight day of outflows ($30 million), even as corporate treasury buyers (Bitmine, SharpLink) attempted to absorb selling by accumulating ETH. The brief push above $1,600 in ETH failed to hold. ARK Invest deployed $43.5 million into crypto-linked equities (Coinbase, Circle) during the selloff — a contrarian accumulation signal, but insufficient to reverse the broader sentiment.

Regulatory developments: Australia has enforced strict KYC requirements for all crypto transfers on regulated exchanges, with no minimum amount threshold — a significant compliance burden for retail users. In Europe, the approaching MiCA regulatory deadline is accelerating a migration of crypto firms to Dubai, which is positioning itself as a permissive alternative jurisdiction. In the US, Galaxy Research cut the odds of the CLARITY Act (digital asset market structure legislation) passing in 2026 to 50%, citing a compressed Senate calendar.

Overall sentiment: Bearish. Persistent ETF outflows, a bearish technical setup in Bitcoin, and accumulating regulatory headwinds across multiple jurisdictions present a challenging near-term backdrop for digital assets. Strategy (Michael Saylor) continues to tease additional Bitcoin purchases and has launched a new capital framework with a $2.55 billion reserve, providing a floor of institutional conviction — but it is not currently enough to reverse the trend.
