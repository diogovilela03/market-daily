With major banks — JPMorgan, Goldman Sachs, and Citigroup — reporting beginning Monday, the market's next direction will hinge on whether corporate earnings can justify still-elevated multiples in a 5%-plus rate environment.

  • Tech and semiconductors led the market lower — Nvidia (NVDA) fell 2.94% to $230.48 and Intel (INTC) shed 5.34% to $107.08, dragging the QQQ down 1.34% as investors rotated out of high-valuation growth ahead of earnings season.

Section 02
Macro

The Federal Reserve remains in a data-dependent holding pattern as 10-year Treasury yields hold at 5.28%, reinforcing market consensus that high-for-longer rates are firmly entrenched well into 2026. The modestly steepening yield curve — with the 10Y–2Y spread at +51 basis points — suggests investors are beginning to price in longer-term growth concerns even as near-term inflation remains sticky. Elevated energy costs and geopolitical tensions continue to complicate the Fed's calculus on any potential easing path, keeping terminal rate assumptions elevated heading into the bank earnings season.

Section 03
Equities

SPY ended Thursday at $773.93 (−0.42%), while QQQ dropped more sharply to $747.58 (−1.34%) as technology names bore the brunt of selling pressure. IWM was nearly flat at $277.57 (−0.05%), underscoring the relative outperformance of small caps versus mega-cap tech in the session. Among notable movers, Pacira BioSciences (PCRX) surged +44.4% to $36.39 on heavy volume — a standout on the gainers board. On the downside, the semiconductor complex continued to struggle: Intel (INTC) dropped 5.34% and Nvidia (NVDA) fell 2.94%, while leveraged semiconductor ETF SOXL shed over 10% in the session.

Section 04
Fixed Income

The 10-year Treasury yield held at 5.28% while the 2-year yield sat at 4.77%, leaving the curve with a positive 10Y–2Y spread of +51 basis points — a modestly steepening configuration. The absence of inversion signals markets are not yet pricing an imminent recession, though yields at these levels continue to pressure rate-sensitive sectors including real estate, utilities, and highly leveraged growth companies. Investors will closely monitor Q3 bank earnings beginning Monday for forward guidance on net interest margins, credit quality, and deposit dynamics in the current rate environment.

Section 05
Currencies

EUR/USD trades at 1.1233, reflecting modest dollar softness against the euro as markets digest mixed U.S. growth signals and an uncertain global macro backdrop. USD/JPY holds elevated at 158.20, with the yen continuing to weaken as the wide interest rate differential between the U.S. and Japan remains dominant — despite periodic verbal intervention signals from the Bank of Japan. The 160.00 handle remains a closely watched threshold where BoJ intervention risk historically increases, keeping currency traders alert to any sudden policy shift from Tokyo.

Section 06
Commodities

Gold reached $4,194.52 spot, holding near record territory as persistent macro uncertainty, elevated geopolitical risk, and dollar-hedge demand continue to attract flows to the metal. WTI crude oil held at $96.24 per barrel — slightly off the $99.77 peak seen on October 1 but remaining comfortably above the $90 psychological support level. OPEC+ supply discipline, combined with ongoing demand from Asia and elevated transportation fuel costs, continues to provide a structural floor under energy prices heading into the Northern Hemisphere winter season.

Section 07
Earnings Radar

Q3 earnings season begins in earnest this week, with Delta Air Lines reporting today and the major U.S. banks launching a critical barrage of reports starting Monday. Consensus focus is on net interest margins, loan growth, credit reserve builds, and forward guidance on the consumer given the persistent 5%-plus rate backdrop. Any guidance indicating deteriorating credit quality or compressed NIM could weigh meaningfully on financials and broader market sentiment.
