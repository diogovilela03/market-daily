Section 2

Macro

The Federal Reserve's higher-for-longer posture continues to dominate market structure, with 10-year yields at 4.96% reflecting market consensus that the policy rate is unlikely to ease materially before mid-2027. The 2s10s spread turning meaningfully positive at +31bps marks a departure from the prolonged inversion and may signal improving growth expectations at the margin — though the level of absolute rates remains restrictive for credit-sensitive sectors. A still-elevated oil price above $107 adds complexity to the disinflation narrative, keeping headline CPI risks tilted to the upside as Q4 unfolds.

Section 3

Equities

US equity markets ended last week on a cautious note: the S&P 500 tracker SPY slipped 0.52% to $759.97, while the Nasdaq-100 ETF QQQ held near flat at $704.40 (−0.01%), suggesting resilience in large-cap tech even as broader sentiment softened. Small-cap IWM underperformed at −0.33% to $275.75, consistent with the elevated rate environment penalizing rate-sensitive smaller companies disproportionately.

Notable movers (Oct 23): AIOK surged +182.8% to $12.02 on 41M shares — an outsized catalyst-driven move suggesting a significant news event. On the downside, SHFS collapsed −61.4% to $0.1324 on extraordinary volume of 175M shares, indicating a material company-specific event such as a dilutive financing or adverse regulatory action.

Section 4

Fixed Income

The 10-year Treasury yield stands at 4.96%, within touching distance of the 5% level that has historically triggered heightened equity volatility. The 2-year yield at 4.65% positions the 2s10s curve at a positive +31 basis points — a modestly normal shape that represents a meaningful shift from the prolonged inversion seen earlier this cycle. Both tenors moved lower week-on-week (10Y: −17bps, 2Y: −16bps), potentially reflecting some cooling in near-term rate expectations ahead of key macro data and the peak of earnings season. The curve shape is steepening, not inverted.

Section 5

Currencies

EUR/USD at 1.1214 — the euro has sustained its recovery above 1.10, supported by relative ECB hawkishness and a softening dollar as US rate expectations consolidate. A break above 1.13 would represent a significant technical level. USD/JPY at 157.08 remains stubbornly elevated, testing tolerance thresholds that previously triggered Bank of Japan intervention. The yen's persistent weakness reflects the divergence between the BOJ's ultra-accommodative stance and the Fed's restrictive posture; any surprise hawkish pivot from Tokyo could deliver an outsized correction in the pair.

Section 6

Commodities

Gold at $4,004.92/oz has crossed the $4,000 milestone for the first time, underpinned by sustained central bank accumulation, de-dollarization demand from emerging markets, and the persistent uncertainty around the global rate cycle. The metal's strength alongside elevated yields is unusual and reinforces the safe-haven and inflation-hedge thesis. WTI crude at $107.89/bbl eased 1.93% from the prior session but remains well above the $100 level, driven by supply discipline from OPEC+ and resilient global demand. Elevated energy costs continue to pose a headwind for corporate margins and consumer spending heading into Q4.
