US equity markets staged a powerful recovery on June 11, led by a 3.38% surge in the Nasdaq-100 as semiconductor and tech names — battered by last week's blowout May jobs report — mounted a sharp oversold bounce. The VIX's 14.3% decline to 19.44 and broad participation across small- and large-caps signal that markets have largely repriced the "higher-for-longer" Fed backdrop, with the Israel-Iran ceasefire providing an additional tailwind to risk appetite.

Section 01
Market Drivers
  • Semiconductor recovery leads the charge. QQQ surged 3.38% as Intel (INTC +9.27%) and Super Micro Computer (SMCI +9.22%) rebounded from deeply oversold levels. The 3× inverse semiconductor ETF SOXS plunged 24.66%, confirming broad-based tech buying and a decisive unwind of defensive hedges.
  • Israel-Iran ceasefire improves geopolitical risk premium. Reports of a ceasefire deal lifted equity futures overnight and sustained gains through the session, with risk-sensitive assets across equities and credit moving in tandem. Oil's reaction was muted, held up by residual Strait of Hormuz supply concerns.
  • May CPI/PPI data being absorbed. Released June 10–11, the latest inflation prints reinforced the "higher-for-longer" narrative but did not worsen the macro picture materially. Markets appear to be treating the data as largely priced-in following last week's NFP-driven shock repricing.
  • Risk-off unwind sweeps across asset classes. VIX fell 14.3% to 19.44 as IV sellers stepped in aggressively, inverse ETFs (SQQQ −9.77%, SPDN −1.78%) gave back recent gains, and small caps (IWM +2.96%) confirmed a broad-based, not just mega-cap, recovery in risk appetite.
Section 02
Macro

The macro backdrop remains shaped by May's blowout NFP, which crushed rate-cut expectations and forced a broad "higher-for-longer" repricing across asset classes last week. The subsequent May CPI and PPI prints (released June 10–11) kept inflation front-and-center but did not materially worsen the picture, allowing equities to stabilize. New Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh is viewed by some investors as more open to easing if AI-driven productivity gains accelerate — a theme that is beginning to offset the hawkish consensus, at least at the margin.

Geopolitically, the Israel-Iran ceasefire reduced near-term tail risk and buoyed risk assets. However, OPEC+ supply discipline and residual Strait of Hormuz risk continue to underpin crude near $95/bbl. Markets are now in a data-watching mode, with no major US macro releases expected in the immediate near term.

Section 04
Volatility

The CBOE VIX declined 14.30% to 19.44, sitting just below the psychologically important 20.00 threshold that separates an elevated-fear from a complacent regime. The compression was sharp and broad-based: IV sellers stepped in aggressively as the market unwound its post-NFP defensive hedges. At 19.44, VIX is in a transitional zone — another leg lower toward 15 would confirm full complacency, while any fresh macro catalyst could rapidly push it back above 20. Intraday low touched 19.36, reinforcing that options markets are actively repricing risk lower.

Section 05
Fixed Income

The 10-year Treasury yield stands at 4.55% while the 2-year yields 4.13%, producing a 10Y–2Y spread of +42 basis points — a positively sloped, gently steepening curve. This configuration reflects the market's expectation that the Fed will hold short rates steady while the long end prices in gradually improving growth prospects and a modest term premium rebuild. The curve is not inverted, and there is no immediate inversion risk at these spread levels. The May CPI and PPI data, while confirming lingering stickiness, did not shift the curve dynamics materially. Markets now await further Fed communication and the next employment or inflation release for the next directional catalyst.

Section 06
Currencies

EUR/USD 1.1571 — The euro is trading firmly above 1.15, a level that reflects broad-based dollar softening as geopolitical risk premiums retreat following the ceasefire and markets accept that the Fed's hiking cycle is behind us. The EUR has additionally benefited from resilient Eurozone data and reduced European energy shock risk.

USD/JPY 160.34 — The yen remains under sustained pressure at 160.34, approaching levels that historically trigger intervention warnings from Japanese monetary authorities. The Bank of Japan's cautious pace of policy normalization preserves the wide US-Japan rate differential, keeping yen carry trades attractive and the currency structurally weak. A sustained move above 162 would likely escalate BOJ verbal intervention risk and market volatility around the pair.

Section 07
Commodities

Gold $4,180.75/oz — Gold is trading near historic highs, supported by structural central bank demand, a weakening dollar (EUR/USD above 1.15), and persistent geopolitical uncertainty that ceasefire headlines alone cannot displace. De-dollarization trends and inflation hedging demand remain the primary drivers. Gold mining equities have recently underperformed spot (NEM −3.3% earlier in the week), a typical divergence in elevated real-rate environments where cost pressures squeeze margins.

WTI Crude $95.00/bbl — WTI holds at $95, sustained by OPEC+ production discipline and a residual Strait of Hormuz supply risk premium that did not fully unwind on ceasefire headlines. A durable Middle East de-escalation would likely test downside toward the $88–90 range, but OPEC+ capacity management and geopolitical uncertainty provide a near-term floor under crude.
