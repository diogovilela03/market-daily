⚡ WATCH
Market Drivers
  • Iran ceasefire collapses: President Trump declared the tentative US–Iran ceasefire "over," saying "We hit them very hard last night — we'll probably hit them hard again tonight." The announcement reignited geopolitical risk premium across energy and safe-haven assets.
  • Oil shock cascades into rates: WTI crude surged 5.12% to $74.04/barrel on fears of Middle East supply disruption. Treasury yields edged higher as investors repriced the inflation risk from an energy spike, complicating the Fed's path toward rate normalisation.
  • Chip deals provide a tech floor: Broadcom rallied 4.8% after expanding its agreement with Apple to manufacture US-made components, while Nvidia gained 3.6% on reports that Chinese firms plan to significantly increase H200 chip purchases.
  • Credit-sensitive names sold off: JPMorgan shed 2.5% and Visa fell 1.3% as rising yields and a deteriorating macro backdrop pressured financials and consumer payment volumes ahead of Q2 bank earnings.
Section 2
Macro

The Fed's disinflation narrative took a fresh hit as oil's geopolitical surge reintroduced upside inflation risk ahead of the June CPI print (due July 14). The 10-year Treasury yield climbed to 4.56% — its highest level since early June — as investors repositioned for a potentially stickier price environment, while the 2-year yield ticked up 2 basis points to 4.21%, keeping the yield curve in positive territory at +35bps. With no Fed speakers scheduled in the next two sessions and the FOMC's next meeting still weeks away, the market will be laser-focused on the CPI data as the swing factor for rate-cut timing.

Section 3
Equities

US equities closed broadly lower on Wednesday, with the S&P 500 proxy SPY off 0.59% to $743.14, QQQ (Nasdaq-100) down 0.50% to $711.44, and small-cap IWM (Russell 2000) the relative laggard at -0.91% to $293.48 — reflecting small-caps' heightened sensitivity to both rate moves and domestic economic sentiment.

Notable movers:

  • Broadcom (AVGO) +4.8% — Expanded US chip manufacturing agreement with Apple boosted sentiment around domestic semiconductor supply chains.
  • Nvidia (NVDA) +3.6% — Reports that major Chinese firms plan to ramp purchases of H200 GPUs provided a strong demand catalyst.
  • JPMorgan (JPM) -2.5% — Financials bore the brunt of yield uncertainty and geopolitical risk ahead of Q2 earnings on July 14.
  • Visa (V) -1.3% — Consumer payment stocks weakened on macro uncertainty tied to oil-driven inflation headwinds.
Section 4
Volatility

The CBOE VIX index closed at 16.90, rising +4.56% (+0.77 points) on the session — reflecting a measured uptick in hedging demand as geopolitical risk resurfaced, though still comfortably in the neutral zone (15–20). The VIX has not yet breached the psychologically significant 20 threshold that signals elevated market fear, suggesting investors are nervous but not yet in panic mode. The intraday high of 18.56 is worth monitoring; a sustained push through 20 could trigger a more defensive repositioning across risk assets.

Section 5
Fixed Income

The US 10-year Treasury yield settled at 4.56%, its highest close in several weeks, as energy-driven inflation concerns prompted a sell-off in longer-dated bonds. The 2-year yield edged up 2bps to 4.21%, leaving the 10Y–2Y spread at a positive +35bps — a moderately steep, normally shaped curve. The curve's continued positive slope reflects the market's baseline assumption that near-term Fed policy will remain restrictive, with cuts priced later rather than sooner. A further oil spike or a hot June CPI reading next week could push the 10-year materially higher.

Section 6
Currencies

EUR/USD edged fractionally higher to 1.1419 (+0.06%), demonstrating relative resilience as the dollar lost mild safe-haven momentum versus the euro despite the risk-off backdrop — suggesting the euro is benefiting from some diversification flows. USD/JPY held firm near 162.55 (essentially unchanged from a prior close of 162.58), as the yen's traditional safe-haven appeal was offset by the Bank of Japan's still-accommodative policy stance relative to the Fed. The yen's failure to strengthen meaningfully during a geopolitical risk episode underscores the carry trade's continued structural support for USD/JPY at elevated levels.

⚡ WATCH
Commodities

WTI Crude Oil surged +5.12% to $74.04/barrel — the sharpest single-session move in months — as Trump's announcement of renewed US–Iran strikes stoked fears of disruption to Middle Eastern supply routes. The move reinstates a meaningful geopolitical risk premium in energy markets and raises the stakes for June CPI data; a sustained move above $75 would materially complicate the Fed's disinflationary narrative.

Gold held firm at $4,075/troy oz, trading essentially flat on the session. Despite the geopolitical shock, gold's muted response suggests the surge in real yields and a modestly firmer dollar offset typical safe-haven buying. At these levels, gold remains elevated on a historical basis, underpinned by central bank accumulation and residual macro uncertainty.
