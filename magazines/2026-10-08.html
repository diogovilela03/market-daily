Section 2
Macro

The Federal Reserve's higher-for-longer stance continues to anchor short-end rates with the 2-year yield at 4.79%, while the 10-year sits at 5.27%, keeping borrowing costs historically elevated across the economy. The yield curve's modest positive slope (+48 bps) signals that markets are not yet pricing a near-term recession, but the absolute level of rates is exerting gravitational pull on housing, corporate investment, and risk appetite. Geopolitical risk premia — reflected in elevated gold prices and cautious positioning — remain a headwind for sentiment, even as US economic data has been broadly resilient.

Section 4
Fixed Income

The US Treasury curve remains positively sloped with the 10-year yield at 5.27% and the 2-year at 4.79%, a spread of approximately +48 basis points. The curve has been gradually steepening from the deeply inverted levels seen in prior years — suggesting markets are incrementally pricing out near-term recession risk while acknowledging the Fed will hold rates higher for longer. The 10-year's persistence above 5% is a key constraint on equity multiple expansion and continues to provide meaningful competition for risk assets in portfolio allocation decisions.

Section 5
Currencies

EUR/USD at 1.1191, holding within recent range as the euro benefits from relative ECB hawkishness but faces headwinds from slowing European growth and energy cost pressures. USD/JPY at 158.15, with the yen near multi-decade lows as the Bank of Japan's ultra-loose yield curve control policy creates a stark contrast with the Federal Reserve's restrictive stance. The persistent 400+ basis point rate differential continues to fuel yen carry trades, though Japanese authorities have signalled ongoing vigilance around disorderly moves — intervention risk remains elevated above the 158–160 level.

Section 6
Commodities

Gold at $4,124.21/oz has reached a new all-time high, extending a multi-year structural bull run driven by central bank reserve diversification, geopolitical hedging, and rising interest in hard assets amid sovereign debt concerns globally. The metal's advance, achieved even in the face of elevated real yields, underscores the extraordinary depth of demand from non-traditional buyers. WTI crude at $96.24/bbl has retreated from last week's high of $99.77 — a pullback that reflects softening global demand expectations, particularly from China, offsetting the production discipline maintained by the OPEC+ coalition. A break below $95 would challenge the bullish supply-side narrative.

JPMorgan, Goldman Sachs, Citigroup, and UnitedHealth all report on October 13, making it a pivotal session for gauging the impact of elevated rates on financial sector margins and consumer credit quality.
