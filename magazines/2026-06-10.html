Section 02

Macro

The May non-farm payrolls blowout has effectively frozen the Federal Reserve, erasing near-term rate-cut expectations and reigniting debate about whether the next policy move could be a hike — a material shift in market psychology that is repricing assets across the duration spectrum. Treasury yields reflect a "no landing" scenario: the 10-year holds at 4.56% while the 2-year sits at 4.15%, maintaining a constructive +41 basis-point spread that signals durable growth rather than recessionary risk. On the geopolitical front, the Israel-Iran ceasefire represents meaningful near-term risk reduction, though the durability of any agreement will be tested quickly; the pass-through to energy prices and risk assets will be tracked closely in sessions ahead.

Section 03

Equities

SPY slipped 0.29% to $737.05 and QQQ fell 1.15% to $707.83 as Nasdaq bore the brunt of the rate-repricing trade — with semiconductor and mega-cap tech names leading losses through the session. IWM stood apart, gaining 0.32% to $285.02, as small-cap value and domestic cyclicals attracted rotational interest from investors rewarding economic strength over speculative growth. The SOXS 3× semiconductor bear ETF finished as the most actively traded instrument in the US market, surging 4.1%, a signal of the depth of conviction in the semis selloff.

Among the day's standout movers: Broadcom (AVGO) was the session's most consequential loser, its conservative AI chip guidance becoming the catalyst for a cascade across the semiconductor complex; Wells Fargo (WFC) was among the largest gainers after the Fed formally ended a years-long enforcement action, reinforcing the regional-banking thesis in a higher-rate environment. Top small-cap gainers included CCTG (+271%) and CHAI (+127%), though both are micro-cap names driven by idiosyncratic catalysts rather than macro moves.

Section 04

Volatility

The VIX closed at 19.87, rising 4.78% on the session and sitting just below the psychologically significant 20-level that separates complacency from elevated fear. An intraday spike to 23.34 — triggered by the depth of the semiconductor selloff and rate-hike speculation — reflected episodic stress that options buyers did not sustain into the close. At sub-20, the market is anxious but not panicked; however, with Oracle and Adobe earnings imminent, and the rate-hike narrative live, a sustained breach of 20 would meaningfully tighten financial conditions and may force de-risking among systematic strategies.

Section 05

Fixed Income

The US 10-year Treasury yield holds at 4.56% while the 2-year sits at 4.15%, maintaining a normal upward-sloping curve with a +41 basis-point spread — a modestly steepening configuration that reflects markets pricing durable US growth rather than recession risk. The jobs-driven rate repricing reinforced "higher-for-longer" as the base case, compressing valuations for long-duration equities and pressuring corporate debt refinancing economics. Any near-term catalyst — a softer CPI print, a Fed speaker strike in a dovish direction, or weak forward guidance from tech — could shift the curve rapidly; for now, the term premium remains bid and long-duration assets face continued headwinds.

Currencies

A move through 162 would likely force a BoJ response; any surprise in US growth data or a Fed pivot would generate sharp two-way volatility in the pair.

Section 07

Commodities

Gold trades at a remarkable $4,204.31/oz, near all-time highs and defying the usual inverse relationship with real yields — structural central bank buying, de-dollarisation flows, and long-horizon inflation hedging are the dominant drivers rather than short-term fear. The Israel-Iran ceasefire reduces near-term safe-haven urgency at the margin but is unlikely to dislodge the macro bid. WTI crude last traded at $95.96/bbl (data through June 1); the ceasefire should exert near-term downward pressure on crude as Middle East supply-disruption premiums ease, with OPEC+ supply policy and the trajectory of US demand becoming the next key swing factors.
