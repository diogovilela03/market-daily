Market Analysis
Market Drivers
  • Iran ceasefire collapse triggers risk-off rotation. President Trump's declaration that the Iran ceasefire was "over" on July 13 sent oil prices surging, lifted Treasury yields, and triggered broad selling across homebuilders, consumer discretionary, and financials — with Insperity, OSI Systems, Omnicom, PayPal, Ameriprise, and Ally Financial among notable casualties of the geopolitical shock.
  • Semiconductor sector under concentrated selling pressure. ARM Holdings fell 6.94% on competitive threats from RISC-V architecture and broad chip valuation recalibration; NVIDIA shed 3.52% (120M shares) and Intel dropped 6.12% (99M shares) on heavy volume, marking a sector-wide de-risking episode that weighed heavily on the QQQ.
  • Big bank earnings season begins with high expectations. JPMorgan Chase, Goldman Sachs, and Citigroup report Q2 results today (July 14) amid elevated analyst forecasts driven by strong trading revenues, AI-driven capital markets activity, and resilient consumer credit quality. Citigroup's Q1 revenue had already come in 14.1% above estimates, setting a high bar.
  • Morgan Stanley flags AI capex sustainability risk. The bank warned that the current market rally, built on $1.2 trillion in projected AI infrastructure spending, faces scrutiny over whether free cash flow can pace capex commitments — Oracle's $42B FCF deficit cited as a key pressure point for the thesis.

Macroeconomic
Macro Backdrop

The Trump administration's abrupt end to the Iran ceasefire injected a sharp geopolitical risk premium into markets at a sensitive juncture: CPI and PPI data are due this week (likely Tuesday–Wednesday), and a hot inflation print would further cement the Federal Reserve's higher-for-longer posture and push rate-cut expectations into late 2026 or beyond. The oil price surge triggered by the Iran shock is stagflationary — it simultaneously compresses consumer purchasing power and raises the Fed's inflation bar, narrowing the path to near-term easing.

In contrast, Brazil's June inflation surprised to the downside, pushing Banco Bradesco shares up 4% and validating emerging market central banks' more aggressive easing cycles. The divergence between EM easing and US stalling highlights the dollar's continued structural bid even as EUR/USD holds above 1.13.

Stock Markets
Equities

All three major US benchmarks declined on Monday's session (July 13). SPY fell 0.77% to $749.17, while QQQ shed 1.90% to $711.74 — its heavier technology and semiconductor weighting amplifying the sector-specific selloff. The small-cap IWM fell 0.85% to $293.48, reflecting broad-based risk reduction rather than an isolated sector story.

Notable movers from the session: NVIDIA (NVDA) fell 3.52% to $203.53 on 120M shares as the AI infrastructure trade faced macro-driven pressure; Intel (INTC) dropped 6.12% to $103.12 on 99M shares, among the heaviest decliners in the large-cap space. On the upside, energy majors rallied sharply — BP +4.27% and Chevron (CVX) +3.16% — as tighter oil supply expectations from the Iran breakdown boosted energy sector sentiment. American Airlines (AAL) fell 3.78% as rising fuel costs weighed on airline margin outlooks.

Volatility
Volatility

The CBOE VIX closed Monday at 17.16, up +12.41% on the session from an implied prior close near 15.03. While the 17 handle keeps VIX below the "elevated fear" threshold of 20, the magnitude of the single-session spike indicates the market was caught meaningfully offside — the sub-15 readings of recent weeks reflected complacency that quickly unwound on the Iran headline. A sustained push above 20 would signal structural concern and likely catalyse further de-leveraging; for now the move reads as an event-driven repricing of tail risk rather than a regime shift.

Rates & Bonds
Fixed Income

The US 10-year Treasury yield stands at 4.56% (latest data: July 10) while the 2-year yields 4.21%, placing the 2Y–10Y spread at a positive +35 basis points. The curve has re-steepened materially from the deep inversion seen through 2023–24, as term premium rebuilds and investors price rate cuts on the front end. This week's CPI release is the decisive near-term catalyst: a hot print risks pushing the 10Y back above 4.60–4.70% and further deferring Fed easing, while a soft read could deepen the steepening trade and bring the 2-year below 4.10%. The Iran-driven oil shock adds an upside risk to the inflation print, keeping the front end under pressure.

FX Markets
Currencies

EUR/USD trades at 1.1392, holding near cycle highs as dollar-negative macro dynamics — a widening US twin deficit and lingering Fed rate-cut expectations — continue to support the euro. The pair's resilience above 1.13 reflects sustained European capital inflows and reduced safe-haven dollar demand even on risk-off days, a structural shift from the 2022–23 dollar dominance era. USD/JPY sits at 162.35, firmly in the zone that has historically drawn Bank of Japan verbal intervention warnings. Yen weakness persists despite the BOJ's slow-motion tightening cycle, with the US–Japan rate differential — still over 400 basis points — overwhelming domestic policy signals. A fresh oil shock that widens Japan's import bill could push the pair further into intervention territory.

Commodities
Commodities

Gold spot is quoted at $4,028.59/oz — a historically significant level above the $4,000 milestone, reflecting structural demand from central bank accumulation, de-dollarisation flows, and persistent geopolitical uncertainty. The Iran ceasefire collapse on July 13 reinforced gold's safe-haven bid, and with CPI data incoming, the precious metal may sustain its bid as an inflation hedge as well. WTI crude was last reported by the API at $69.60/bbl (July 6 settlement); intraday on July 13, prices surged materially as markets priced supply disruption risk from the renewed Iran tensions — BP's +4.27% and Chevron's +3.16% gains confirm the market's bullish oil read. The eventual July 13 WTI settlement will likely print materially above the $69.60 reference level.
